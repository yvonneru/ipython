## 0. Framing: what AXIOMALITY actually competes with

AXIOMALITY sells a *per-record, machine-checkable certificate* ("Data Passport") for robot-learning trajectories/datasets, backed by a formal knowledge kernel (TUpper → physics → robotics → instances), plus runtime "semantic guardrails" compiled from the same kernel. No company found in this survey sells that exact artifact. The market instead splits into five adjacent layers, each of which could absorb a verification feature:

1. **AV/robot V&V suites** (Applied Intuition, Foretellix, Cognata, dSPACE/AVL) — verify *behaviour* against scenario coverage, not *training data* against an ontology.
2. **Robot data platforms** (Foxglove, Rerun, Encord, Scale, Voxel51, LeRobot) — ingest/curate/visualise; "quality checks" are heuristic, not certificates.
3. **Data supply** (Build AI, Mecka, Human Archive, Claru, Dyna, Lightwheel, Genesis, PI, Skild, Figure, DeepMind, NVIDIA) — produce data; their incentive is volume, and they self-certify.
4. **Safety/runtime assurance** (3Laws, SAIF Autonomy, reasonX Labs, Edge Case Research, NVIDIA Halos, DeepMind ASIMOV) — runtime guardrails and safety cases for *deployed* robots.
5. **Provenance/certification standards** (C2PA 2.1, Data & Trust Alliance, Croissant, Fairly Trained, CertifiedData, Pebblous) — metadata and licensing attestations, mostly for media/LLM data, not physics-checked robot trajectories.

## 2. Scarcity assessment

**Is anyone selling a per-record, machine-checkable certificate / verification layer for robot training data?** **No.** After ~75 searches across V&V vendors, data platforms, suppliers, safety-assurance startups and provenance standards, no commercial product issues a signed, offline-verifiable, per-trajectory attestation combining semantic coverage, physical-consistency residuals, logical consistency against a formal ontology, calibrated uncertainty and provenance/consent hashes. The concept exists in academia ("Data Passports: confidentially provable provenance", OpenReview; "AI Model Passport" for health, arXiv 2506.22358) and in adjacent form for synthetic-privacy data (CertifiedData) and licensing (Fairly Trained), but not for robot learning.

**Who is closest (functionally)?**
1. **NVIDIA Cosmos Evaluator** (open-source, Mar 2026) — already "scores, verifies and filters" physical-AI data for physical correspondence / temporal stability / semantic accuracy. It is a learned VLM judge, unsigned, non-formal, and aimed at NVIDIA's synthetic pipeline; but it is free and bundled into the Data Factory Blueprint that Skild, Uber, Teradyne and FieldAI already use. This is the product that will be on every VC's "why not just use Cosmos?" slide.
2. **Foretellix** — the true conceptual analog (independent coverage evidence feeding a safety case), but AV-only, shrinking (18% layoff, "slower than expected demand" for validation), and with no data-level certificate.
3. **Pebblous** (Korea) — small firm explicitly pitching "data-quality diagnosis, refinement and certification" for physical-AI behaviour data with EU-AI-Act evidence chains. Not a formal-methods shop, but the closest *positioning* match.
4. **Applied Intuition (Dana)** — has validation as core DNA, robot fleets, $15B balance sheet, and a July-2026 agentic platform that "validates that a robotic device behaves safely". It certifies *behaviour*, not *data*, today.

**Most likely incumbent to add the feature:** **NVIDIA first** (it already ships Cosmos Evaluator and now owns the certification on-ramp through the ANAB-accredited Halos Inspection Lab; adding a signed "data provenance/quality manifest" to the Data Factory Blueprint is a small step). **Applied Intuition second** (validation + robotics ambition + Dana). **Hugging Face third** — 58k LeRobot datasets with only descriptive cards is a badge-shaped hole, and HF is a natural *distribution partner* rather than a builder of formal kernels. **Scale** is *less* likely to be trusted as a neutral verifier post-Meta (customers already fled over data-leak fears), which is a genuine opening for an independent evidence layer.

**Scarcity verdict:** the *category* is empty; the *ingredients* (VLM plausibility judges, provenance metadata standards, safety-case tooling) are commoditising fast and are all owned or open-sourced by NVIDIA/standards bodies. AXIOMALITY's defensible piece is the formal kernel (machine-proven consistency, SMT-checkable claims, TUpper alignment) and the *independence* of the signer — not the pipeline.

## 3. Three analogies a US VC would use

1. **"Foretellix for robot learning."** *Helps:* instantly explains coverage-driven, evidence-for-the-safety-case positioning; Foretellix's NVIDIA investment shows incumbents will fund rather than build this. *Hurts:* Foretellix raised $135M over 8 years, never became a platform, and just cut 18% because "demand for validation tools was slower than expected" — the analogy invites the question "why will robots pay for verification before regulation forces them?"
2. **"C2PA / Content Credentials for robot data."** *Helps:* signed, offline-verifiable, per-asset manifests are a well-understood primitive; C2PA has 6,000+ members and a regulatory tailwind (AI Act Art. 50). *Hurts:* C2PA is a consortium standard, not a company — VCs will ask what stops LeRobot/NVIDIA from standardising an open "robot data manifest" and zeroing the margin. Also C2PA attests *origin*, not *physical or logical validity*, so it undersells the formal kernel.
3. **"Applied Intuition's validation suite as a standalone startup."** *Helps:* Applied's $15B valuation proves validation software commands premium multiples in physical AI, and unbundling is a familiar VC narrative. *Hurts:* Applied is now explicitly doing robotics (Dana, humanoid fleets) with ~$1.5B raised; the analogy makes AXIOMALITY look like a feature Applied could ship in a quarter unless the formal kernel is shown to be non-replicable.

(Runner-up framings: "Cosmos Evaluator, but signed and formal"; "UL/TÜV inspection lab for datasets"; "Halos for data".)

## 4. Ten sharp competitive questions a top-tier VC will ask

1. NVIDIA's Cosmos Evaluator already scores physical accuracy and semantic correctness for free inside the Data Factory Blueprint that Skild, Uber and Teradyne use. What does a customer get from a Passport that a Cosmos score plus an HF dataset card does not, and how do you prove it in a bake-off?
2. Who signs the certificate and why is that signature worth anything? Are you seeking recognition from TÜV/UL/exida or the NVIDIA Halos Inspection Lab, and what happens if NVIDIA adds a "data manifest" to Halos?
3. EU high-risk obligations for embedded products just slipped to Aug 2028. Which buyer pays for regulatory-grade data evidence in 2026–27, and how much? Foretellix says validation demand was slower than expected — why is robot learning different?
4. Scale, Encord, Mecka and Claru all ship "QA'd" data. Why would a buyer pay a second vendor to re-verify it, and would suppliers ever let an independent verifier grade their product?
5. Free data (Build AI's 1M hours, LeRobot's 58k datasets) is the fastest-growing supply. Is your wedge verifying *free* data for labs, or certifying *paid* data for suppliers? Pick one; the GTMs are different.
6. The formal kernel: how many axioms, what fraction of real trajectory failures does SMT/Z3 actually catch versus a learned judge, and what is the false-reject rate that would make a customer turn it off?
7. World Labs Atlas reconstructs scenes as Gaussian splats from 2–3 images. How much of your 100k measured 3D asset library remains an advantage in 18 months, and is it a moat or a sunk cost?
8. Runtime "semantic guardrails" compete with 3Laws (control-barrier functions), DeepMind's ASIMOV/constitutional guardrails and NVIDIA Halos OS. Is guardrails a product, a proof point for the kernel, or a distraction?
9. What is the interop and partnership plan with Foxglove, Rerun and LeRobot — do you become the "verified" badge on those rails, and what stops them from doing it themselves once the schema is public?
10. Toronto/China structure: with Lightwheel and the AgiBot World datasets Chinese-capitalised and US buyers sensitive to data provenance, how do you keep the signer credible to both US labs and Chinese OEMs — and which regulator (EU Art. 10 vs. China GB/Z 218.1) do you build the first audit pack for? **[GB/Z 218.1-2026 could not be verified in this survey.]**
