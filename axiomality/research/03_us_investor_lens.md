## 0. Bottom line (one paragraph)

The deck's regulatory facts are essentially **correct** (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 exists, dates check out), but the *implication* is weaker than the deck suggests: (i) the omnibus **pushed the robot-relevant deadline out to 2 Aug 2028**, (ii) Art. 10/12 reach a robot only when its learned component is a **safety component of machinery subject to third-party conformity assessment**, which excludes most manipulation policies as currently deployed, and (iii) the **US federal posture in 2026 is actively deregulatory** (Dec-2025 preemption EO, Colorado's high-risk law repealed and replaced with a narrower disclosure statute). For a Silicon Valley VC, "EU compliance" is a *tailwind slide*, not a "why now". The fundable "why now" is the **robot-foundation-model data race** (2026 YTD robotics funding ~$55.8B; Generalist, PI, Skild, Genesis, Mind, Atoms all raising nine-to-ten figure rounds) and the emerging, still-unowned "**data QA / evaluation / evidence**" layer between data collectors (Cortex, Servo7, Origami, Shotwell) and labs. Comparable seed/A companies in that layer (Instance YC S26, Shotwell, Cortex AI $6.5M, Alloy A$11.5M, 3Laws $4.1M) show the slot is open but priced at seed, not "platform".

### 1.2 Do AI Act Art. 10 / Art. 12 actually reach robots?

Mechanism (AI Act Art. 6(1), **[pre-2025 knowledge]**, consistent with the 2026 explainers found): an AI system is high-risk under the Annex I route only if **both** (a) it is a *safety component* of a product (or is itself a product) covered by Annex I Section A legislation — which includes machinery — **and** (b) that product must undergo **third-party conformity assessment** under that legislation. [euai-act.com](https://www.euai-act.com/articles/annex-i-explained), [Physical AI Atlas](https://www.d-fairy.fr/atlas/en/eu-ai-act-standards/).

The Machinery Regulation supplies the hook: Annex I Part A (mandatory third-party assessment) item 5 covers "safety components with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour using machine learning approaches ensuring safety functions", and item 6 covers machinery embedding such systems (in respect of those systems only) **[pre-2025 knowledge for item 6]**. [There's A Robot For That — Machinery Reg checklist](https://theresarobotforthat.com/blog/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230/), [Inkog Labs](https://inkog.io/labs/eu-machinery-regulation-ai-agents), [Physical AI Safety primer](https://physical-ai-safety.com/blog/eu-machinery-regulation-2027-primer).

So: **yes, Art. 10 (data governance) and Art. 12 (automatic logging) apply to a robot's learned component — but only when that learned component "ensures a safety function"**, and only from **2 Aug 2028**. Consequences a US VC will notice:

1. Most robot-learning deployments today keep safety in deterministic, safety-rated layers (safety PLC, speed-and-separation monitoring per ISO 10218 / ISO/TS 15066) and treat the VLA/policy as *task* logic, not a *safety* component. Under that architecture the policy is **not** Annex I high-risk, and Art. 10/12 do not bite on its training data. The deck's "mapping to Art. 10/12" is therefore correct for a subset (ML-based safety functions, humanoids in shared spaces where the policy *is* the safety function) and over-broad for the rest. [There's A Robot For That — humanoid timeline](https://theresarobotforthat.com/blog/eu-ai-act-humanoid-robots/).
2. Art. 10 content **[pre-2025 knowledge of Reg. 2024/1689 text]**: training/validation/test data must follow governance practices covering design choices; data collection processes and *origin* of data; annotation/labelling/cleaning; assumptions; availability and suitability; bias examination and mitigation; identification of data gaps; and data must be "relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible free of errors and complete" for the intended purpose. Art. 12 requires automatic event logging over the system's lifetime. These map cleanly onto the Data Passport fields (coverage, physics residuals, consistency, provenance/consent, real/synthetic ratio) — the mapping is a genuine asset, but its *legal trigger* is 2028 and conditional.
3. Harmonised standards are still being written (CEN-CENELEC JTC 21); the omnibus deferral was explicitly justified by missing standards. [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act). Any vendor claiming "Art. 10 compliant" today is ahead of the standard, which is a sales risk (buyers wait for the standard) and an opportunity (be in the standard).

### 1.3 United States, 2026

- **Federal**: Executive Order "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence" (11 Dec 2025) created a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws, directed Commerce to list "onerous" state laws by 11 Mar 2026, directed the FTC to declare state-mandated bias mitigation deceptive, and allows conditioning discretionary grants on states not enforcing AI laws; carve-outs for child safety, data-center/compute, and state procurement. The White House followed with legislative preemption recommendations (Mar 2026); Congress has so far declined preemption (OBBBA, NDAA). [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/president-trump-signs-executive-order-challenging-state-ai-laws), [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/state-ai-laws-under-federal-scrutiny-key-takeaways-executive-order-establishing), [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/the-white-house-legislative-recommendations-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-an), [Baker Botts Jan-2026 update](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/january/us-ai-law-update).
- **NIST**: no "AI RMF 2.0". AI RMF 1.0 is being revised under the July-2025 AI Action Plan; NIST released the Cyber AI Profile preliminary draft (IR 8596, Feb 2026) and a concept note for a Critical-Infrastructure AI RMF profile (7 Apr 2026); GenAI profile NIST-AI-600-1 dates from Jul 2024. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/cybersecurity-nist-draft-cybersecurity-framework-for-ai-reg-alert.html), [NIST CI profile](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/concept-note-ai-rmf-profile-trustworthy-ai-critical-infrastructure).
- **Colorado**: SB 24-205 was delayed to 30 Jun 2026 (SB25B-004, Aug 2025), then **repealed and replaced by SB 189** (signed 14 May 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027): "high-risk AI system" framework replaced with an ADMT/consequential-decision **disclosure** regime; duty of care, risk-management programs and impact assessments removed. [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/colorado-ai-act-repeal-new-transparency-law), [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/colorado-legislature-repeals-and-replaces-colorado-ai-act-what-sb-189-means-for-your-business.html), [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/colorado-governor-signs-sb-189).
- **California**: AB 2013 (Generative AI Training Data Transparency) effective 1 Jan 2026 requires *generative* AI developers to publish high-level training-data documentation (sources, licensing, PII, synthetic data); SB 53 (frontier-model safety, 1 Jan 2026); SB 942 (AI content provenance). None targets robot policies or embodied data. [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/alerts-otherindustries-californias-ab-2013-takes-effect), [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/californias-ab-2013-requires-generative-ai-data-disclosure-by-january-1-2026).
- **Robotics-specific**: no federal or state AI-data-governance obligation for robots was found. US robot safety remains standards-driven (ANSI/A3 R15.06, ISO 10218 series) **[pre-2025 knowledge]**.

### 1.4 Is "compliance" a credible "why now" for a US VC?

**Weak as the lead; acceptable as a secondary tailwind.** Reasons: (a) the only binding robot-relevant obligation is EU, conditional, and now dated 2028; (b) the US is moving the other way (preemption EO, Colorado rollback); (c) EU harmonised standards do not yet exist, so buyers cannot yet be "audited" against Art. 10; (d) the AI-governance software cohort built on AI-Act timing (Credo AI, Holistic AI, Saidot, Fairly/Asenion) has stayed sub-scale for five years (see §3). What *does* work: compliance as the **second reason to buy** after a first reason rooted in model performance/economics (Vanta's SOC 2 wedge worked because *customers*, not regulators, demanded the badge). Reframe the Data Passport as **"proof your data is worth training on"** with regulatory mapping as a free by-product.

### 2.3 What these investors treat as "venture-scale" infra

Synthesised from the RFS language, the AD/physical-AI pages and the round data above (treat as heuristics, not quotes):

1. **Market**: a16z wants a path to $1B+ revenue; YC wants a path to a $1B+ company in ~10 years. A bottom-up $12–60M TAM (80–200 orgs) is a *feature* market; the "500 × $200k = $100M ARR" platform path is still below the bar unless pricing is usage-based (per-trajectory / per-passport) and rides the growth of robot-data spend.
2. **Wedge**: a narrow, painful, measurable job (Vanta: "fastest way to SOC 2" — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/companies/vanta); Instance: "did the rollout succeed?"). Ontologies and SMT are *how*, not *what*.
3. **Speed**: robotics-data companies are moving from seed to $100M+ in 12–18 months (Genesis $105M seed → $3B talks in 12 months; Generalist $2B → $3B in ~10 weeks). [Bloomberg/Genesis](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/robotics-startup-genesis-in-talks-to-raise-about-500-million), [TechCrunch/Generalist](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/robotics-startup-generalist-reaches-3b-valuation-sources-say/)
4. **Founder-market fit**: a16z AD and YC both index heavily on founders who have shipped in the domain; an ISO ontology author + two prior exits is strong on *technical* and *commercial* axes but there is no robot-learning-lab operating experience on the team as described — expect the "who on your team has trained a VLA?" question.

## 4. Data-provenance and certification landscape

- **C2PA / Content Credentials**: spec v2.4 (Apr 2026); v2.1 (Sep 2024) added AI-training/data-mining assertions; 2026 conformance programme; native signing on Leica, Samsung Galaxy S25, Pixel 10; LinkedIn/TikTok/Cloudflare preserve credentials; used to satisfy EU AI Act Art. 50 and California SB 942 machine-readable disclosure. C2PA is per-*asset* provenance for media, not per-*trajectory* semantic/physics evidence — complementary, and a natural container for a passport's signature chain. [C2PA explainer](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/explainer/Explainer.html), [c2paviewer](https://c2paviewer.com/articles/what-is-c2pa), [OpenEmpower](https://www.openempower.com/blog/digital-provenance-content-authenticity-2026-c2pa)
- **Data Provenance Initiative (MIT)**: audited 1,800+ fine-tuning datasets across 44 collections; license omission >70% and error >50% on popular hubs; Data Provenance Explorer. Evidence that provenance metadata is broken even for text — robotics datasets (Open X-Embodiment, DROID, LeRobot hub) are less audited still. [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/bringing-transparency-to-data-used-to-train-artificial-intelligence), [Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00878-8), [GitHub](https://github.com/Data-Provenance-Initiative/Data-Provenance-Collection)
- **Data & Trust Alliance Data Provenance Standards** v1.0.0, handed to an OASIS TC (announced 6 Mar 2025, first meeting 8 Apr 2025; sponsors Cisco, IBM, Intel, Microsoft, Red Hat); simplified from eight categories to three (source, provenance, use). This is the closest thing to an enterprise "data passport" schema and AXIOMALITY should be schema-compatible with it. [OASIS](https://www.oasis-open.org/2025/03/06/oasis-to-advance-data-provenance-standards/), [D&TA](https://www.dtaalliance.org/work/data-provenance-standards)
- **Datasheets for Datasets** (Gebru et al., 2018) and **Model Cards** (2019) **[pre-2025 knowledge]** are broadly adopted as documentation norms; **Croissant** (NeurIPS 2024 D&B) is now the machine-readable metadata standard across Hugging Face, Kaggle and OpenML (>400k datasets), auto-generated by the HF dataset viewer and a NeurIPS 2025 submission requirement; "Croissant Baker" (May 2026) automates generation. A Data Passport that does not emit Croissant + D&TA fields will look proprietary. [HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/dataset-viewer/en/croissant), [Croissant paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9547b09b722f2948ff3ddb5d86002bc0-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf), [Croissant Baker](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15079)
- **Vendors selling AI-Act compliance**: Credo AI, Holistic AI, Fairly AI/Asenion, Saidot, Enzai, Trustible, OneTrust, Cranium, plus Vanta/Drata adding ISO 42001. None surfaced offering **per-dataset cryptographic certification** for embodied data. [EU-Startups, Aug 2026](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/08/10-european-compliance-startups-to-watch-as-ai-act-enforcement-kicks-in), [AI Compliance Vendors](https://aicompliancevendors.com/best/eu-ai-act-compliance-tools)
- **"Passport" in EU policy**: the **Digital Product Passport** (ESPR) is an established EU concept — registry operational 20 Jul 2026, first mandatory product group (batteries) 18 Feb 2027, then category-by-category delegated acts through 2030; it concerns physical-product sustainability and lifecycle data, not AI datasets. [EC DPP](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport_en), [DPP developer guide](https://dev.to/julien786534/eu-digital-product-passports-in-2026-a-developers-implementation-guide-2njo). **Naming verdict**: in the EU, "Data Passport" will be heard as "DPP for data" — that is a *helpful* mental model (signed, machine-readable, lifecycle record) but invites the question "is this an official EU scheme?" (it is not). In the US it is neutral-to-positive. Keep the name; add a one-line disambiguation ("not a regulatory instrument; a signed evidence record") and consider "Trajectory Passport" for the per-episode object.
- ISO/IEC 21838-4:2022 (TUpper) exists as a published top-level-ontology standard **[pre-2025 knowledge; not re-verified]**; the founder's authorship is a credibility asset with standards bodies and notified bodies, less so with US VCs.

## 5. Verdict

### 5(a) Would a YC partner fund this today?

**Probably not as pitched; plausibly yes if re-cut.** YC has already funded the neighbouring bets (Shotwell = data QA, Instance = eval, One Robot = world-model eval, Cortex/Servo7/Origami = data supply) and its RFS asks for *doing the work* and *dense physical-world data*, not for ontologies or compliance. The founder profile (repeat founder, >$50M year-one revenue, ISO author) is exactly what YC likes; the blocker is "no customers, engine in internal test Q4 2026" — YC funds pre-product teams, but wants a launched wedge within the batch.

YC-application answers that would work:
- **One-liner**: "Axiomality certifies robot training data. We run every trajectory through physics, logic and coverage checks and sign a passport that says what it proves."
- **What are you building**: "A verifier that ingests robot episodes (teleop, autonomous, synthetic), scores them on physical plausibility, semantic coverage and provenance, and emits a signed, offline-verifiable passport. Labs use it to reject bad data before training; data vendors use it to prove quality; OEMs use it as audit evidence."
- **Traction needed**: 2–3 labs or data vendors running the verifier on real episodes (even free), one quantified result ("rejecting the bottom 12% of episodes by passport score improved policy success X pts"), and one paid pilot. Without any of these, a YC partner will ask for the demo and the numbers.

### 5(b) Would an a16z infra / American-Dynamism partner?

**Not at seed as a stand-alone "compliance evidence" company; possibly as a Series A once it is the de facto QA layer used by two named labs.** a16z's physical-AI bets are full-stack or model companies (Atoms, Mind, World Labs); their question will be "why doesn't Mind/PI/Skild build this internally, and why doesn't Foxglove/Rerun/Applied Intuition add it?" What they need to see:
1. **Design partners**: two of {PI, Skild, Generalist, Genesis, Dyna, Mind, Figure, Apptronik, 1X, Agility} or two of the data vendors feeding them (Cortex, Servo7, Mercor's robotics unit) using the passport in their ingestion pipeline.
2. **ARR/usage**: $0.5–1M ARR or, more persuasively, millions of trajectories passported per month with metered pricing.
3. **A demo** that catches something a human labeller would miss (physics-inconsistent teleop, consent-missing episodes, synthetic-real leakage) in a live dataset, with the passport verified offline on a laptop.
4. **A 10x insight**: a defensible claim such as "passport score predicts downstream policy success better than any existing filter" or "we are the only cryptographically verifiable proof of data lineage a notified body/insurer will accept" — with evidence.
5. **Market story**: not 80–200 orgs × $200k, but a take-rate on robot-data spend (if labs spend 20–30% of the ~$10B+ raised in 2025–26 on data, a 3–5% verification tax is a $60–150M/yr pool that grows with data volume).

### 5(c) The 10 hardest questions a US VC would ask

1. Your legal trigger is 2 Aug 2028 and only for ML *safety components*; what does a lab buy from you in 2026–27, and why now?
2. Why isn't this a feature of Foxglove, Rerun, Applied Intuition, Foretellix, or of the lab's own data pipeline? What is the moat beyond an ontology?
3. Show me evidence that passport score correlates with downstream policy performance. If it doesn't, why should a lab pay?
4. Who on the team has trained and deployed a VLA/policy at scale? Where is the robot-learning operator?
5. Physics residuals and SMT consistency on teleop data: what is your false-reject rate, and what does it cost a lab when you reject good data?
6. Name the two design partners. What did they say when you asked for $25k?
7. The US is preempting state AI law and Colorado rolled back; EU standards for Art. 10 don't exist. What happens to your roadmap if EU enforcement slips again?
8. Your bottom-up TAM is $12–60M. What has to be true for this to be a $1B company — per-trajectory metering, an OEM/insurer channel, or something else?
9. Data vendors (Mercor, Surge, Cortex) have every incentive to self-certify. Why would they let a third party score their product, and why would labs trust *your* signature?
10. Synthetic and world-model data (Genesis, World Labs, NVIDIA Cosmos) will dominate volume. Does your passport still mean anything when the "provenance" is a generator seed?

### 5(d) Rewritten one-line pitch, three variants

- **YC-style**: "Axiomality tells robot labs which training data is real, physically plausible and safe to use — and signs a certificate they can show anyone."
- **a16z-style (infra category)**: "The verification layer for physical-AI data: a formally grounded engine that scores, signs and audits every robot trajectory, so foundation-model builders train on evidence instead of assumptions."
- **Analogy-style**: "Vanta for robot training data — a SOC 2-style signed report for every dataset, backed by physics and logic checks instead of checklists."
