## A. Memo to the PI — why the program needs this allocation, and why the ask is sized as it is

**To:** Prof. Michael Grüninger · **From:** Yi Ru · **Re:** RAC 2027 RRG request — justification · **Date:** [20 Oct 2026]

**1. What the allocation is for.** My research program — verified mereological ontologies for object–part representation in physical AI — has three objectives. O1 (ontology as specification) and the first audit under O2 are theorem-proving and CPU work that I complete before April 2027 on my current allocations at Harvard [HMS O2; NSF ACCESS and NAIRR requests in progress]. Two pieces of work cannot be done before April 2027, and once I leave Harvard they cannot be done at all without Alliance resources. (a) The re-audit of all seven corpora with the released ontology version: a CPU-and-storage job of about [1.4] core-years (≈ 12,200 core-hours) and [20 TB] of project storage. (b) The full O3 experimental matrix: the policy-training experiments that test whether the ontology, used as an inductive bias, improves generalization to unseen object categories. That is a sustained GPU job of about [7,000] GPU-hours. The allocation year, 1 April 2027 – 31 March 2028, covers months 7–18 of the programme if we count from October 2026. That includes all of the 12-month plan's policy experiments; the 24-month plan's constraint-training block continues into 2028–29.

**Before you decide: the RAC minimum.** Search results on 26 September attribute two rules to the Alliance [I could not open the page; please verify, or ask research services]. First, a RAC application must request at least 200 core-years of CPU or 25 RGU-years of GPU. Second, a PI can request up to 40 TB of project storage and 100 TB of nearline storage through the Rapid Access Service with no RAC at all. My request is well below both compute minimums. The storage fits inside RAS. I am not going to enlarge the experiment to clear the threshold. So I propose: (i) you file a RAS storage request for 20 TB of project storage now; (ii) the audit runs on RAS opportunistic CPU; (iii) we submit the RRG on 30 Oct only if, by 20 Oct, the RAC 2027 Guide shows a threshold this request meets, or a measured per-run GPU cost puts the matrix above 25 RGU-years. The break-even is about [61] H100-hours per run, against my assumed [24].

**2. Why the GPU number is what it is.** I have not padded the request; I have derived it from the experimental design and I show the derivation so that a technical reviewer can cut it at a specific line rather than by a percentage. The O3 hypotheses are three: ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories; ontology-violation rate predicts task failure; pooled ontology-aligned data transfers across part vocabularies. Testing them with ablations by axiom family gives {no ontology loss, ontology loss} × {augmentation off, on} plus four single-family ablations = 8 conditions; [5] held-out category splits and [3] seeds are the minimum for confidence intervals that a robotics reviewer will accept; that is 120 runs. The pooled-transfer block adds 18. At [24] GPU-hours per run of ~[1M] environment steps with SAPIEN rendering — a figure I will replace with the measured value from the Harvard pilot before you submit — training is ~[3,300] GPU-hours. Verification-in-the-loop evaluation adds [25 %]; a half-matrix replication in [Isaac Lab] adds ~[2,000]; development and failed runs add [30 %] of training. Total ~[7,000] GPU-hours, range [4,000–10,000], or ≈ [9.7] RGU-years if all of it ran on H100 (12.15 RGU each [verify the Alliance factor]). The Isaac Lab share cannot run on H100: Isaac Lab camera rendering needs RT cores [verify NVIDIA's current requirements]. So it needs an RTX partition [Killarney L40S, if confirmed] or the NVIDIA Academic Grant workstation. If the reviewers scale the request back, drop these lines in order: the Isaac Lab replication, the third seed, the fifth split.

**3. Why the CPU number is what it is.** Tier 1 (compiled SMT/Datalog subset) checks every annotated part instance: [~20M] instances across seven corpora × [0.2 s] per instance × [15] passes ([3] ontology versions × [5] axiom-family conditions) ≈ [16,700] core-hours over the whole programme. Tier 2 (Prover9/Mace4 on residual cases) is bounded by the [300 s] timeout: ~[4,000] core-hours expected; [20,800] in the worst case, where every one of [50k] obligations × [5] passes times out. O1 verification is under 500 core-hours. About [9,000] core-hours run at Harvard before April 2027, which leaves ≈ 12,200 core-hours (≈ [1.4] core-years) for the allocation year, with ≈ [3.3] core-years as the worst case. SAPIEN physics needs ~[8] cores per GPU, which comes to ≈ 56,000 core-hours over the matrix. Those cores run inside the GPU jobs. (The compute bundle's "~1 core-year" for this line is an arithmetic slip, and I have corrected it here.)

**4. Why the storage number is what it is.** The raw corpora are large (Open X-Embodiment ~9 TB in RLDS; AgiBot World Beta tens of TB; DROID ~1.7 TB — registry figures, unverified) but they are extraction inputs, not outputs: only annotations, kinematics and episode metadata enter the instance store, which is [10–20 %] of raw size. So the need is [20 TB] of project storage for the instance store, audit outputs, mappings and checkpoints, which fits inside the 40 TB RAS ceiling. On top of that, scratch space for the raw corpora, extracted one at a time, peaks at ≈ [50 TB] when AgiBot World Beta is processed. Nearline is tape-backed and the wrong place for data under active extraction. No frames are ever re-hosted.

**5. Why the plan is credible for a group without GPU history.** First, the CPU half continues the lab's established theorem-proving workflow; only the GPU half is new. Second, the per-run GPU cost will be a measured number, not an estimate. If the sponsored role is active by about 10 October, I will run [1–2] calibration runs on RAS opportunistic GPU. The Harvard pilot then refines the figure. Third, from October 2026 the sponsored RAS role and the group's RAS storage carry the pipeline and the instance store. The RAC 2028 application will therefore show a year of real usage whether or not we apply this year. If there is no RRG in 2027, the programme still runs: the audit completes on RAS CPU, and the matrix runs more slowly on opportunistic GPU plus an NVIDIA Academic Grant workstation. The cut order in §2 applies.

**6. What I will not do with the allocation.** I founded a company, AXIOMALITY, that works on robotics-data verification. No company work will run on this allocation or on any academic resource; the datasets I register for academically will not be used by the company; company workloads run on separately funded company accounts. I would like this stated in the application so that it is on record.

**7. Why this matters beyond my own program.** The audit toolkit and the violation metric are reusable for any hierarchical annotation — CAD assemblies, building information models, part labels in medical imaging — and the ontology goes back to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42. The allocation buys the first measurement of a data-quality problem that the whole field of robot learning has assumed away.

— Yi
