## A. Memo to the PI — why the program needs this allocation, and why the ask is sized as it is

**To:** Prof. Michael Grüninger · **From:** Yi Ru · **Re:** RAC 2027 RRG request — justification · **Date:** [20 Oct 2026]

**1. What the allocation is for.** My research program — verified mereological ontologies for object–part representation in physical AI — has three objectives. O1 (ontology as specification) and the first audit under O2 are theorem-proving and CPU work that I complete before April 2027 on my current allocations at Harvard [HMS O2; NSF ACCESS and NAIRR requests in progress]. What cannot be done before April 2027, and cannot be done at all without Alliance resources once I am in Toronto, is (a) the re-audit of all seven corpora with the released ontology version, which is a CPU-and-storage job of roughly [2] core-years and [20 TB] of instance data, and (b) the full O3 experimental matrix — the policy-training experiments that test whether the ontology, used as an inductive bias, improves generalization to unseen object categories — which is a sustained GPU job of roughly [7,000] H100-hours. The Rapid Access Service defaults (opportunistic GPU behind RAC groups; ~[1 TB] project storage) cannot carry either. The allocation year, 1 April 2027 – 31 March 2028, is exactly the period in which this work falls in both the 24-month and the 12-month plan.

**2. Why the GPU number is what it is.** I have not padded the request; I have derived it from the experimental design and I show the derivation so that a technical reviewer can cut it at a specific line rather than by a percentage. The O3 hypotheses are three: ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories; ontology-violation rate predicts task failure; pooled ontology-aligned data transfers across part vocabularies. Testing them with ablations by axiom family gives {no ontology loss, ontology loss} × {augmentation off, on} plus four single-family ablations = 8 conditions; [5] held-out category splits and [3] seeds are the minimum for confidence intervals that a robotics reviewer will accept; that is 120 runs. The pooled-transfer block adds 18. At [24] GPU-hours per run of ~[1M] environment steps with SAPIEN rendering — a figure I will replace with the measured value from the Harvard pilot before you submit — training is ~[3,300] GPU-hours. Verification-in-the-loop evaluation adds [25 %]; a half-matrix replication in [Isaac Lab] adds ~[2,000]; development and failed runs add [30 %] of training. Total ~[7,000] GPU-hours, range [4,000–10,000]. If the reviewers scale it back, the lines to drop in order are: the Isaac Lab replication, the third seed, the fifth split.

**3. Why the CPU number is what it is.** Tier 1 (compiled SMT/Datalog subset) checks every annotated part instance: [~20M] instances across seven corpora × [0.2 s] per instance × [15] passes ([3] ontology versions × [5] axiom-family conditions) ≈ [17,000] core-hours. Tier 2 (Prover9/Mace4 on residual cases) is bounded by the [300 s] timeout: ~[4,000] core-hours expected, [60,000] worst case if every escalation times out. SAPIEN physics and rendering need ~[8] cores per GPU during training, ~[1] core-year. Together ~[2] core-years planned; I state the worst case so that nobody is surprised.

**4. Why the storage number is what it is.** The raw corpora are large (Open X-Embodiment ~9 TB in RLDS; AgiBot World Beta tens of TB; DROID ~1.7 TB — registry figures, unverified) but they are extraction inputs, not outputs: only annotations, kinematics and episode metadata enter the instance store, which is [10–20 %] of raw size. So the request is [20 TB] project storage for the instance store, audit outputs and mappings, and [50 TB] nearline/scratch for raw corpora during extraction and for rollouts and checkpoints. No frames are ever re-hosted.

**5. Why the ask is credible for a group without GPU history.** Three things. First, the CPU half continues the lab's established theorem-proving workflow; only the GPU half is new. Second, the per-run GPU cost will be a measured number from pilot runs at Harvard, not an estimate. Third, from October 2026 I will use the sponsored Rapid Access role to prototype the pipeline and hold the instance store, so that the "past usage" section can show real usage rather than none. If a first-year cut is severe, the program still runs: the audit completes on CPU, the matrix shrinks as in §2, and a RAC 2028 request carries the rest with a year of usage behind it.

**6. What I will not do with the allocation.** I founded a company, AXIOMALITY, that works on robotics-data verification. No company work will run on this allocation or on any academic resource; the datasets I register for academically will not be used by the company; company workloads run on separately funded company accounts. I would like this stated in the application so that it is on record.

**7. Why this matters beyond my own program.** The audit toolkit and the violation metric are reusable for any hierarchical annotation — CAD assemblies, building information models, part labels in medical imaging — and the ontology goes back to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42. The allocation buys the first measurement of a data-quality problem that the whole field of robot learning has assumed away.

— Yi
