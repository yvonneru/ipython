## A. For the PI

**What the program is.** The Alliance's annual competition for compute, GPU, storage and cloud on the national systems, for needs beyond what the Rapid Access Service provides. RRG is the stream for individual faculty PIs and their sponsored users. Allocation year 1 Apr 2027 – 31 Mar 2028; renewable; Fast Track renewal for up to two further years if the science score exceeds 2.0 out of 5 (confirmed on alliancecan.ca). RAC 2027 closes **3 November 2026** (confirmed).

**The eligibility floor (read first).** Search results on 2026-09-26 attribute to the Alliance a minimum RAC request of **200 core-years CPU or 25 RGU-years GPU**, and a RAS ceiling of **40 TB project / 100 TB nearline** that needs no RAC [verify in the RAC 2027 Guide]. Our request is ≈ 9.7 RGU-years, ≈ 1.4 core-years and 20 TB project storage. As drafted, then, it is below the floor, and its storage part belongs in a RAS request. The package recommends a RAS storage request now, and an RRG only if the gate in budget_and_timeline.md §0 passes by 20 Oct.

**What the form asks.** Project title and summary; research description [length limit — verify]; resource request by type with technical justification and evidence of scaling; HQP list with CCDB usernames; past usage and progress (first request: state RAS usage or "none"); publications from prior allocations; the PI's CCV [variant — verify]. Text fields; the draft in proposal.md is sized to be pasted section by section.

**What reviewers score (as best known).**
- *Award basis* (2024 results page, via search): resources are awarded on "the appropriateness of the computational resources requested to achieve the project's objectives and the likelihood that these resources will be efficiently used, rather than on the evaluation of a complete research program". In 2024, applications with a science score above 2.0/5 received an allocation, and requests were scaled by score, request size and supply.
- *Science review* (score out of 5): quality and significance of the research, feasibility, and [HQP training and productivity: whether these are scored separately, verify in the Guide]. Committees are discipline-based. The project sits in computing science / AI with an engineering PI [choose the committee consistently with the CPRA subject-code decision].
- *Technical review*: are the requested resources justified by the described workload? Does the software run on the target hardware? (SAPIEN needs Vulkan on the nodes; Isaac Lab needs RT-core/RTX GPUs, which H100 lacks.) Are the storage categories used correctly? (Nearline is tape-backed; raw corpora go on scratch.) Has the group shown it can use what it asks for? A derivation that can be cut line by line survives better than a round number.

1. The research description reads as the PI's program, not a postdoc's side project: it opens with COLORE, TUpper/ISO 21838-4 and the lab's dataset-evaluation work, and places Dr. Ru's theory (the two Synthese submissions) inside it. Keep that framing; edit the voice to your own.

3. The "past usage" section is honest and non-empty: RAS usage from Dr. Ru's sponsored role (Oct 2026 onward) plus any calibration or Harvard pilot results, and the statement that the CPU half continues the lab's existing theorem-proving workflow.
4. The outputs are public and cross-disciplinary: the ontology goes to COLORE and SC 42; audit reports, mappings and an Apache-2.0 validator are released; the toolkit is reusable for CAD, BIM and medical-image labels. [Whether reuse or open outputs are scored: verify in the Guide.]

**What to avoid.** Submitting below the minimum (≥ 25 RGU-years GPU or ≥ 200 core-years CPU [verify]). Clearing that minimum by enlarging the experiment rather than on measured per-run costs: the technical review checks the derivation. Asking RAC for storage that RAS already provides.
