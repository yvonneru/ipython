## Verified Mereological Ontologies for Object–Part Representation in Physical AI

**Problem.** Robot manipulation policies are trained on part-level datasets — PartNet [1], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [2], GAPartNet [3], AgiBot World [4], Open X-Embodiment [5], DROID [6] — that annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each defines "part" operationally: a segmentation mask, a movable link, an actionable region. No dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. Poor transfer across part vocabularies and brittle generalization to new categories are widely observed but never measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. The question is one in the metaphysics of parts and wholes as much as in machine learning: *can the part representations that robot-learning systems acquire be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

**What I bring.** I axiomatize ontologies of physical objects in first-order logic and verify them, so that every claim about an ontology is a theorem or a counter-model. As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology [7], I worked on [its axiomatization and machine-checked verification — confirm exact role wording]. With Michael Grüninger (University of Toronto) I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two papers submitted to *Synthese* [8, 9]: stated in first-order logic and checked with automated provers and model finders, the account says exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of one object (the statue and the clay; a cabinet's kinematic and functional hierarchies) to be compatible. My doctoral thesis [10] developed an architecture in which a verified ontology governs an AI system's data, learned models and simulation layer, and I have carried that discipline into practice through ontology-governed data systems in production at companies I co-founded and nine invention patent applications in 3D recognition and indoor modelling [reconcile counts]. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School, [laboratory; one sentence on the work].

**Objectives.** (1) *Ontology as specification:* axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of object parts — rigid, articulated, functional, assembly — as an extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language [11], so that changes of parthood under manipulation (detaching a lid, opening a drawer) are fluents. (2) *Ontology as audit:* translate the annotation hierarchies of the datasets above into ontology instances and check them automatically, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot data and cross-dataset label mappings that are provably meaning-preserving. (3) *Ontology as inductive bias:* train and evaluate part-aware manipulation policies against the verified ontology and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories.

**State of the art.** Formal mereology [12] supplies axiom systems for parthood, and TUpper's mereotopology modules are verified [7, 13], but no verified ontology distinguishes the four senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data, and the constitution literature [8, 9, 12] has never been connected to a dataset. Part-level datasets [1–6] carry no formal semantics; [prior ontological analysis of ShapeNet/PartNet — reference 13, from Prof. Grüninger] produced neither a scalable audit nor a cross-dataset alignment. Neuro-symbolic constraint methods [14] have not been applied to part structure in manipulation, and no evaluation protocol checks a policy's inferred object structure against any specification. No specification, no measurement, no training signal: the objectives close the gap in that order.

**Plan.** Verification (do the axioms have exactly the intended models?) follows the COLORE repository methodology [15]: each module is characterized up to isomorphism with Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [16]. Validation (are those the right models?) is settled by competency questions taken from the datasets: each annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute. The tools — COLORE, Prover9/Mace4, Z3 — are open and travel with me; Prof. Grüninger [continues as co-author on the ontology component — confirm].
*Year 1 — Parts and audit.* Axiomatize the four parthood modules in Common Logic, verified (consistency, non-triviality, representation theorems); apply the constitution mappings [8, 9] to state when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible. Build a two-tier audit: bulk checking of millions of part instances by Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [17], residual hard cases by first-order proving, with UNKNOWN and timeout as first-class outputs. Questions answered: is every dataset's hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not? Do violation rates differ between simulated and real-robot corpora?
*Year 2 — Learning.* Release corrected annotations, proven label mappings and a merged, ontology-aligned corpus with an audit toolkit. Implement differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on a policy's inferred part relations [14] and constraint-guided augmentation for under-represented categories; report ontology-violation rate alongside task success on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories in SAPIEN [2] and [second simulator — agree with host], with ablations by axiom family. Questions answered: does ontology-consistent training improve generalization to unseen categories? Does violation rate predict task failure?
*Year 3 — From result to method.* Replicate on [host group's robot platform]; apply the audit unchanged to a second hierarchical-annotation domain (CAD assemblies, or part labels in medical imaging); contribute the parts ontology to COLORE and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42. Three years make this sequence possible: ontology and audit must exist before the learning experiments, and the replications turn a result into a method.

**Significance.** For robotics: an interpretable, machine-checkable failure signal — ontology-violation rate — and a public, ontology-aligned corpus. For philosophy: the constitution and pluralism debates become theories with empirical consequences — a dataset either is or is not a model of a given mereology. For standards and assurance: the ontology, audit results and toolkit are released under an open licence and contributed to COLORE and SC 42 — the evidence about training data that the EU AI Act (AI in machinery and robots, from 2 August 2028) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework will require.
