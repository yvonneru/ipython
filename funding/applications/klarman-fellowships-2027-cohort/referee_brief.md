## What the selection committee weighs (confirmed wording)
Fellows are selected "based on their research accomplishments, potential for future contributions, and alignment of scholarly interests with those of their proposed faculty mentors in Arts & Sciences"; competitive applications "demonstrate the candidate's capacity for original thought, combined with intellectual rigor and discipline to investigate their ideas in meaningful ways." The program asks that referees be scholars very familiar with the applicant's research who can speak in detail about (i) their prior working relationship with the applicant, (ii) the importance of the proposed research, and (iii) the scholarly and professional qualities that enable the applicant to complete it. The committee is college-wide and compares candidates from fields it cannot judge technically, so the most useful letters (a) explain the work in terms a scholar from another field will understand, (b) give one or two concrete examples — a result you saw me obtain, a problem I solved without direction, a piece of work you would not have expected at my stage — and (c) state plainly where I stand relative to other early-career researchers you have known.

## Points that would help
1. **Original thought:** the theoretical work on material constitution and mereological pluralism (two papers submitted to *Synthese* with Prof. Grüninger) — that it is a genuine contribution to philosophy as well as to computer science, and that the idea of treating robot datasets as models (or non-models) of a mereology is new.
2. **Rigor and discipline:** the ISO/IEC 21838-4 contribution and the practice of machine-checking every claim about an ontology; what it shows about persistence and the ability to work in an international standards body at an early career stage.
3. **Capacity to complete the program:** the move from theory to deployed systems (patents, production data-integration work, the founded companies) and why it predicts that the audit (Objective 2) and learning (Objective 3) components will actually be built and released.
4. **Independence and fit:** the proposed program is my own — it uses the verification methodology on a problem, datasets and experiments that none of my supervisors has worked on — and it is a three-year program designed around a Cornell host and the Sage School of Philosophy, not a continuation of a Toronto project.
