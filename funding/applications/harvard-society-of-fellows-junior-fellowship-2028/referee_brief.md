About [~10 — registry figure, unverified] fellows are elected each year across all fields; the Senior Fellows who choose them come from every faculty, so the letter must persuade a historian and a mathematician equally.

**What you would be asked to do.** Submit the nomination yourself through the online form at https://socfell.onlinesubmissionportal.com/nomination/ **(confirmed URL)** — the Society does not accept nominations by email, and candidates cannot nominate themselves **(confirmed)**. The form requires **(confirmed, https://socfell.fas.harvard.edu/nomination-procedure)**: (1) "a letter of nomination [with] a thorough assessment of the candidate's work and promise, i.e. a full letter of recommendation, ideally addressing how this candidate ranks in comparison with other outstanding graduate students known by the nominator"; (2) my complete contact information including current residential address and email [I will supply]; (3) the names, institutions and emails of three additional referees who have agreed to write [I will supply]. You cannot also be one of the three referees **(confirmed, FAQ)**. No Harvard affiliation is required of the nominator **(confirmed, https://socfell.fas.harvard.edu/faq/who-can-nominate-me-junior-fellowship)**. No length limit for the letter was found [check the portal]. The Society's FAQ calls the nomination letter "the most important letter in a candidate's file" and says it should come from "the individual who knows your work best".

**When.** Nominations for the July 2027 cohort closed in early August 2026 [1 or 4 August — the Society's pages differ]; the July 2028 window is expected to open about May 2027 and close in the first week of August 2027 [unverified — I will confirm the date on 1 May 2027]. I completed all PhD requirements in March 2025, so I would be about three years past at July 2028. I have asked the Society whether such a candidate is considered [answer: ______]. If you proceed, the letter should say plainly what the years since the doctorate produced — the two Synthese papers, [the Harvard work], the standard — so that the gap reads as a record rather than a delay.

**What reviewers look for.** No rubric is published. The stated standard is "highest caliber of intellectual achievement, i.e., manifesting extraordinary creativity and the capacity to shape the future direction of their chosen fields", and "resourcefulness, initiative, and intellectual curiosity" **(confirmed wording)**. In practice the file is read by senior scholars from all fields who are asking: is this person's mind unusual, is the work real, and would three unconstrained years at Harvard be transformative for them and for a field?

**The four points that would help most.**
1. **The theory, ranked.** The two *Synthese* papers — constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies; validation of mereological pluralism — what is new in them, that I developed the theory and proofs and wrote them, and where the work stands against the other outstanding students and postdocs you have known. 2. **Rigour that a standards body accepted.** The ISO/IEC 21838-4 contribution: what a core contributor to a top-level ontology standard has to be able to do, and that every claim in TUpper is a theorem or a counter-model.
3. **The unusual shape of the record.** Theory, an international standard, patents and production systems at companies I founded, and now physical AI. The Society prizes profiles that do not fit one department; say plainly that this one does not, and why that is a strength rather than a lack of focus.
4. **Independence.** The proposed program is mine — complementary to but distinct from your NSERC programs (Physical Turing Test; Commonsense Cobotics) — [and, if you agree with the characterization, that the Synthese work answers a question — are multiple parthood relations needed? — which your own program left open; confirm wording with Grüninger before it is written]. The proposal now states in one sentence what is distinct (dataset audit; policy training); please contradict it if it is wrong.
