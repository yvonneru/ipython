## B. Statement (one page; background for nominator and referees)

I am a knowledge-representation researcher who works at the boundary of formal ontology and machine learning for physical AI. I build formally verified first-order ontologies of physical objects and their parts, and I use them as specifications, audits and inductive biases for the datasets and policies of robot learning. I have carried formal methods into practice through an international standard, patents and production systems at companies I founded.

The question I most want to answer is old and new at once. Philosophers have asked since the statue and the clay whether one object can have more than one parthood structure. Robot-learning datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, AgiBot World — now record, for hundreds of thousands of objects, three or more part structures at once: kinematic, functional, visual. Nobody has asked whether these structures are compatible, because there has been no formal specification to ask against. With Prof. Michael Grüninger I have written that specification: a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, both under review at *Synthese*, both machine-checked. The proposed studies are the empirical continuation of that theory: verify an ontology of parts as an extension of ISO/IEC 21838-4, audit the datasets against it, and measure what enforcing the axioms does to learning.
