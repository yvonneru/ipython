**Yi Ru — Verified mereologies for the parts of physical objects: from the metaphysics of constitution to the data that robots learn from**

**Where the work stands.** An ontology is a specification of what a vocabulary means, stated as first-order axioms so that every claim about it is a theorem or a counter-model. My work is on mereology, the ontology of parts and wholes, and on carrying it from philosophy into standards and working systems. Classical mereology assumes a single parthood relation; the puzzles of material constitution (the statue and the clay) arise because ordinary objects seem to demand more than one. With Michael Grüninger I have treated constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies and shown that the resulting pluralism is machine-checkable: consistency and non-triviality are established with the Prover9 prover and the Mace4 model finder, and the mapping is proved to preserve the structure it must [1, 2]. Given two part decompositions of one object, the theory says what must hold for them to be compatible. I am a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology [3], and my doctoral thesis developed an architecture in which a verified ontology governs the data, the learned models and the simulation layer of an AI system [4].

**The question.** Robot-learning datasets — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility [6], GAPartNet [7], AgiBot World [8], Open X-Embodiment [9], DROID [10] — annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. A "part" is a segmentation label in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third; no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood, no principled mapping relates labels across datasets, and pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. Poor transfer and brittle generalization to new object categories are observed but never measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. The metaphysician's question (when are two decompositions of one object compatible?) and the engineer's question are the same question. *Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

**What I would do.** Three studies, one a year, each settled by theorem, measurement or experiment.

*Year 1 — the ontology as specification.* I will axiomatize a modular first-order ontology of object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper, integrated with the Process Specification Language [11] for state change under manipulation, and verify it in the strict sense: consistency and non-triviality proved, the models of the axioms characterized up to isomorphism and compared with the intended ones. Every annotation pattern the ontology must accept or reject is a question the axioms must answer. Two questions are open and decidable: do kinematic and functional decompositions require distinct parthood relations, or is one definable from the other? Is material constitution, in the sense of [1], needed to relate a visual segmentation to an articulated link? Representation theorems relating each dataset's annotation schema to the ontology make cross-dataset mappings meaning-preserving by proof.

*Year 2 — the ontology as audit.* A two-tier pipeline — Datalog/SMT rules for the bulk of the data, first-order proving for residual hard cases, "unknown" a first-class outcome — will translate each dataset's annotation hierarchies into ontology instances and check them: the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot data. I will release violation rates by dataset and axiom family, corrected annotations, the proven mappings and a merged ontology-aligned corpus. Whether violations cluster by object category or by annotation source is a fact about the data physical AI is built on that no one now knows.

*Year 3 — the ontology as inductive bias.* Differentiable relaxations of the parthood constraints become auxiliary losses and constraint-guided augmentation; evaluation in the SAPIEN simulator [6] reports, beside task success, how often a policy's inferred object structure violates the axioms, with ablations by axiom family. Three hypotheses: ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories; violation rate predicts task failure before it occurs; pooled ontology-aligned data transfers across part vocabularies. If time allows, the audit is repeated on medical-imaging part labels and CAD assemblies.

**What exists and what does not.** Formal mereology is mature as philosophy [12, 13], and its upper-ontology descendants (BFO, DOLCE, TUpper) are standardized as parts of ISO/IEC 21838; but they have been verified without being validated against data: no one has asked whether they suffice to axiomatize what annotators record. Neuro-symbolic learning turns logical constraints into differentiable losses [14], but not, so far, with a verified parthood ontology as the constraint or verification in the evaluation loop. The program is my own: it grows from the constitution theory, of which I am first author, and from my thesis, and it is distinct from Prof. Grüninger's Toronto programs on ontologies for cobotics, which neither audit datasets nor train policies [confirm this characterization with Prof. Grüninger].

**Why the Society.** The work sits between philosophy, logic, computer science and robotics and needs three unconstrained years and interlocutors from all four — a philosopher to ask whether the axioms I verify are the ones humans use, a mathematician to ask whether the representation theorems are the right ones. It offers in return a verified theory of parts contributed to the COLORE ontology repository [15] and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 32, the first audit of the data physical AI is built on, and public tools. The philosophical result — constitution as a mapping between mereologies — becomes testable against [hundreds of thousands of annotated part instances — confirm figure from the datasets]; the engineering result, if the axioms improve learning, becomes an argument about what human commonsense knowledge of parts contains.
