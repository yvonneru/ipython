**Yi Ru — Verified mereologies for the parts of physical objects: from the metaphysics of constitution to the data that robots learn from**

**1. Recent progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what a set of terms means, stated as axioms in first-order logic so that every claim about it is either a theorem or a counter-model. My work is on the ontology of parts and wholes — mereology — and on carrying it from philosophy into standards and working systems.

*Constitution and pluralism.* Classical mereology assumes a single parthood relation; the puzzles of material constitution (the statue and the clay, the ship and its planks) arise because ordinary objects seem to demand more than one. With Michael Grüninger I have treated constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies and shown that the resulting pluralism is machine-checkable: the axioms are stated in first-order logic, consistency and non-triviality are established with the Prover9 theorem prover and the Mace4 model finder, and the mapping is proved to preserve the structure it is required to preserve [1, 2]. Given two part decompositions of the same object, the theory says exactly what must hold for them to be compatible.

*Standards.* I am a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology, the first top-level ontology adopted internationally with a complete first-order axiomatization and machine-checked verification [3].

*Systems.* My doctoral thesis developed an architecture in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer of an AI system [4]. As a founder I have shipped ontology-governed data-integration systems at production scale and am first inventor on [number — confirm] patents in three-dimensional recognition and indoor modelling.

**2. Objectives.** Robot-learning datasets — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility [6], GAPartNet [7], AgiBot World [8], Open X-Embodiment [9], DROID [10] — annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. A "part" is a segmentation label in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. No dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies, brittle generalization to new object categories — are observed but have never been measured, because there has been no formal specification to measure against. The metaphysician's question (when are two decompositions of one object compatible?) and the engineer's question are the same question. The studies I propose are motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

The program has three objectives. **O1 — Ontology as specification**: axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper, integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [11] for state change under manipulation. **O2 — Ontology as audit**: translate the annotation hierarchies of the major datasets into instances of the ontology and check them, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot data and provably meaning-preserving mappings between datasets. **O3 — Ontology as inductive bias**: use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies and to report, alongside task success, how often a policy's inferred object structure violates the axioms.

**3. State of the field.** Formal mereology is mature as philosophy [12, 13] and its upper-ontology descendants (BFO, DOLCE, TUpper) have been standardized as parts of ISO/IEC 21838, yet they have been verified without being validated against data: no one has asked whether they suffice to axiomatize what annotators actually record. On the other side, robot datasets carry no formal semantics at all; the only ontological analysis of PartNet-style decompositions is a set of open questions in Grüninger's program [14]. Neuro-symbolic learning has shown that logical constraints can be turned into differentiable losses [15], but never with a verified parthood ontology as the constraint, and never with verification in the evaluation loop.

**4. Method.** Ontologies will be designed with the lifecycle methodology of the COLORE repository [16] and verified in the strict sense: the models of the axioms are characterized up to isomorphism and compared with the intended models. Competency questions come from the datasets themselves — every annotation pattern that the ontology must accept or reject is a question the axioms must answer. I distinguish verification (do the axioms have the intended models?) from validation (are the intended models the right ones for the data?), and the program alternates between the two.

*Theme 1 — Parts (O1). Which parthood relations do robot datasets presuppose, and are they jointly consistent?* Project 1.1 axiomatizes rigid, articulated, functional and assembly parthood as TUpper modules and proves consistency, non-triviality and the relationships between modules (Prover9/Mace4). Project 1.2 proves representation theorems relating each dataset's annotation schema to the ontology, so that cross-dataset mappings are meaning-preserving by proof. Open questions: Do kinematic and functional decompositions require distinct parthood relations, or is one definable from the other? (Y1) Is material constitution, in the sense of [1], needed to relate a visual segmentation to an articulated link? (Y1)

*Theme 2 — Audit (O2). What are current models actually trained on?* Project 2.1 builds a two-tier pipeline: Datalog/SMT rules for the bulk of the data (millions of part instances), first-order theorem proving for the residual hard cases, with "unknown" as a first-class outcome. Project 2.2 releases the violation rates and patterns per dataset, corrected annotations, the proven mappings, and a merged ontology-aligned corpus. Open questions: What fraction of part instances in each dataset violate the axioms, and which axiom families are violated? (Y1–Y2) Do violations cluster by object category or by annotation source? (Y2)

*Theme 3 — Learning (O3). Does enforcing the axioms improve generalization?* Project 3.1 develops differentiable relaxations of the parthood constraints as auxiliary losses and constraint-guided augmentation for under-represented categories. Project 3.2 adds verification-in-the-loop evaluation in SAPIEN [6] [+ second simulator], with ablations by axiom family. Hypotheses: ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories; violation rate predicts task failure; pooled ontology-aligned data transfers across part vocabularies. Open questions: Does the violation rate predict failure before it occurs? (Y2–Y3) Does the audit method transfer to part labels in medical imaging and to CAD assemblies? (Y3)

**5. Why the Society.** The work sits between philosophy, logic, computer science and robotics, and needs three unconstrained years and interlocutors from all four. In return it offers a verified theory of parts contributed to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, the first audit of the data that physical AI is being built on, an interpretable failure signal for learned policies, and public data and tools. The philosophical result — that constitution is a mapping between mereologies — becomes testable against a hundred thousand annotated objects; the engineering result — that the axioms improve learning — becomes an argument about what human commonsense knowledge of parts contains.
