## A. For the expert reviewers

**What the fellowship is.** The Alexander von Humboldt Foundation's Research Fellowship for postdoctoral researchers funds a self-chosen research project of 6–24 months at a German host institution, for researchers of any nationality who completed the doctorate less than four years before applying. I am applying for a 12-month stay [confirm] with Prof. [host name] at [institute], to begin [month] 2027 [confirm]. There is no fixed deadline; I am submitting by [early November 2026] for the selection committee meeting of [March 2027].

**What the Foundation asks of you.** Two expert reviews are required, from important collaborative partners and/or academics from my own institute and other institutions, if possible also from outside the country where I now work; the review from my doctoral supervisor is mandatory. [Confirm on the portal whether reviewers upload the review directly on a Foundation link or send it to me for upload; expected: the Foundation emails you a link after I enter your name.] There is no fixed form or length; a review of one to two pages on letterhead, signed, is customary. Please write in English or German. I would be grateful for the review by [date — four weeks before submission].

**What the committee weighs.** The selection committee (15–20 researchers from various fields) decides on the basis of the two independent reviews and the host's statement, on my academic record commensurate with career stage, and on the originality and independence of the research proposal. The Foundation states that "the originality of the research proposal is a crucial criterion" and that the applicant must have developed the proposal themselves; that the project must not be a direct continuation of the doctoral thesis; and that returning to the same academic environment is generally judged as a lack of independent development. No numerical weights are published. Reviews that name a concrete result the reviewer saw the applicant obtain, and that place the applicant plainly relative to other researchers at the same stage, carry the most weight with a committee drawn from many fields.

**The four points that would help most.**
1. The theoretical work on material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies and on mereological pluralism (two Synthese submissions, 2026): that it is mine, that it is machine-checked rather than proposed, and why it matters for knowledge representation.
2. The ISO/IEC 21838-4 (TUpper) contribution: what it shows about rigour and about the ability to work to a standards body's requirements.
3. The move from theory to deployed systems (patents; production data-integration systems at companies I founded): why that predicts delivery of the audit pipeline and the data releases within twelve months.
4. Independence and the German stay: the proposed programme is my own, distinct from my thesis and from my supervisors' programmes; the host's knowledge-based robotics environment is the right and new place to validate it; and I am a person who will build a lasting collaboration rather than a visitor.

**Attached.** Research outline (~5 pp), statement texts, CV and publication list.
