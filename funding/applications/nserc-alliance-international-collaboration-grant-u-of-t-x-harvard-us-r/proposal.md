## 1. Relevance and expected outcomes

An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification that declares what terms a system uses and what they mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies as first-order theories and verifies them: we characterize the models of an ontology up to isomorphism and determine whether they are the intended models [1, 2]. This methodology produced the COLORE repository of 2,580 verified ontologies, the Process Specification Language (PSL) ontology, and the upper ontology TUpper, published as ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 [3]. Dr. Yi Ru, a core contributor to that standard and now a postdoctoral researcher in [the international collaborator's] group at Harvard, developed with me a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies and a validation of mereological pluralism [4, 5]: verified first-order theories that state exactly when two part decompositions of the same object are compatible.

That question is now an engineering problem. Robot-learning datasets — PartNet [6], PartNet-Mobility in SAPIEN [7], GAPartNet [8], PartNet-Ensembled [9], and the real-robot corpora AgiBot World [10], Open X-Embodiment [11] and DROID [12] — annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each defines "part" operationally: a segmentation label in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. No dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies, brittle generalization to new object categories — are observed but unmeasured, because there has been no formal specification to measure against. My Discovery Grant programs have shown that benchmark datasets carry implicit ontological presuppositions and logical errors, and that no prior ontological analysis of them exists [1, 2]. The project is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

The joint project has three objectives.

1. **Ontology as specification.** Axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts — rigid, articulated, functional and assembly parts — as an extension of TUpper integrated with PSL for state change under manipulation. Verification — consistency, non-triviality, module relationships, and representation theorems relating the ontology to each dataset's annotation schema — uses Prover9 and Mace4 within the COLORE methodology, so that cross-dataset label mappings are meaning-preserving by proof.
2. **Ontology as audit.** A two-tier pipeline — Datalog/SMT rules for millions of part instances, first-order theorem proving for the residual hard cases — translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances and checks them. Output: the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations, proved label mappings, a merged ontology-aligned corpus, and a reusable audit toolkit, which [Harvard] applies to [a second annotation domain in the collaborator's group] to test the method's generality.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias.** Training methods that use the ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies: differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses; constraint-guided augmentation of ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories; and verification-in-the-loop evaluation that reports a policy's ontology-violation rate alongside task success. Hypotheses: ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories; violation rate predicts task failure; pooled ontology-aligned data transfers across part vocabularies. Simulation in SAPIEN [and a second simulator] in Toronto; [real-robot validation on the collaborator's platform] at Harvard.

**Expected outcomes and milestones.** Year 1: verified parts ontology released under an open licence and submitted to COLORE; representation theorems for five annotation schemas; audit pipeline and first audit of PartNet, PartNet-Mobility and GAPartNet. Year 2: audit extended to AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment and DROID; public release of corrected annotations, mappings and the merged corpus; pooled-data and augmentation experiments; audit of the [Harvard] domain. Year 3: constraint-based training and verification-in-the-loop evaluation; [real-robot] validation; contribution of the parts module to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; two machine-learning papers, one robotics paper and one applied-ontology paper.

**Benefit to Canada.** The verified ontology, audit toolkit and aligned corpus are open infrastructure built and maintained in Canada, in the only laboratory with working tool support for verified first-order ontologies. They give Canadian robotics and manufacturing users a machine-checkable statement of what their training data means — evidence of the kind the EU AI Act will require of AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028 [16]. The project keeps Canada's contribution to ISO/IEC 21838 current as physical AI becomes its main application, and brings a University of Toronto-trained researcher back from a leading US group to train Canadian students.

## 3. Training

The grant funds one postdoctoral fellow (Dr. Ru, three years), [one MASc (Y1–Y2) and one PhD student (Y1–Y3)] and [two undergraduate summer students] in Toronto. Each open research question is assigned to a trainee, as in my Discovery Grant programs.

*Theme 1 — Parts as specification (Objective 1).* Which parthood relations are implicit in the decompositions of PartNet, PartNet-Mobility and GAPartNet, and are they distinct mereologies with independent axiomatizations? **PDF.** Does the axiomatization of articulated parts require the PSL process ontology, or is a static mereotopology sufficient? **PhD1.** Are the schema translation definitions entailed by the ontology, so that each cross-dataset mapping is a theorem? **MASc1.**

*Theme 2 — Parts as audit (Objective 2).* What fraction of the hard cases left by the Datalog/SMT tier need a first-order prover, and which axiom families cause them? **MASc1.** Do the violation patterns differ between simulated-asset datasets and real-robot corpora? **PDF, with [Harvard] trainee.** Does the audit toolkit transfer to [the Harvard domain] without new axioms? **PhD1 during exchange visit.**

*Theme 3 — Parts as inductive bias (Objective 3).* Which axiom families, when relaxed as auxiliary losses, improve generalization to unseen categories? **PDF.** Does ontology-violation rate predict task failure before it occurs? **PhD1.** Do policies trained on the pooled ontology-aligned corpus transfer across part vocabularies? **PDF and [Harvard] trainee.**

**Training environment.** Trainees learn first-order axiomatization and theorem proving, large-scale data engineering (adapters for LeRobot, RLDS and ROS 2), simulation-based policy learning, and standards work; the PhD student attends [one] ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 meeting as the laboratory's delegate. Each graduate student spends [four to eight weeks] a year in the Harvard group. Dr. Ru co-supervises the graduate students on the ontology and audit components and is mentored toward an independent faculty career, leading the papers and the data release.
