## 1. Relevance and expected outcomes

An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification that declares what terms a system uses and what they mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies as first-order theories and verifies them by characterizing their models up to isomorphism; it produced COLORE, an open repository of 2,580 first-order ontologies, the Process Specification Language (PSL), and the upper ontology TUpper, published as ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 [1]. With Dr. Yi Ru, [a core contributor to that standard — confirm role wording], I developed verified theories of material constitution and mereological pluralism that state exactly when two part decompositions of one object are compatible [2, 3].

That question is now an engineering problem. Robot-learning datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, [PartNet-Ensembled], [AgiBot World], Open X-Embodiment and DROID [4–9] — annotate objects as part hierarchies, each defining "part" operationally: a segmentation label, a movable link, an actionable region. Nothing requires a dataset or a learned model to satisfy the axioms of parthood, labels cannot be mapped across datasets in a principled way, and pooled training silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. Poor transfer and brittle generalization to new categories are observed but unmeasured, because there has been no formal specification to measure against. My Discovery program [title — confirm it is funded and active] asks which mereotopologies the ShapeNet and PartNet decompositions presuppose; this project extends that analysis to articulated and real-robot corpora, automates it at corpus scale, and uses the ontology in training. The long-term challenge is:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

The Canadian component has three objectives.

1. **O1 — Ontology as specification.** Axiomatize a modular first-order ontology of object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as a TUpper extension integrated with PSL for state change under manipulation; prove consistency, non-triviality and module relationships with Prover9 and Mace4 in the COLORE methodology [10]; and prove representation theorems relating it to each dataset's annotation schema, so that every cross-dataset label mapping is a theorem.
2. **O2 — Ontology as audit.** A two-tier pipeline (Datalog/SMT rules for the bulk of part instances; first-order proving for residual hard cases) translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances and checks them. Outputs: the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations, proved mappings, a merged ontology-aligned corpus, and an audit toolkit tested for generality on [the international team's annotation domain].
3. **O3 — Ontology as inductive bias.** Differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses; constraint-guided augmentation for underrepresented categories; verification-in-the-loop evaluation reporting a policy's ontology-violation rate beside task success. Hypotheses: (H1) ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories; (H2) violation rate predicts task failure; (H3) pooled aligned data transfers across part vocabularies. Each is tested against an unconstrained baseline with ablations by axiom family, in SAPIEN [and a second simulator]; [real-robot validation on the international team's platform — option A only].

**Milestones.** M8: ontology v0.1 proved consistent; representation theorems for PartNet, PartNet-Mobility and GAPartNet; first quantitative audit. M12: ontology v1 and audit toolkit released openly and submitted to COLORE. M18: real-robot corpora audited; corrected annotations and merged corpus released; audit/data paper. M24: H3 and augmentation results; methods paper. M30: H1–H2 results; robotics-venue paper. M36: parts module contributed to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 32; applied-ontology paper.
