# Brief for the international collaborator (this program has no referees)

Alliance International Collaboration grants take no reference letters. The external person whose input decides the application is the **international academic collaborator**, who must supply a letter of support, a biographical sketch, evidence of their own funding, and a sensitive-technology attestation. This brief is written to send to that person — [Dr. Ru's Harvard supervisor, name] — with email 2. If a U of T Robotics Institute co-applicant is added, send them the "What reviewers score" and "Points that help" sections and ask for a CCV.

## What the program is

NSERC Alliance International Collaboration grant: up to CAD 100,000 per year for up to three years to a Canadian university researcher (Prof. Michael Grüninger, University of Toronto) for a defined joint project with an academic researcher at a foreign university or public research institution. The foreign side is not paid by NSERC. The grant funds the Canadian component of a project that the foreign collaborator's own national granting agency has newly funded: the collaborator's award letter must be dated no earlier than 120 days before the Canadian application is submitted, a copy of the international proposal and the official award letter are included in the NSERC application, and cost-sharing is 1:1 — the Canadian request cannot exceed the annual amount of your award, converted to Canadian dollars [NSERC instructions as read from search summaries; the U of T research office will confirm]. An older existing grant does not qualify. Deadline 25 November 2026, 8 pm ET; the University of Toronto's internal review is about ten days earlier, so all collaborator materials are needed by **30 October 2026**.

## What the form asks of the collaborator

0. **The international project and its award letter** — the gating item: the title, agency, award number, award-letter date (on or after 28 July 2026 for a 25 November submission) and annual amount of the national-agency award whose project this application joins, plus a copy of the funded proposal and the award letter. If no such award is in hand by 30 October, the application cannot be made this round.
1. **Letter of support** (one to two pages, on letterhead): your role in the project, what your group contributes (datasets, platform, compute, co-supervision, hosting of visiting students), and the award above, with its annual amount and end date. A draft you can adapt is in statement.md, Part B.
2. **Biographical sketch**: NSERC requires one for each international collaborator named in the proposal. If NSERC gives no template, your NIH or NSF biosketch is the natural basis; it must list the grant counted as your contribution.
3. **Statement of annual support**, in a form Prof. Grüninger can convert to Canadian dollars and cite in the budget justification: agency, title, number, award date, annual amount, currency, end date, and the share directed to the joint work.
4. **Attestation for Research Aiming to Advance Sensitive Technology Research Areas**: the project is in advanced AI and robotics, so every collaborator named in the proposal completes the Government of Canada attestation confirming no affiliation with a listed research organization of concern. It is a short form; the research office will send the link.
5. [Whether the letter is uploaded by the applicant or emailed to NSERC — the research office will confirm.]

## What reviewers score

The three-page proposal has five fixed sections (relevance and expected outcomes; collaboration; training; equity, diversity and inclusion; budget). NSERC evaluates applications internally on two criteria: **impact and benefit to Canada** (60%) and **quality of training in Canada** (40%, including EDI in training), and funds fundable applications in rank order until the round's budget is committed. The collaboration section is where your letter matters: reviewers look for distinct, reciprocal roles, a reason this specific collaborator was chosen, and a funded international project for the Canadian side to join.

## The four points that would help most

1. **What your group contributes that Toronto cannot supply** — [real-robot validation / a second annotation domain / evaluation methodology / named datasets]. Be concrete: name the platform, the dataset, the grant.
2. **Why the verification methodology matters to your own work** — one or two sentences on what an audited, ontology-aligned dataset or a machine-checkable specification would change in your pipeline.
3. **Dr. Ru as the bridge** — what Dr. Ru has done in your group, and that Dr. Ru will remain connected after moving to Toronto in [June 2027].
4. **Training** — that you will host Toronto graduate students for short visits of [two to four] weeks a year and take part in monthly joint meetings; reviewers weight training *in Canada* at 40%, so the visits are framed as brief and the training as Toronto-based.
