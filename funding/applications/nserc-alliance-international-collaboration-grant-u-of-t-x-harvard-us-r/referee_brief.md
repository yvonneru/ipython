# Brief for the international collaborator (this program has no referees)

Alliance International Collaboration grants take no reference letters. The external person whose input decides the application is the **international academic collaborator**, who must supply a letter of support, a biographical sketch, evidence of their own funding, and a sensitive-technology attestation. This brief is written to send to that person — [Dr. Ru's Harvard supervisor, name] — with email 2. If a U of T Robotics Institute co-applicant is added, send them the "What reviewers score" and "Points that help" sections and ask for a CCV.

## What the program is

NSERC Alliance International Collaboration grant: up to CAD 100,000 per year for up to three years to a Canadian university researcher (Prof. Michael Grüninger, University of Toronto) for a defined joint project with an academic researcher at a foreign university or public research institution. The foreign side is not paid by NSERC; it contributes through its own funding, which must be at least equal to the amount requested from NSERC, and the amount NSERC will award is sized to that contribution. Deadline 25 November 2026, 8 pm ET; the University of Toronto's internal review is about ten days earlier, so all collaborator materials are needed by **30 October 2026**.

## What the form asks of the collaborator

1. **Letter of support** (one to two pages, on letterhead): your role in the project, what your group contributes (datasets, platform, compute, co-supervision, hosting of visiting students), and the grant that supports your side, with its annual amount and end date. A draft you can adapt is in statement.md, Part B.
2. **Biographical sketch**: NSERC requires one for each international collaborator named in the proposal. If NSERC gives no template, your NIH or NSF biosketch is the natural basis; it must list the grant counted as your contribution.
3. **Statement of annual support**, in a form Prof. Grüninger can convert to Canadian dollars and cite in the budget justification: agency, title, number, annual amount, currency, end date, and the share directed to the joint work.
4. **Attestation for Research Aiming to Advance Sensitive Technology Research Areas**: the project is in advanced AI and robotics, so every collaborator named in the proposal completes the Government of Canada attestation confirming no affiliation with a listed research organization of concern. It is a short form; the research office will send the link.
5. [Whether the letter is uploaded by the applicant or emailed to NSERC — the research office will confirm.]

## What reviewers score

Merit is judged on five equally weighted sections of a three-page proposal: relevance and expected outcomes; collaboration; training; equity, diversity and inclusion; and budget. Fundable applications are then ranked 60% on **impact and benefit to Canada** and 40% on **quality of training in Canada**. The collaboration section is where your letter matters: reviewers look for distinct, reciprocal roles, a reason this specific collaborator was chosen, and evidence that the foreign side has resources committed.

## The four points that would help most

1. **What your group contributes that Toronto cannot supply** — [real-robot validation / a second annotation domain / evaluation methodology / named datasets]. Be concrete: name the platform, the dataset, the grant.
2. **Why the verification methodology matters to your own work** — one or two sentences on what an audited, ontology-aligned dataset or a machine-checkable specification would change in your pipeline.
3. **Dr. Ru as the bridge** — what Dr. Ru has done in your group, and that Dr. Ru will remain connected after moving to Toronto in spring 2027.
4. **Training** — that you will host Toronto graduate students for [four to eight] weeks a year and take part in monthly joint meetings; reviewers weight training in Canada at 40%, and time in your group is part of that training.
