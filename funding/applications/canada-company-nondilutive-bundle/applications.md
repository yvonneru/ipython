### Project B — Verification Engine: dataset-scale checking of heterogeneous robot episodes with grounding under abstention, physics residuals and calibrated uncertainty

**Field:** Information technology — data engineering, machine learning, formal verification. **Project code:** [B]. **Personnel (Canadian payroll):** [CTO; robotics lead; 3D asset lead; ENG1; founder fraction]. **Contractors (Canadian):** [cloud/inference provider; none other]. **Assistance to deduct:** [IRAP contribution on these salaries once the agreement is signed; YEP on ENG1 if applicable].

**B.1 Technological uncertainties.**
The Engine is an offline pipeline — ingestion → scene graph → SMT check — over data from teleoperation, world models and simulation, with adapters for LeRobot, RLDS, ROS 2/MCAP, OpenUSD/USDZ, PLY and JSON, and with physics residuals under an observation contract and conformal calibrated uncertainty that controls abstention. Standard practice (inspection and labelling tools; runtime safety filters; per-dataset validation scripts) does not check per-record data against a machine-proven axiom set, and does not produce evidence that re-verifies offline. The uncertainties:

- *U-B1 — Grounding with abstention.* What fraction of frames from heterogeneous episodes can be grounded automatically — identities, affordances, relations proposed by vision-language and graph models with per-candidate confidence, constrained by type, timing, geometry and task checks — at a fixed audited accuracy, without inventing a physical fact where evidence (friction, contact) is missing; and which unknown types dominate the residual.
- *U-B2 — Two-tier checking at scale.* Whether bulk checks in the compiled subset (Datalog/SMT), with residual hard cases routed to first-order theorem proving, can process 10,000 episodes across partner and open datasets and assign every record a disposition (valid success; valid failure with its phase; correctable defect with a derived version; unresolved with the exact missing evidence; unsupported synthetic) within [time/compute budget], and what the parthood- and contact-consistency violation rate is by dataset and by axiom family. No prior measurement exists because no formal specification existed to measure against.
- *U-B3 — Physics residuals.* Whether a residual of the form r_t = ‖M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇) + g(q) − τ − Jᵀf‖ can be evaluated on real robot recordings once an observation contract (synchronized states, torque and contact signals, calibration, declared units, frames, handedness, rotation order) is enforced; whether layered geometry (scale, extent, penetration, transforms, reachability), kinematics (position, velocity, acceleration, timing) and dynamics checks can be calibrated on held-out measurements; and whether a cheaper surrogate (PINN or neural-operator proxy) reduces checking cost at the same false-accept rate without hiding out-of-domain error.
- *U-B4 — Calibrated abstention.* Conformal prediction gives population coverage under stated assumptions, not a safety probability for one trajectory. The right coverage target, the grouping by episode, and the abstention policy that keeps the false-accept rate within the customer's tolerance were unknown.
- *U-B5 — Episode contract across formats.* Whether a single episode contract (episode_id, asset_ids, robot configuration, sensor references, timestamps, frame tree, calibration, state/action schema, task phases, contact events, outcome, interventions, rights, real/synthetic lineage) can be defined so that original recordings stay immutable, conversion is an explicit versioned operation, and support is reported per adapter version, across six formats with incompatible conventions. Only the contract design and the loss-of-information analysis are claimed; routine format conversion is excluded.
- *U-B6 — Augmentation validity.* A generator and its checker may share blind spots. Whether controlled variants generated around coverage gaps (lighting, viewpoint, pose, calibrated material/contact, task order, failures) can be validated on held-out real views, measured contacts and unseen layouts, and whether ontology-consistent augmentation improves generalization to unseen object categories under a fixed budget (SAPIEN [and a second simulator]), was unknown.

Targeted: (i) the first quantitative measurement of parthood- and contact-consistency in robot-learning corpora, by dataset and axiom family, with localized findings and dispositions; (ii) a grounding procedure whose abstention is calibrated and whose unknowns are typed; (iii) an observation contract and calibrated residual tolerances for real robot recordings, with a measured answer on surrogate cost; (iv) a versioned episode contract with documented information loss per format; (v) evidence, positive or negative, on augmentation validity. ### Recent progress
An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of the intended models of a vocabulary, and it is verified by characterizing those models and comparing them with the models the axioms actually have. My laboratory's work provides the tools this project reuses: the COLORE repository (2,580+ first-order ontologies with verified module relationships); the Process Specification Language; TUpper, the top-level ontology standardized as ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023; the PRAxIS ontologies; and the programs "Ontologies for the Physical Turing Test" and "Commonsense Cobotics" [current NSERC support — confirm no overlap]. ### Objectives
Robot-learning datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment, DROID — annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels, but each defines "part" operationally; no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled mapping between vocabularies; and pooled training silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. The consequences are observed but unmeasured because no specification has existed to measure against. The long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

The proposed research program has three primary objectives: (1) **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with PSL, with representation theorems relating it to the annotation schemas of the major corpora; (2) **Ontology as audit** — a two-tier pipeline (Datalog/SMT for bulk; theorem proving for residual cases) that translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances, measures parthood consistency across five datasets and the partner's asset base, and produces provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset mappings and a merged, ontology-aligned corpus; (3) **Ontology as inductive bias** — training methods that use the ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies (differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses; constraint-guided augmentation; verification-in-the-loop evaluation reporting ontology-violation rate beside task success), tested in SAPIEN [and a second simulator] with ablations by axiom family.
