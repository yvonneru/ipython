## Auditing and Aligning Hierarchical Annotations against a Verified Specification: Part-Level Data Quality for Physical AI and Medical Imaging

**1. Recent Progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what terms a data resource uses and what those terms mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects as theories in first-order logic and verifies them, so that every claim about the ontology is a theorem or a counter-model, and then uses the verified theory as a specification against which data can be checked. As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 (TUpper) [1], I worked on the axiomatization, modular organization and machine-checked verification of a top-level ontology adopted as an international standard [confirm role wording]. With Prof. Michael Grüninger (University of Toronto) I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two papers submitted to Synthese [2, 3]; the theory states exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible. My doctoral research developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer [4]. Alongside the doctorate I co-founded Uing Technologies, where I built ontology-based representations of objects, indoor environments and spatial relations for structured 3D datasets used to train embodied-AI models and led nine invention patent applications in 3D recognition and indoor modelling [reconcile granted and pending patent counts with the CV before submission], and YourTable Inc., where I led development of a personalized recommendation system. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School, in [laboratory/department], working on [one sentence].

**2. Objectives.** The largest datasets now driving physical AI — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility in SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10] and DROID [11] — annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each dataset defines "part" operationally: a segmentation label in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. No dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; and pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies, brittle generalization to new object categories — are reported but have not, to my knowledge, been measured against any formal specification of parthood, because none has been written down. The same structure recurs in the health sciences: anatomical segmentation datasets annotate the body as a hierarchy of parts against partonomies whose axioms are stated informally, if at all, and, to my knowledge, no audit exists of whether the annotations satisfy them. The program is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the hierarchical annotations on which data-driven systems are trained be shown, at dataset scale and by machine-checked proof, to satisfy the axioms of parthood that their annotators presuppose — and does enforcing those axioms measurably improve what is learned from them?*

The proposed research has three primary objectives:
1. **Ontology as specification.** Axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for state change under manipulation, and prove representation theorems relating it to the annotation schemas of the major datasets.
2. **Ontology as audit — the data-science core.** Build a scalable two-tier pipeline that translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances and checks them against the specification, and deliver from it a quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets (to my knowledge the first), corrected annotations, provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings, a merged ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable open-source toolkit; then apply the toolkit to anatomical part labels in medical imaging.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias.** Measure, with matched statistical comparisons, whether ontology-aligned pooled data and ontology-derived training constraints improve generalization of part-aware manipulation policies, and whether the ontology-violation rate of a policy's inferred object structure predicts task failure.

**3. Literature Review.** Part-level datasets have grown from static segmentation hierarchies (PartNet [5]) to articulated and actionable parts (PartNet-Mobility [6], GAPartNet [7]) and to real-robot corpora with part-level or affordance annotations (AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10], DROID [11]); none states the semantics of its part relation and, to my knowledge, none has been checked against a formal theory of parthood [14]. Formal mereology supplies candidate axioms — reflexivity, antisymmetry, transitivity, supplementation, and the constitution relations that distinguish a whole from what makes it up [13] — but the applied-ontology community has verified upper ontologies (BFO, DOLCE, TUpper, all standardized within ISO/IEC 21838) without validating them against real datasets [14]. Data-quality research on hierarchical and structured data addresses integrity constraints and schema matching, but the constraints are typically expressed in database logics too weak to state parthood axioms, so unintended models are admitted and violations that are entailed by the axioms are not detected. Neuro-symbolic learning offers mechanisms — semantic loss [15], constraint-guided augmentation — to inject logical knowledge into training, but has been applied to propositional or lightly relational constraints rather than to verified first-order theories of physical structure. In medical imaging, anatomical partonomies exist as ontologies [17] and large multi-organ segmentation datasets exist [18], but the two are not machine-checked against each other. The gap the program fills is a general, verified, scalable method for auditing hierarchical annotations against a first-order specification, together with the first measurements that method makes possible.

**4. Methodology.** The ontologies will be designed and evaluated using the ontology lifecycle methodology of [16] and the modular repository techniques of [19], as practised in the COLORE repository (2,580+ first-order ontologies in Common Logic [20]). All ontologies will be verified: we characterize the models of an ontology up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models. Competency questions come from the application: "Is this part decomposition internally consistent?", "Are these two decompositions of the same object compatible?", "Which labels in dataset A can be mapped to dataset B without changing what is entailed?" Consistency and non-triviality are established with Prover9 and Mace4 [21]; the bulk audit is compiled to Datalog and SMT (Z3 [22]); UNKNOWN and timeout are first-class outputs, never silently treated as pass.

**Theme 1: Specification.** *Given the kinematic, functional and visual part decompositions used by existing datasets, specify a verified first-order theory that says what a part is and when two decompositions of the same object are compatible.*

*Project 1.1: Parts ontology.* Four modules — rigid parts (classical extensional mereology with supplementation), articulated parts (parthood relative to a kinematic joint state, via PSL fluents), functional parts (parthood relative to an affordance), assembly (parthood that changes under attach/detach activities) — axiomatized as extensions of TUpper, with module relationships (conservative extension, definable equivalence) proved rather than asserted. Open Research Questions: Does each dataset's part relation satisfy strong or only weak supplementation, and does the answer differ between segmentation-derived and kinematics-derived labels? (Y1) Which module relationships hold between the articulated and functional mereologies, and are they definably equivalent over any class of objects? (Y1)

*Project 1.2: Constitution mappings and representation theorems.* Using the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2], specify for each pair of decomposition types the mapping that must exist for them to be compatible, and prove representation theorems relating each dataset's annotation schema to the ontology so that label mappings are meaning-preserving by proof. Open Research Questions: For which dataset pairs does a parthood-preserving mapping exist for every object, and what is the counter-model when it does not? (Y1)

**Theme 2: Audit — data systems design for hierarchical annotations.** *Given millions of part instances annotated under independently designed schemas, measure at scale how often and in what patterns the annotations violate the specification, and construct a corrected, aligned corpus.*

*Project 2.1: Two-tier audit pipeline.* Adapters translate each dataset's native format (PartNet JSON hierarchies, SAPIEN URDF joint trees, GAPartNet actionable-part labels, LeRobot/RLDS episodes) into ontology instances. Tier one compiles the decidable fragment of the axioms to Datalog rules and SMT queries and checks every instance; tier two hands residual cases (existential axioms, constitution mappings) to Prover9/Mace4 with a time budget. Each violation is stored with provenance — dataset, object, axiom, proof or counter-model — so results are reproducible and auditable. Adapters are built in priority order: PartNet, PartNet-Mobility and GAPartNet first (Y1 M2–5; they share an object lineage and are the datasets Theme 3 needs), then the real-robot corpora; the minimum outcome at M12 is a released audit of the first three, so the program degrades gracefully if the episode-format adapters take longer than planned. Open Research Questions: What fraction of violations are decided in tier one, and does the residual grow with hierarchy depth? (Y1) Can violation patterns be clustered into a small number of annotation-practice errors versus genuine ontological disagreements? (Y1)

*Project 2.2: Measurement design and statistical analysis.* The audit yields, per dataset, a violation rate by axiom family with bootstrap confidence intervals over objects, stratified by object category and hierarchy depth; across datasets, the fraction of label pairs for which a meaning-preserving mapping exists. Corrections are proposed where a violation has a unique repair and flagged otherwise; corrected annotations, mappings and the merged corpus are released with the audit log. The unit of analysis is the object: each object contributes a violation indicator per axiom family, modelled with a hierarchical (mixed-effects) logistic model in which object category and hierarchy depth are fixed strata and source dataset and annotating laboratory are random effects, so that inter-source disagreement is estimated rather than averaged away; intervals come from the object-level bootstrap, and the pre-specified comparisons (simulated versus real-robot corpora; segmentation- versus kinematics-derived labels) are corrected for multiplicity. The analysis plan, together with the hypotheses of Theme 3, will be pre-registered before the full audit runs (Y1 M5). Mentor 1 [Statistics / CS] would review the model and the release. Open Research Questions: Do violation rates differ significantly between simulated (PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora after stratifying by category? (Y1–Y2) Does inter-source disagreement concentrate in the articulated and functional modules? (Y2)

*Project 2.3: Health-sciences extension — anatomical part labels.* The same toolkit is applied to a multi-organ medical-image segmentation resource [with Mentor 3 — e.g., a dataset such as TotalSegmentator [18] against an anatomy partonomy such as the Foundational Model of Anatomy [17]; confirm resource and access], asking whether the label hierarchy satisfies the partonomy's axioms and where segmentation practice and anatomical theory diverge. This project tests the method's generality on a domain HDSI names, and produces a data-quality report that imaging groups can act on. Open Research Questions: Do anatomical segmentation labels satisfy the same supplementation axioms as engineered-object labels, or does anatomy require a distinct mereology module? (Y2) Which violation classes are shared between robotics and imaging, indicating annotation-practice rather than domain errors? (Y2)

**Theme 3: Learning — measured effect of the specification on what is learned.** *Given ontology-aligned data and ontology-derived constraints, determine whether part-aware policies generalize better and whether specification violations predict failure.*

*Project 3.1: Pooled ontology-aligned data.* Train part-aware manipulation policies in SAPIEN [6] [and a second simulator — confirm with Mentor 2] on (a) native pooled data and (b) ontology-aligned pooled data, and evaluate on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories with matched comparisons and confidence intervals, the number of held-out categories and training seeds fixed by a power analysis on a Y1 pilot rather than chosen after the fact. Open Research Questions: Does alignment improve cross-category success, and is the effect larger for categories with high native violation rates? (Y2) Does pooled aligned data transfer across part vocabularies where native pooled data does not? (Y2)

*Project 3.2: Constraints and verification-in-the-loop evaluation.* Differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses [15] on a policy's inferred part relations; constraint-guided augmentation that synthesizes ontology-consistent configurations for underrepresented categories; and an evaluation protocol that reports, alongside task success, the rate at which inferred object structure violates the ontology. Ablations by axiom family. Open Research Questions: Does violation rate predict task failure well enough to serve as an interpretable failure signal, and which axiom families account for any generalization gain? (Y2)

**5. Impact.** In the near term the program delivers a data-science method — specification-based auditing of hierarchical annotations — and the first measurements it makes possible: a public audit of the datasets much of physical AI trains on, corrected annotations and a merged corpus, a toolkit reusable on medical image labels, CAD assemblies and building information models, and an interpretable failure signal for learned policies. For the health sciences it delivers a machine-checked comparison of segmentation practice with anatomical theory. For data provenance it delivers machine-checkable evidence about what a training set asserts, with every violation traceable to an axiom and a proof. Outputs: the verified ontology (open licence; contributed to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 32), the audit release, the toolkit, two machine-learning-venue papers and one robotics- or imaging-venue paper. All axioms, code and data produced under the fellowship will be developed in Harvard repositories and released under open licences; they are separate from, and not owned or restricted by, the company activity disclosed in my statement.

In the longer term the program supplies what HDSI's mandate asks for across schools — a method born in formal ontology, made scalable by data-systems engineering, evaluated statistically, and applied in robotics and medicine — and leaves Harvard with an ontology-aligned data infrastructure that fellows and faculty in statistics, engineering and medicine can build on. It positions me for a faculty career in knowledge representation for engineering and biomedical data systems.
