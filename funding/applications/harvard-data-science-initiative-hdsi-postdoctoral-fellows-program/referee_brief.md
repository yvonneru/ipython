**What the program asks.** No prescribed letter structure is published [verify]. A letter of one to two pages is typical; the committee will read it against the research statement, which leads with the data-science method (a scalable, specification-based audit of hierarchical annotations) and its measured effects in robotics and medical imaging.

**What reviewers weigh (working assumption; HDSI has not published weights).** The quality and originality of the data-science contribution, methodological or methodological plus applied; the applicant's independence and record; fit with the named Harvard faculty and the HDSI community across schools; feasibility within two years; breadth of impact. The committee is known to weigh statistical and computational methodology, so a sentence on my quantitative and engineering competence (the audit pipeline, the measurement design) carries more than a sentence on philosophy. The most useful letters give one or two concrete examples — a result you saw me obtain, a problem I solved without direction, a piece of work you would not have expected at my stage — and state plainly where I stand relative to other postdoctoral researchers you have known.

**Points that would help.**
1. The theoretical work on material constitution and mereological pluralism (two papers under review at Synthese), and why a machine-checkable account of when two part decompositions are compatible is a data-integration result, not only a philosophical one.
2. The ISO/IEC 21838-4 contribution and what it shows about rigour and the ability to work in a standards body.
3. The move from theory to deployed data systems (nine invention patent applications and ontology-based 3D datasets at Uing Technologies; the recommendation system at YourTable; the systems built on the founding team of MICAS; the AXIOMALITY verification engine, at internal-test stage) and why that predicts delivery of the audit pipeline and the released corpus at dataset scale. Please describe only what you have seen directly.
4. Independence and breadth: the program is my own, distinct from my thesis and from any mentor's grant; the method transfers from robotics to anatomical labels in medical imaging, which is the kind of cross-school reach HDSI asks for.
