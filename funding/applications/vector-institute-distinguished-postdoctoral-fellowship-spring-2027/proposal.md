## Knowledge-Grounded Representation Learning for Physical AI: Verified Ontologies of Object Parts as Specification, Audit and Inductive Bias

**1. Recent progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what terms an agent uses and what those terms mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects as theories in first-order logic, verifies them so that every claim is a theorem or a counter-model, and uses them to govern what learned systems are trained on and what they are permitted to infer. Three lines of work lead to this proposal.

- **A verified top-level ontology adopted as an international standard.** As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 (TUpper) [1], I worked on the axiomatization, modular organization and machine-checked verification of a top-level ontology adopted by ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 [confirm exact role wording]. TUpper showed that a top-level ontology can be given the rigour of a mathematical theory rather than the informal glosses that characterize most upper ontologies; it is the foundation on which the parts ontology proposed here is built.
- **A machine-checkable theory of parts.** With Prof. Grüninger I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two first-author papers submitted to Synthese [2, 3]. The axioms are first-order; consistency and non-triviality are established with automated provers and model finders; the mapping is proved to preserve the parthood structure it is required to preserve. The theory states exactly when two part decompositions of the same object are compatible — the question robot datasets pose, where kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object co-exist without any statement of how they relate.
- **Ontology-governed learning systems in production.** My doctoral research at the University of Toronto developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer [4]. As a co-founder of two technology companies I designed and shipped ontology-governed data-integration and recommendation systems that ran in production, and I am first inventor on [number — confirm] patents in 3D recognition, indoor modelling and automatic reconstruction. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School, [one sentence on the work and any machine-learning publications]. My publications to date are in knowledge-representation venues [FOIS, JOWO, ICBO — confirm]; my machine-learning experience is in the learned components of the doctoral architecture and in deployed systems. This proposal is built to close that gap: Theme 3 is designed with, executed in, and reported from [sponsor's] group at machine-learning venues.

**2. Objectives.** Robot manipulation policies increasingly operate on part-level representations of objects — segmented, articulated, functional parts — learned from PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10] and DROID [11]. These representations are learned from annotations that carry no formal semantics: nothing requires the parts a model infers to form a consistent whole, nothing relates the part vocabularies of different datasets, and nothing lets a policy reason about why a part decomposition is wrong. Each dataset defines "part" operationally — a segmentation mask in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third — and pooled training silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. The result is a familiar pattern in embodied learning: strong in-distribution performance and brittle transfer to new object categories, with failures diagnosed by inspection rather than by principle. These consequences have never been measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. The program is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms during training improve generalization?*

To address this challenge, the program has three objectives:
1. **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for state change under manipulation, with representation theorems relating it to the annotation schemas of the major datasets.
2. **Ontology as audit** — translate the annotation hierarchies of those datasets into ontology instances and check them automatically, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations, provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings and a merged, ontology-aligned corpus.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias** — the machine-learning core: training methods that use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies (differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses; constraint-guided augmentation), and a verification-in-the-loop evaluation protocol that reports ontology-violation rate alongside task success, with the effect on generalization to unseen object categories measured in simulation.

**3. Literature review.** Formal mereology [13] supplies axiom systems for parthood, and TUpper's mereotopology modules have been verified [1, 14], but no verified ontology distinguishes the rigid, articulated, functional and assembly senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data. Part-level datasets [5–11] carry no formal semantics, and ontological analysis of vision benchmarks [14] found logical errors in ShapeNet and PartNet but produced neither a scalable audit nor a cross-dataset alignment. On the learning side, neuro-symbolic methods that impose logical constraints on neural models — semantic loss [15], DL2-style constraint losses [21], DeepProbLog [22] — have been demonstrated on classification and structured prediction, not on the part structure that manipulation policies infer; part-aware manipulation [7] and cross-embodiment policy learning [10] take part labels as given. No evaluation protocol reports whether a policy's inferred object structure is consistent with any specification, so claims about cross-category generalization cannot be separated from annotation artefacts in the training data.

**4. Methodology.** The ontologies will be designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of the Semantic Technologies Laboratory [16] and verified with the COLORE repository methodology [17]: we characterize the models of each module up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models, using Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [18]. Competency questions come from the datasets: every annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute. I would be resident at Vector for the full term. Themes 1 and 2 draw on the Semantic Technologies Laboratory's verification tooling (co-sponsor); Theme 3, the majority of effort from month 8, is executed with [sponsor's] group at Vector, where the compute and the policy-learning expertise sit. The program is my own: it is complementary to, but distinct from, the Laboratory's NSERC programs on ontologies for the Physical Turing Test and commonsense cobotics, which do not address robot-learning datasets or policy training. Open questions are milestones tagged by fellowship year (Y1, Y2).

**Theme 1: Parts (Objective 1).** *Which parthood relations are implicit in robot-learning annotations, and can they be axiomatized as verified extensions of TUpper?* **Project 1.1 — Parts ontology.** Axiomatize rigid parthood, articulated parthood (links and joints), functional parthood (affordance-bearing regions) and assembly parthood as separate Common Logic modules [19], each verified for consistency and non-triviality, with representation theorems characterizing their models. **Project 1.2 — Constitution mappings.** Apply the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2, 3] to state when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible, and integrate PSL so that parthood changes under manipulation (detaching a lid, opening a drawer) are fluents. *Open questions:* Are the four parthood relations mutually definable, or do they require distinct axiomatizations? (Y1) Is every dataset's part hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not? (Y1)

**Theme 2: Audit (Objective 2).** *What is the rate and pattern of parthood-axiom violation in the datasets that physical-AI models are trained on?* **Project 2.1 — Two-tier audit pipeline.** Translate annotation hierarchies into ontology instances through adapters for each dataset format; check the bulk (millions of part instances) with Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [20], and send residual hard cases to first-order theorem proving; UNKNOWN and timeout are first-class outputs. **Project 2.2 — Cross-dataset alignment.** Derive label mappings that are provably meaning-preserving under the constitution theory, correct violating annotations, and release a merged, ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable audit toolkit. This is also a study of what current models are actually trained on. *Open questions:* Which fragment of the ontology compiles to SMT without losing violations the prover finds? (Y1) Do violation rates differ systematically between simulated (PartNet-Mobility) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora? (Y1–Y2)

**Theme 3: Learning (Objective 3).** *Does enforcing the parthood axioms during training improve a manipulation policy's generalization to unseen object categories, and does violation of those axioms predict failure?* **Project 3.1 — Constraint-based training.** A part-aware policy — one whose perception head predicts part segmentation and part–part relations on which the action head conditions [backbone to be chosen with the sponsor, e.g. a diffusion-policy or vision-language-action backbone — confirm] — is trained with an auxiliary loss that is a differentiable relaxation of the parthood axioms: each axiom is compiled to a semantic-loss or fuzzy-logic term over the predicted relation probabilities [15, 21], so that a batch in which a predicted part is both contained in and disjoint from another is penalized in proportion to the violation. Constraint-guided augmentation uses Mace4 to generate ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories and instantiates them in SAPIEN [6]. **Project 3.2 — Verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** The Theme 2 pipeline is run on the policy's inferred object structures at evaluation time, and ontology-violation rate is reported alongside task success. Experiments use held-out PartNet-Mobility categories in SAPIEN and [second simulator — confirm], matched comparisons with confidence intervals, and ablations by axiom family. Hypotheses: (H1) ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen categories; (H2) violation rate predicts task failure and serves as an interpretable failure signal; (H3) policies trained on pooled, ontology-aligned data transfer across part vocabularies. *Open questions:* Which axiom families carry the generalization gain under ablation? (Y2) Is violation rate a better predictor of failure than the policy's own confidence? (Y2) Does the gain survive transfer from PartNet-Mobility to real-robot corpora? (Y2)
