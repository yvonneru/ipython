## A. Cover letter (1 page)

[Date — February 2027]

Postdoctoral Fellowship Committee
Vector Institute for Artificial Intelligence
Toronto, Ontario

Dear Committee,

I am applying to the Distinguished Postdoctoral Fellowship for the Spring 2027 cohort, with [Prof. name — Vector Faculty Member / Faculty Affiliate, department, institution] as proposed faculty sponsor and Prof. Michael Grüninger (University of Toronto, Mechanical and Industrial Engineering) as co-sponsor. My research is knowledge-grounded representation learning for physical AI: I build formally verified ontologies of physical objects and their parts and use them as specifications, audits and inductive biases for learned manipulation policies.

I completed all requirements of my PhD in Information Engineering (Department of Mechanical and Industrial Engineering; research in artificial intelligence and knowledge representation) at the University of Toronto in March 2025, and have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School since [month year], working on [one sentence]. I am a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology; first author, with Prof. Grüninger, of two papers under review at Synthese that give a machine-checkable theory of when two part decompositions of one object are compatible; and co-founder of two technology companies where I shipped production data-integration and recommendation systems, with [number — confirm] patents. My publications are in knowledge-representation venues; my machine learning has so far been built rather than published, and the third part of this program is written to change that in the sponsor's group.

The attached research statement has three parts. The first is a verified ontology of object parts, extending the ISO standard I helped develop. The second is an audit of the part-level datasets on which robot-learning models are trained — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled, AgiBot World — which no one has checked against any formal specification; it yields the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in those datasets, and public data. The third is the machine-learning core and the reason I am applying to Vector: training methods that compile the ontology's axioms into differentiable losses and constraint-guided augmentation for part-aware policies, and an evaluation protocol that reports ontology-violation rate alongside task success. The hypotheses are falsifiable — ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen object categories; violation rate predicts failure; ontology-aligned data pools across part vocabularies — and testing them needs the representation-learning expertise, compute and critical community Vector provides. The program sits in Vector's stated directions of safe and trustworthy AI and physical AI in robotics [re-check wording before submission].

I would take up the fellowship from [April 2027 or later — confirm Vector's start window], resident in Toronto for its full term. [Only if Vector asks about concurrent awards: I have also applied for the NSERC Canada Postdoctoral Research Award; if both succeed I will follow Vector's guidance.] I would be glad to present this work to the committee or to interested faculty. Thank you for your consideration.
