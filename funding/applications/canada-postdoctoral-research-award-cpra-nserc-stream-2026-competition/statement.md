### Part II — Most significant contributions

The three contributions below are those I consider most significant to my field and most relevant to the proposed research. For each I state my role, the significance of the work, and its bearing on the program of work in the attached outline.

**1. Axiomatization and verification of TUpper as an international standard (ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023).** Top-level ontologies are the foundation on which domain ontologies are built, yet before this standard no top-level ontology with a complete first-order axiomatization and machine-checked verification had been adopted internationally. As a core contributor within ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, I worked on the axiomatization of TUpper, its modular organization, and the formal verification of consistency and of the relationships between its modules. [Specify precisely which modules and which verification results you were responsible for.] The significance is twofold. Scientifically, TUpper demonstrated that a top-level ontology can be given the same standard of rigour as a mathematical theory — every claim is a theorem or a counter-model — rather than the informal glosses that characterize most upper ontologies. Practically, the standard gives implementers a reference theory against which domain ontologies can be proven, not merely asserted, to be consistent extensions. This is directly the methodology the proposed program applies: the physical-object parts ontology of Theme 1 (Objective 1) in the outline will be built as a verified modular extension of TUpper, and my familiarity with the standard, its editors and SC 42 procedures is what makes the dissemination plan (contribution back to the standard) realistic.

**2. A formal theory of material constitution and mereological pluralism (with M. Grüninger; two papers submitted to Synthese, 2026).** Classical mereology assumes a single parthood relation; the puzzles of material constitution (the statue and the clay, the ship and its planks) arise because ordinary objects seem to demand more than one. My work treats constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies, and shows that the resulting pluralism can be validated by the same verification techniques used for ontologies: the axioms are stated in first-order logic, consistency and non-triviality are established with automated provers and model finders, and the mapping is proved to preserve the parthood structure it is required to preserve. [Insert one sentence with the paper's central theorem in plain language.] I developed the theory and the proofs and wrote both papers; Prof. Grüninger contributed the verification framework and the placement of the results within the COLORE repository. The significance for knowledge representation is that the account is not a philosophical proposal but a machine-checkable theory that a reasoning system can use: given two part decompositions of the same object, the theory says exactly what must hold for them to be compatible. That is precisely the question posed by robot datasets, where kinematic, functional and visual part decompositions of the same object co-exist without any statement of how they relate. Project 1.2 (constitution mappings) and Theme 2 (audit) of the outline are the empirical continuation of this theoretical work.

**3. Knowledge-system architecture for machine-learning-based simulation, with industrial deployment.** My doctoral research at the University of Toronto developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data, model and simulation layers, and demonstrated it with machine-learning-based simulation in engineering settings. [Insert one sentence naming the application domain of the thesis and the principal quantitative result.] In parallel, as a founding team member of MICAS (cross-border direct-to-consumer commerce) and co-founder of YourTable Inc., I designed and shipped production recommendation, CRM/ERP and influencer-allocation systems whose data models were ontology-governed, and as co-founder and CEO of Uing Technologies I built structured 3D physical-world datasets and ontology-based representations of objects, indoor environments and spatial relationships for embodied AI, and led nine invention patent applications covering 3D recognition, indoor modelling and automated 3D reconstruction. [Insert the confirmed count of granted patents and their jurisdictions.] The significance is that the ontology-first architecture has been shown to work outside the laboratory, at production scale, with real data-integration problems — the same class of problem, at the same scale, that Theme 2 addresses for robot datasets. This contribution is also the basis of my claim to be able to deliver Theme 3: I have integrated formal constraints with learned models in deployed systems before, and the engineering discipline required (versioned axioms, regression tests on inferences, reproducible pipelines) is one I have practised rather than proposed.

## C. Justification for eligibility of proposed research (optional attachment, 1 page)

Read only by NSERC staff, not by reviewers. Included because the program touches learned policies and object manipulation, areas where an application could conceivably be routed elsewhere.

The proposed research falls within the natural sciences and engineering. Its subject matter is the formal representation of physical objects and their parts, the verification of first-order theories by automated reasoning, and the use of those theories to audit engineering datasets and to constrain machine-learned manipulation policies. The methods are those of knowledge representation, mathematical logic, software engineering and robotics; the data are engineering datasets of three-dimensional objects and robot trajectories; the evaluation is by theorem proving, dataset statistics and simulation experiments on manipulation tasks.

The research is not health research and does not involve human participants, animals, clinical data or biological material; it is therefore outside the CIHR mandate. It is not social-sciences or humanities research: although the theory of parts and wholes has a philosophical literature, the program uses that literature as a source of axioms to be verified and applied, not as an object of philosophical inquiry, and its outputs are verified ontologies, software, aligned datasets and measured effects on robot policy generalization. The two submitted papers cited in the outline are formal results in knowledge representation that were submitted to a philosophy-of-science journal because that journal publishes formal mereology; the proposed work is their engineering application.

The applicant's doctorate is in Information Engineering; the proposed supervisor holds an NSERC-funded [confirm] laboratory in a faculty of engineering; and the outputs will be disseminated in AI and robotics venues and through ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42. On every criterion NSERC uses to determine the appropriate agency — subject matter, methodology, training environment and dissemination — this is NSERC research.
