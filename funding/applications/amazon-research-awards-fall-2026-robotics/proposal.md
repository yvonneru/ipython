## Abstract

Robot-learning datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled, AgiBot World — annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels, and manipulation policies are trained on them in pooled form. Each dataset defines "part" operationally, none is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood, and there is no principled way to map labels across datasets, so pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across vocabularies, brittle generalization to new categories — are observed but never measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. We propose to supply one and enforce it by automated reasoning. (1) We will axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts as an extension of TUpper (ISO/IEC 21838-4) integrated with the Process Specification Language, verified with Prover9, Mace4 and SMT. (2) A two-tier audit — Datalog/SMT for the bulk, first-order theorem proving for residual hard cases — will check annotation hierarchies against the ontology on AWS, yielding the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations and provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset mappings. (3) We will use the ontology as an inductive bias on part-aware manipulation policies and test in SAPIEN whether ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen categories. [Robotics call: *The result is a cross-embodiment part schema whose consistency is a theorem.* / Automated Reasoning call: *The result is a deployed application of SMT and theorem proving to a data-quality problem at the scale of millions of part instances.*] All axioms, mappings, code and corrected annotations will be released openly, with an AWS runbook so any dataset holder can rerun the audit.

## 1. Significance of the research and prior work

Physical AI is being built on a handful of large part-level datasets [5–9] whose annotation schemas were designed independently: a "part" is a segmentation label in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. Nothing requires the parts a model infers to form a consistent whole, nothing relates the vocabularies of different datasets, and nothing lets a policy reason about why a decomposition is wrong. Fulfilment-scale manipulation meets far more categories than any dataset contains, so transfer of part representations to unseen categories is decisive — and today it is settled by inspection. We propose to settle it by proof.

- **Verified first-order ontologies and COLORE (PI).** The PI's research axiomatizes ontologies as first-order theories and develops techniques for their design and verification [13, 14]. COLORE, an open repository in Common Logic (ISO/IEC 24707) [16], holds 2,580 first-order ontologies in hierarchies, verified by relating their models to known mathematical structures [14]. The PI's mereotopology, time, location and units-of-measure ontologies (FOUnt, Distinguished Paper Award, FOIS 2018 [confirm citation]) are incorporated in TUpper, ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 [1]; the PSL ontology [11] supplies the verified theory of activities and state change reused here. The PI's NSERC Discovery program ["Commonsense Cobotics" — confirm title] asks which mereotopologies the part decompositions of ShapeNet and PartNet presuppose. **This proposal takes that question to the robot-manipulation datasets and to data scale — an audit of millions of part instances and its use in policy training — which the Discovery grant neither funds nor could run without AWS-scale compute.**
- **A machine-checked theory of parthood under constitution (named postdoc, with the PI).** Classical mereology assumes one parthood relation; ordinary objects seem to demand more. Dr. Ru's theory treats material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies, verified by Prover9/Mace4 consistency and non-triviality proofs and a proof that the mapping preserves the required structure (two papers submitted to Synthese, 2026 [2, 3]). Given two part decompositions of one object, it says exactly what must hold for them to be compatible — the question robot datasets pose, where kinematic, functional and visual decompositions co-exist with no statement of how they relate.
- **Ontology-governed systems in practice (named postdoc).** Dr. Ru's thesis [4] developed an architecture in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer; Dr. Ru is a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 within ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 [confirm role wording]. As co-founder of Uing Technologies, Dr. Ru shipped ontology-based representations of objects and indoor environments for embodied AI and led nine invention patent applications in 3D recognition and reconstruction.

**Relevance to Amazon.** For automated reasoning, the audit is an industrial-scale application of SMT and theorem proving in which UNKNOWN and timeout are first-class outputs and the compiled fragment must be shown not to miss what the prover finds. For robotics, the deliverable is a cross-embodiment part schema whose consistency is a theorem, plus a failure signal — ontology-violation rate — computable before a policy is deployed. The same adapters and checker apply to any part-annotated corpus, including internal ones, and machine-checkable evidence about training data is what regulators are beginning to require (EU AI Act Annex I covers AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028).

**Readiness.** The constitution theory is written and under review [2, 3]; the verification toolchain is in daily use in the PI's laboratory; the five datasets are public [confirm each licence]; the SMT tier compiles axioms already written. What the award funds is the audit at scale and the measured effect on learning.

## 2. Technical approach

The long-term question: *can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?* Three objectives:

1. **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with PSL for state change under manipulation.
2. **Ontology as audit** — translate the annotation hierarchies of PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled and AgiBot World into ontology instances, check them automatically, and derive cross-dataset mappings that are meaning-preserving by proof.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias** — use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies and measure the effect on generalization to unseen categories, as a pilot in months 8–12.

Ontologies are designed with the PI's methodology [13, 14] and verified by characterizing the models of each module against the intended models. Competency questions come from the audit itself ("Is any part annotated both as contained in and disjoint from another?" "Which PartNet-Mobility links correspond to GAPartNet actionable parts?").

**Theme 1 — Parts (Objective 1).** *Specify a verified theory of which each dataset's part vocabulary is a definable extension.* 1.1 axiomatizes rigid, articulated, functional and assembly parthood as Common Logic modules over the TUpper mereotopology; consistency and non-triviality by Prover9/Mace4; module relationships (conservative extension, definable equivalence) proved or refuted by counter-model. 1.2 applies the constitution mappings of [2, 3] to kinematic, functional and visual decompositions, yielding representation theorems that state when two decompositions are compatible. 1.3 integrates PSL so that parthood change under manipulation (detach, insert, open) is an activity occurrence with axiomatized preconditions and effects. *Open question (M3):* are the PartNet-family parthood relations definable in one mereology, or do they require distinct mereologies related by constitution mappings?

**Theme 2 — Audit (Objective 2).** *Given an annotation hierarchy and the ontology, decide which axioms it violates, at dataset scale, with an explicit UNKNOWN.* 2.1 writes adapters from each native schema (and the LeRobot and RLDS formats of real-robot corpora) to ontology instances with per-part provenance. 2.2 builds the two-tier checker: the decidable fragment is compiled to Datalog and SMT (Z3 [17]) and run over millions of part instances on AWS; undecided instances go to Prover9/Mace4 under a time budget, and timeouts are reported as UNKNOWN, never as passed. 2.3 derives cross-dataset mappings from the Theme 1 theorems, proves each meaning-preserving, and releases corrected annotations and an ontology-aligned index as patches against the original releases, so that no licence is breached. *Open questions (M6–M7):* does the SMT fragment miss violations the prover finds, and on which axiom families? Is each dataset's violation rate concentrated in particular categories or annotation sources?

**Theme 3 — Learning (Objective 3).** *Does a policy trained under the ontology generalize better, and does its violation rate predict its failures?* 3.1 compiles the parthood axioms to differentiable relaxations (semantic-loss terms after [12, 18]) as auxiliary losses on the part relations a policy infers. 3.2 uses Mace4-generated models as constraint-guided augmentation of underrepresented categories in SAPIEN [6]. 3.3 defines a verification-in-the-loop protocol that reports, alongside task success, the rate at which a policy's inferred object structure violates the ontology. Hypotheses, tested on one part-aware backbone [named by the Robotics Institute advisor] with matched comparisons across held-out PartNet-Mobility categories and ablations by axiom family: H1, ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen categories; H2, violation rate predicts task failure; H3, pooled, ontology-aligned data transfers across part vocabularies.

**Use of AWS.** The Datalog/SMT tier is embarrassingly parallel and runs on EC2 batch fleets with datasets staged in S3; the prover tier runs under explicit time budgets; Theme 3 training and SAPIEN evaluation use GPU instances [types and hours sized with the advisor — see budget]. The toolkit ships with a runbook so any dataset holder can rerun the audit in their own account.
