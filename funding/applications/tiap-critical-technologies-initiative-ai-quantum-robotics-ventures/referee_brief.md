# Brief for supporters — TIAP Critical Technologies Initiative

TIAP takes no referee letters. What its investment committee weighs instead, according to the registry and the program text, is (a) evidence of institutional linkage — a University of Toronto Innovations & Partnerships Office (IPO) disclosure or a faculty letter placing the technology's origin at a member institution — or, failing that, (b) a third-party match term sheet, plus (c) design-partner letters that show commercial intent and the data the project will run on. This brief is for the three kinds of supporter: Prof. Grüninger (and the IPO), a candidate match investor, and the design partners. Send it with the relevant email from emails.md.

## What the program is

Toronto Innovation Acceleration Partners (TIAP) is the venture-building organization affiliated with the University of Toronto and its partner teaching hospitals. Its FedDev Ontario-supported Critical Technologies Initiative funds early-stage ventures using AI, quantum computing and/or robotics with **up to CAD 200K: a grant of up to CAD 100K plus a dilutive match** from TIAP (for ventures from member institutions) or from a third party (for others), applied to technology development and executive advisory. Intake is rolling; applicants submit a project proposal with budget and milestones; an investment committee decides; initiation is roughly 3–4 months after application [all scout-stated; verified on the official page only as search snippets — see README]. TIAP's stated focus is health-science commercialization, so the proposal carries a regulated-health design-partner track.

## What the committee scores (search snippet, 2026-09-25 — verify on tiap.ca; weights unpublished)

"Team's expertise and execution plan, as well as market potential, demand, viability, clinical validation, and patient impact." Eligibility: based in Ontario or technology developed in Ontario; fewer than 500 FTE; 1:1 cash match. Because clinical validation and patient impact are scored, the regulated-health design partner is the lead partner and its letter is the most valuable one in this brief.

## What I am asking of each supporter, and by when

**Prof. Grüninger — institutional linkage (by 31 Oct 2026).** Two things. First, a meeting with the IPO, with me, to determine whether the verified parts ontology / knowledge kernel is U of T-created IP under the Inventions Policy; if it is, I will file the invention disclosure and open the licence discussion, and the disclosure record is what TIAP treats as evidence of linkage. Second, a short letter (half a page) stating: that the kernel's theory — TUpper (ISO/IEC 21838-4) and the material-constitution / mereological-pluralism results — was developed in the Semantic Technologies Laboratory with you; that I was a core contributor to the standard and first author of the two Synthese submissions; and, if you agree, that the laboratory is willing to act as contractor for kernel verification under a U of T research agreement. Points that would help: (1) the verification standard — every claim is a theorem or a counter-model — and why that distinguishes the kernel from research ontologies such as KnowRob; (2) that no comparable tool-supported verification methodology exists outside the laboratory; (3) that the proposed program is the applicant's own and complementary to your NSERC programs. Please also confirm the conflict-of-interest arrangement if I hold a U of T postdoctoral appointment from spring 2027 while remaining CEO.

**U of T Innovations & Partnerships Office (determination by 30 Nov 2026).** The question is narrow: which elements of the kernel were created at U of T (thesis work; the Synthese theory), which are company-authored background technology (kernel specification as compiled, adapters, evidence schema), and which are Uing Technologies' patents. A written IP-ownership map is the deliverable; an introduction to TIAP's program manager, which third-party guides describe as the usual path, would also help.

**Candidate third-party match investor (letter of intent by 31 Dec 2026).** If TIAP does not itself match, the CTI requires a match "from another third-party institution". What TIAP will need to see: an investor identified, the instrument (SAFE or priced equity), the amount matching the grant (up to CAD 100K), and conditionality on the grant award. What the investor gets: first instrument in a round sized to an 18-month operating plan; a company with dated gates (three co-development partners and 10,000 verified episodes by Q1 2027; Passport v1 recognized by Q2 2027; five paying customers and two renewals by month 18); and the pre-registered experiments in the proposal as the diligence plan.

**Design partners — [hospital network; medical-device company; robot OEM / platform company] (letters by 30 Nov 2026).** A one-page letter stating: the task family and data the partner will supply (format, episode count, rights and consent scope); the acceptance test the partner will run against its own baseline; the commercial intent (pilot pricing USD 25–50K for 8–12 weeks, conversion to annual deployment on acceptance); and, for the health partner, the validation or quality-system record the Passport must support. Points that would help: that data stays in the partner's environment under role-based, logged access; that the partner owns the acceptance criteria; that negative results are preserved.
