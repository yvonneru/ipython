I was educated entirely at the University of Toronto, first as an undergraduate in Industrial Engineering with a minor in Engineering Business (BASc 2015; President Scholarship, Dean's Honours List, 5T3 Alumni Scholarship) and then as a doctoral student in Information Engineering in the Department of Mechanical and Industrial Engineering, where I completed all requirements in March 2025 through the Fast Track PhD. My thesis developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified formal ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer; parts of that work were contributed to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School, in [laboratory/department], working on [one sentence on the current work].

My path through higher education was not a straight line, and I think that is its main value. Between the two degrees I co-founded YourTable Inc., a recommendation-system company incubated by the University of Toronto, the Schulich School of Business and Imperial College London, which received the Lo Family Social Venture Fund and represented Canadian startups at Collision 2019. During the doctorate I served on the founding team of MICAS, where I led a team of more than one hundred people and built the CRM, ERP and warehouse systems on which a business with more than USD 50 million in first-year revenue ran, and I co-founded Uing Technologies, which builds ontology-based 3D datasets for embodied AI and shipped one of the first spatial-computing applications on the Apple Vision Pro. I returned from each of these to formal logic with a sharper question: a theory that cannot be checked by a machine and cannot survive contact with real data is not yet finished. That conviction is why my doctoral work ended in an international standard rather than a preprint, and why the research I propose treats the datasets that robot-learning models are trained on as objects that can be audited against a specification.

My experience of the university has also been as a participant in its governance and its labour. I was Vice President of the graduate students' association of my department (AMIGAS), the department's representative on the University of Toronto Graduate Student Union, and a steward and representative for CUPE 3902, the union of teaching assistants and sessional instructors. In each role the work was the same: listening to students whose circumstances differed from mine, carrying their concerns to people with authority, and staying with a problem until it was resolved. As a teaching assistant in Engineering Economics and Accounting, Database Systems, Knowledge Modeling and Management, and Resource and Production Modeling, I taught students who arrived with very different preparation, and I learned to explain a formal idea three ways before expecting it to land. I chaired a session at the IISE Annual Conference in 2017 and sit in ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 meetings [confirm designation], where agreement is reached across languages and national delegations; I work in English and Mandarin and have presented to academic and industry audiences in North America and Asia.

These experiences shape the faculty member I intend to become. I would run a laboratory in which formal rigour and working systems are held to the same standard, and in which students who come from industry, from other disciplines, or from institutions without a formal-methods tradition can enter through the data and the tools rather than only through the logic.
