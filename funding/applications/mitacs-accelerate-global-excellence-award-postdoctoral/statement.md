I am writing to open a Mitacs Accelerate Global Excellence Award application to be held at the University of Toronto under Prof. Michael Grüninger, with [Partner organization] as partner, starting [May 2027 or later]. I currently reside in the United States as a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School; I completed my PhD in Information Engineering at the University of Toronto in March 2025; and I intend to relocate to Toronto for the award.

My research is on knowledge-grounded representation learning for physical AI: I build formally verified ontologies of physical objects and their parts and use them as constraints, audits and inductive biases for learned manipulation policies. I am a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology; I co-authored, with Prof. Grüninger, a formal theory of material constitution now under review at Synthese; and I have built and shipped production data-integration systems as a founder of technology companies. The combination — formal theory that is machine-checked, and systems that run — is what this project brings to the partner.

The attached proposal describes a two-year project in three parts: a verified ontology of object parts; an audit of the part-level datasets that robot-learning models are trained on, which no one has yet checked against any formal specification, delivered to the partner as a report on its own data and a toolkit it keeps; and training methods that use the ontology to improve cross-category generalization of manipulation policies. The partner receives usable results in year one.

I would be grateful for the pre-review of the partner and of my eligibility, the GEA application forms, and your guidance on the partner contribution, the conflict-of-interest disclosure (I am founder of a robotics-data company, AXIOMALITY, which is not proposed as the partner) and on how the award interacts with a tri-agency award I have applied for separately.
