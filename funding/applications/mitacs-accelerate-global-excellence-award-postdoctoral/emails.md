Subject: Co-funded 2-year Mitacs postdoc: an audit of your part-level robot data against a verified specification

Dear [name],

I am a University of Toronto PhD (AI / knowledge representation), currently at Harvard Medical School, and a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology. I am writing to propose a Mitacs-co-funded project with [Partner organization] and Prof. Michael Grüninger's Semantic Technologies Laboratory at U of T.

The problem: robot-learning datasets annotate objects as hierarchies of parts, but each supplier defines "part" differently, nothing checks that the hierarchies are consistent, and pooled training silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. Nobody has measured this, because there has been no formal specification to measure against.

The project: in year one I audit [Partner]'s part-level data (and the public corpora it is pooled with) against a machine-verified ontology of parts, deliver a quantitative report with corrected annotations and provably meaning-preserving label mappings, and leave you a toolkit you can run on every release. In year two I test whether ontology-consistent training improves policy generalization. The audit runs where your data is; the ontology and toolkit core are open; your data, adapters and report stay yours.

The Mitacs Accelerate Global Excellence Award funds two years at CAD 85,000 per year; the partner's share is [CAD 42,500 + tax per year — verify], with Mitacs funding the rest; the fellow spends [at least 25%] of the time with the partner.
