## 2.1 Research abstract (≤ 200 words)
Robot-learning datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment, DROID — annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each defines "part" operationally; no dataset or learned model must satisfy the axioms of parthood; no principled method maps labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across vocabularies, brittle generalization to new object categories — are observed but never measured, because no formal specification exists to measure against. This project supplies both. I will (1) axiomatize and machine-verify a modular first-order ontology of object parts extending the ISO/IEC 21838-4 top-level ontology TUpper, integrated with the Process Specification Language; (2) build a two-tier audit pipeline (Datalog/SMT for bulk checking, theorem proving for residual cases) that measures parthood consistency in the partner's data and the public corpora, produces provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings, and delivers a reusable audit toolkit to [Partner]; and (3) test whether ontology-consistent training improves generalization of part-aware manipulation policies in simulation. Outputs: an open verified ontology contributed to COLORE and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, a toolkit in production at the partner, and three publications.

## 2.2 Background and problem addressed (Recent Progress and Literature Review)

An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification that declares what terms an agent or application uses and what the terms mean; in this project ontologies are theories in first-order logic whose models are characterized and checked by automated reasoning [1, 2]. My research sits at the boundary of formal ontology and machine learning for physical AI: I build formally verified ontologies of physical objects and their parts, and I use them as specifications, audits and inductive biases for robot-learning datasets and manipulation policies.

**Recent progress.**
- *Top-level ontology as an international standard.* I am a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology, within ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 [3]. TUpper is a modular first-order ontology of time, space, process, material objects and location in which every module is verified: every claim about it is a theorem or a counter-model rather than an informal gloss. [Confirm the exact role wording and the working-group designation.] The parts ontology proposed here is a verified modular extension of TUpper, and my familiarity with the standard and with SC 42 procedures is what makes contribution back to the standard realistic.
- *A machine-checkable theory of material constitution and mereological pluralism.* With Prof. Grüninger I developed a theory in which constitution is a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies, and showed that the resulting pluralism can be validated with the same verification techniques used for ontologies: axioms in first-order logic, consistency and non-triviality established with automated provers and model finders, and the mapping proved to preserve the parthood structure it must preserve. Two papers are under review at Synthese (2026) [4, 5]; I developed the theory and proofs and wrote both papers. The theory answers exactly the question posed by robot datasets: given two part decompositions of the same object — kinematic, functional, visual — what must hold for them to be compatible?
- *Ontology-governed knowledge-system architecture, deployed.* My doctoral thesis developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified formal ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer; elements contributed to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 [thesis title; one-sentence application domain and principal quantitative result]. As co-founder and CEO of Uing Technologies (since 2022) I built ontology-based representations of objects, indoor environments, spatial relationships and interactions for embodied AI, and shipped a consumer spatial application on the Apple Vision Pro; I am first inventor on multiple patents in 3D recognition, indoor modelling and automatic reconstruction [reconcile counts: deck says 13 granted + 9 applications; drafts say 3 Chinese invention patents and 9–10 German utility patents]. As a founding team member of MICAS I built production CRM/ERP/WMS and ML-driven marketing systems at a company that reached more than USD 50 million in annual revenue. The engineering discipline this requires — versioned axioms, regression tests on inferences, reproducible pipelines — is one I have practised, not proposed.
- *The verification methodology and tools.* The Semantic Technologies Laboratory maintains COLORE, the Common Logic Ontology Repository of more than 2,580 first-order ontologies organized into hierarchies, with the design-by-reuse, merging and transfer techniques and the semiautomatic verification pipeline (Prover9 / Mace4) that this project reuses [1, 2, 6, 7].

**Literature review and the gap.** Part-level datasets for physical AI have grown from segmentation benchmarks (PartNet [8]) to articulated-object simulation assets (PartNet-Mobility in SAPIEN [9]), actionable-part corpora (GAPartNet [10]), ensembled part vocabularies (PartNet-Ensembled [11 — confirm citation]) and large real-robot corpora (AgiBot World [12], Open X-Embodiment [13], DROID [14]). In one dataset a "part" is a segmentation label, in another a movable link, in a third an actionable region. There is no shared definition, no within-dataset consistency requirement (a part may be annotated as both contained in and disjoint from another), and no principled cross-dataset mapping. Prior ontological work on these datasets is limited to the observation that their parthood decompositions carry implicit mereotopologies [2, 15]; nobody has audited them against a formal specification. In applied ontology, upper ontologies BFO, DOLCE and TUpper have been standardized as parts of ISO/IEC 21838 and verified, but not validated against domain data at scale [3, 15]. In machine learning, neuro-symbolic methods impose logical constraints as losses [16] but with hand-written rules that carry no verification and no relation to the annotation schemas policies are trained on. Data-quality and data-provenance work in ML documents datasets [17] but does not check them against axioms. The gap this project fills is a verified specification of part structure, an audit method that scales to millions of part instances, and a measured link between specification violations and policy behaviour.

## 2.3 Objectives

The long-term challenge that motivates this program is:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

To address this challenge, the project has three primary objectives:

1. **Ontology as specification (O1).** Axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts — rigid, articulated, functional and assembly parthood — as an extension of TUpper (ISO/IEC 21838-4) integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL, ISO 18629) for state change under manipulation; establish consistency, non-triviality, module relationships and representation theorems relating the ontology to the annotation schemas of the major datasets and of [Partner]'s data.
2. **Ontology as audit (O2, the partner-facing core).** Build a two-tier audit pipeline that translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances and checks them; deliver the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations, provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings, a merged ontology-aligned corpus, and a reusable audit toolkit in production use at [Partner].
3. **Ontology as inductive bias (O3).** Develop and test training methods that use the ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies — differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses, constraint-guided augmentation for underrepresented categories, and verification-in-the-loop evaluation reporting ontology-violation rate alongside task success — and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories in simulation.

These objectives are clustered around three themes: Parts (specification), Audit (measurement and alignment) and Learning (inductive bias).

**Relationship to the supervisor's programs and to the fellow's thesis.** Prof. Grüninger's programs ("Ontologies for the Physical Turing Test"; "Commonsense Cobotics") develop ontologies for commonsense reasoning and robot task and motion planning [2, 15]; this project supplies the verification methodology and COLORE infrastructure but none of its objectives: auditing part-level training corpora and using a verified ontology as an inductive bias for learned policies are the fellow's program, begun in the two Synthese papers [4, 5]. The fellow's thesis addressed knowledge-system architecture and top-level ontology; none of the datasets, methods or experiments proposed here appears in it.

## 2.4 Methodology

All ontologies will be designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of [6] as practised in COLORE [1], and verified: we characterize the models of an ontology up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models. Competency questions come from the application — here, the questions a data engineer at [Partner] or a policy-training pipeline must be able to answer about a part annotation: Is this decomposition consistent? Are these two labels the same part? What must be true of this object for this grasp to be well-defined? Verification (do the axioms have exactly the intended models?) is kept distinct from validation (are the intended models the right ones for the data?), and the datasets play the role of validation instruments. Every open research question below is answerable by a theorem or a counter-model, or by a measured quantity with a confidence interval.

### Theme 1: Parts — the specification (O1)
*Problem statement: Which first-order theories of parthood are implicit in the part decompositions used by robot-learning datasets, and can a single modular ontology axiomatize all of them as verified extensions of TUpper?*

**Project 1.1 — Ontological analysis of annotation schemas.** For each of PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment, DROID and [Partner]'s schema, identify the classes and relations the schema requires, characterize the intended semantics of each relation implicit in the data, and identify which COLORE mereology or mereotopology hierarchies already axiomatize it and which require new modules. Output: a schema-to-ontology mapping table with, for each relation, a definability result or a counter-model showing that no existing module suffices.
Open Research Questions: (a) Are the kinematic (link), functional (affordance) and visual (segmentation) part relations in these datasets definable in a single mereology, or do they require distinct mereologies related by parthood-preserving mappings, as our constitution theory predicts [4, 5]? — Y1 milestone. (b) Which datasets' decompositions violate the transitivity or antisymmetry of parthood, and is the violation an annotation error or evidence of a second, undeclared parthood relation? — Y1 milestone.

**Project 1.2 — Axiomatization and verification of the Parts Ontology.** Design the ontology by reuse and merging [1]: rigid parts (mereotopology modules of TUpper), articulated parts (kinematic-chain and motion modules integrated with PSL fluents so that a hinge is a part whose relative location changes during an activity occurrence), functional parts (affordance modules tied to PSL activity classes), and assembly (multi-object constitution). Verify each module with Prover9 (theorem obligations) and Mace4 (counter-models), state module relationships (conservative extension, definable equivalence, interpretation) as theorems, and prove representation theorems that characterize the models up to isomorphism. Everything is written in Common Logic (ISO/IEC 24707) and contributed to COLORE.
Open Research Questions: (a) Does the axiomatization of articulated parts require the full PSL process ontology, or a definable fragment? — Y1. (b) Are the intended models of the assembly module exactly the structures induced by the constitution mapping of [4]? — Y1/Y2.

### Theme 2: Audit — measurement and alignment (O2)
*Problem statement: Given a corpus of part annotations and a verified specification, what fraction of the corpus violates the specification, where, and which cross-corpus label mappings are provably meaning-preserving?*

**Project 2.1 — Two-tier audit pipeline.** Tier 1: compile the decidable, high-volume obligations (transitivity, antisymmetry, supplementation, kinematic-tree well-formedness, disjointness of declared part types) to Datalog and SMT (Z3 [18]) and run them over millions of part instances with UNKNOWN/timeout as a first-class outcome. Tier 2: pass residual hard cases (assembly, constitution, PSL state change) to Prover9/Mace4 against the full ontology. Adapters read the partner's formats and the public ones (LeRobot, RLDS, ROS 2/MCAP, OpenUSD/USDZ, PLY, JSON). Every finding is an evidence record: the violated axiom, the instance, the proof or counter-model, the tool version.
Open Research Questions: (a) Which axiom families account for the majority of violations in each corpus, and does the distribution differ between simulation-derived and real-robot corpora? — Y1 milestone, with confidence intervals by corpus. (b) What fraction of Tier-2 cases are decided within a fixed proof budget, and can Tier-1 rules be strengthened from Tier-2 proofs? — Y2.

**Project 2.2 — Cross-dataset alignment and the aligned corpus.** Using the schema mappings of Project 1.1, define label mappings between datasets and prove for each that it preserves the parthood structure (a mapping is admitted only if its preservation obligation is a theorem). Release corrected annotations, the mapping set and a merged, ontology-aligned corpus; deliver to [Partner] an audit report on its data and the toolkit configured for its formats.
Open Research Questions: (a) Which label pairs across PartNet, PartNet-Mobility and GAPartNet admit a meaning-preserving mapping, and which are provably incompatible? — Y1/Y2. (b) Does pooling only ontology-aligned data reduce the cross-vocabulary transfer gap measured in Theme 3? — Y2.

**Project 2.3 — Generalization of the audit method.** The pipeline is specification-based auditing of hierarchical annotations; it applies beyond robotics to CAD assemblies, building information models and part labels in medical imaging. In year two, run it on [one non-robotics annotation set nominated by the partner or by the Harvard laboratory — confirm] to demonstrate transfer of the method. — Y2.

### Theme 3: Learning — ontology as inductive bias (O3)
*Problem statement: Does constraining a part-aware manipulation policy to satisfy the verified ontology improve its generalization to unseen object categories, and does ontology-violation rate predict task failure?*

**Project 3.1 — Constraint-based training.** Implement differentiable relaxations of the Tier-1 parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on the part relations a policy infers; implement constraint-guided augmentation that synthesizes ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories. Train part-aware manipulation policies in SAPIEN [9] on PartNet-Mobility categories [second simulator to confirm].
**Project 3.2 — Verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** Evaluate on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories with matched comparisons and confidence intervals; report ontology-violation rate alongside task success; ablate by axiom family.
Open Research Questions: (a) Does ontology-consistent training improve success on unseen categories relative to matched baselines? — Y2 milestone. (b) Is a policy's ontology-violation rate a predictor of task failure usable as an interpretable failure signal? — Y2. (c) Do policies trained on pooled, ontology-aligned data transfer across part vocabularies better than policies trained on pooled raw data? — Y2.

## 2.9 Impact
In the near term the project raises the quality of the datasets physical AI is built on, gives manipulation policies an interpretable failure signal, and gives Canadian robotics companies a way to produce machine-checkable evidence about their training data ahead of the 2028 compliance clock. In the long term the verified Parts Ontology and its proved mappings join COLORE and ISO/IEC 21838 as shared infrastructure for an open knowledge network about physical objects, and the audit method — specification-based checking of hierarchical annotations — becomes a general tool for data quality in engineering, construction and medical imaging.
