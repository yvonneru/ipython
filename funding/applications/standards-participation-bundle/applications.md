### C.2 Early Career Symposium research summary (~380 words)

**Title.** Verified mereological ontologies for object–part representation in physical AI.

**Problem.** Robot-learning datasets (PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment, DROID) annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each dataset defines "part" operationally; no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; and pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. Poor transfer across part vocabularies and brittle generalization to new object categories are observed but have never been measured, because there has been no formal specification to measure against.

**Approach.** Three objectives treat one ontology as specification, audit and inductive bias. (O1) Axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts — rigid, articulated, functional and assembly parts — as an extension of TUpper (ISO/IEC 21838-4) integrated with the Process Specification Language for state change under manipulation; verification of consistency, non-triviality, module relationships and representation theorems with Prover9 and Mace4 following the COLORE methodology. The theory base is material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies and mereological pluralism (two Synthese submissions with M. Grüninger). (O2) A two-tier audit pipeline (Datalog/SMT for bulk checking; first-order theorem proving for residual hard cases) translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances and checks them, yielding the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations, provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings, a merged ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable audit toolkit. (O3) Differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses, constraint-guided augmentation for underrepresented categories, and verification-in-the-loop evaluation that reports ontology-violation rate alongside task success; experiments in SAPIEN [and a second simulator], with ablations by axiom family.

**Results to date.** [Fill at submission: verification results for the O1 modules; the first audit numbers across the five datasets; status of the Synthese papers.] Earlier work: core contribution to the axiomatization and verification of TUpper (ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023); a doctoral architecture for AI knowledge systems governed by a verified ontology; production ontology-based 3D datasets (Uing Technologies).

**Questions for the symposium mentors.** (1) Whether representation theorems for the articulated-part module can be obtained relative to the PSL occurrence tree without strengthening TUpper's mereology. (2) How to present a meaning-preserving label mapping so that a robotics venue accepts it as evidence rather than as a definition. (3) How to keep an open-licence ontology, a standards contribution (SC 32, IEEE ORA) and a company product (AXIOMALITY's data Passport) on separate, defensible tracks.

### Parts ontology extension aligned with ISO/IEC 21838-4 (TUpper): a verified first-order ontology of physical object parts for AI systems

**Recent progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of the intended models of a vocabulary; a top-level ontology, in the sense of ISO/IEC 21838-1, is one whose axioms every domain ontology can extend without inconsistency. My work on the axiomatization and the verification of TUpper (ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023) [1; confirm role wording] followed the COLORE methodology [2]: each module's models are characterized up to isomorphism, consistency and non-triviality are checked with Prover9 and Mace4, and relationships between modules are proved as definitional extensions or conservative extensions. *Material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies* [3] and *mereological pluralism* [4] (both submitted to Synthese, 2026, with M. Grüninger) show that objects and the matter that constitutes them can carry distinct mereologies related by a mapping that preserves parthood, and that several mereologies can be entailed by one set of axioms. Production use: ontology-based representations of objects, parts and indoor environments for embodied AI (Uing Technologies), and a machine-proven knowledge kernel aligned with TUpper (AXIOMALITY).

**Objectives.** TUpper axiomatizes objects, processes, spatial and temporal location and mereology in general, but it does not say what a *part of a physical object* is in the senses in which AI systems use the term: a rigid component, an articulated member that moves relative to the rest of the object, a functional part identified by what it does, or a member of an assembly identified by how it was put together. Robot-learning datasets (PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment, DROID) each define "part" operationally, so annotations cannot be mapped across datasets without loss and pooled data silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. *Can the part-level representations that AI systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does an ontology that says so, extending TUpper, have the intended models and only those?* The proposed work item has three objectives: (1) specify a modular first-order ontology of rigid, articulated, functional and assembly parts as a definitional or conservative extension of TUpper; (2) integrate it with a process ontology (the Process Specification Language, ISO 18629 lineage) so that parthood under manipulation — attachment, detachment, articulation — is axiomatized as state change; (3) publish, with the axioms, the verification artefacts (consistency proofs, counter-models, module diagrams, representation theorems) and a conformance profile stating what it means for a dataset's annotations to satisfy the ontology.

**Gap.** Existing part vocabularies in robotics and CAD have limited expressiveness (taxonomies without axioms), admit unintended models (parts that overlap their complements; kinematic chains with no fixed member), are not integrated with a top-level ontology or a process ontology, and have never been subjected to ontological analysis; the IEEE 1872 family axiomatizes robots and tasks, not the parts of the objects they act on.

**Methodology.** The work follows the ontology lifecycle methodology used for TUpper [2]: competency questions are drawn from the application (dataset annotation and manipulation), axioms are written in Common Logic, and the models of each module are characterized up to isomorphism. *Theme 1 — Parts (M1–M8).* Project 1.1, rigid and assembly parts as extensions of TUpper's mereology: *Is the rigid-part module a conservative extension of TUpper's mereology?* *Which assembly structures are definable from parthood and attachment alone?* Project 1.2, articulated parts and kinematic chains: *Does every model of the articulated-part module contain a fixed member, and is that a theorem or an added axiom?* Project 1.3, functional parts: *Can functional parthood be defined from participation in PSL activities without a primitive function relation?* *Theme 2 — Conformance (M6–M14).* Project 2.1, translation of annotation hierarchies to ontology instances: *For which of the five datasets does the translation yield a model of the ontology, and which axiom families are violated when it does not?* Project 2.2, cross-dataset mappings: *Is the mapping between two datasets' part vocabularies a parthood-preserving mapping in the sense of [3]?* *Theme 3 — Verification artefacts (M10–M18).* Project 3.1: representation theorems for each module relative to the intended structures; Project 3.2: the conformance profile and its test suite. Each question is answered by a theorem (Prover9) or a counter-model (Mace4) and recorded in COLORE.

**Impact.** For SC 32, a verified extension of the 21838 series that demonstrates the extension mechanism of 21838-1 on a domain of immediate use in AI, and, by liaison with SC 42, a bridge to the ISO/IEC 5259 series (a data-quality measure for part annotations). For robotics, one meaning of "part" across datasets, meaning-preserving label mappings, and an auditable evidence format ahead of the EU AI Act Annex I date (2 August 2028). For Canada, participation through the SCC mirror committee and open release through COLORE.
