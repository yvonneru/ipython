To the Directorate of The Branco Weiss Fellowship – Society in Science, ETH Zurich

Dear Members of the Directorate,

I am applying for a Branco Weiss Fellowship to be hosted at the University of Toronto [confirm host and see README on the PhD-institution question], for a project that puts formal proof between the data that trains robots and the robots themselves.

Robots that handle objects are trained on a few large datasets that describe objects as hierarchies of parts. No dataset, and no model trained on one, is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood that every person applies without thinking; the field measures task success, not whether what the robot learned is coherent, and it pools datasets whose notions of "part" were never reconciled. My project specifies parthood as verified first-order axioms, audits the major robot-learning corpora against them in public, and tests whether training under the axioms makes manipulation policies generalize.

This is outside the mainstream of both parent fields. Robot learning holds that scale is the answer; applied ontology holds that description is its job and does not audit the training data of learned systems against its own axioms. I hold that coherence can be specified, checked and published, and that this is a precondition for trust at any scale. It is a departure from my doctorate, which was about knowledge-system architecture and top-level categories and contained no object parts, robot datasets or learned policies; only the verification method carries over. Nor would a disciplinary funder carry it: robot-learning programmes fund policies and knowledge-representation programmes fund theories; neither funds a five-year public audit whose deliverables are a standard and an argument with regulators and the public.

I am prepared for this project in an unusual way. I was a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology [confirm role wording]. With Michael Grüninger I developed a machine-checkable theory of material constitution and mereological pluralism, under review at Synthese, that says when two part decompositions of one object are compatible — the question robot datasets pose and do not answer. And at a company I co-founded I built ontology-based representations of objects and indoor environments that shipped as one of the first AR pet-interaction applications on the Apple Vision Pro. Machine-checked theory and shipped systems are rarely held by the same person; both are needed here.

The societal question is concrete and its clock is set. From [2 August 2028 — verify application date] the EU AI Act applies to AI in machinery and robots. Regulators, standards bodies and the public will need evidence about training data that is checkable rather than asserted; today's datasheets and data-quality standards are descriptive and self-reported. I will produce checkable evidence in the open, take it into ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, building on my 21838-4 work in SC 32, and convene yearly workshops that put roboticists, standards bodies, regulators and the people who will work beside these machines in one room. I say openly that I founded a company pursuing commercial data verification; the fellowship research is independent, its outputs are open, and I welcome the argument about whether assurance for physical AI should be a public good.

I would be glad to present this project in Zurich.
