To the Directorate of The Branco Weiss Fellowship – Society in Science, ETH Zurich

Dear Members of the Directorate,

I am applying for a Branco Weiss Fellowship to be hosted at the University of Toronto [confirm host and see README on the PhD-institution question], for a project that puts formal proof between the data that trains robots and the robots themselves.

Robots that handle objects are trained on a small number of large datasets that describe objects as hierarchies of parts. No dataset, and no model trained on one, is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood that every person applies without thinking. The field measures whether a task succeeded; it does not measure whether what the robot learned about the object is coherent, and it pools datasets whose notions of "part" were never reconciled. My project specifies parthood as first-order axioms verified with automated theorem provers, audits the major robot-learning corpora against them and publishes the results, and tests whether training under the axioms makes manipulation policies generalize.

This is outside the mainstream of both of its parent fields. Robot learning holds that scale is the answer; applied ontology holds that description is its job and has never audited the training data of a learned system against its own axioms. I hold that coherence can be specified, checked and published, and that this is a precondition for trust whatever the scale. It is also a departure from my doctorate, which was about knowledge-system architecture and top-level categories: nothing in it concerned object parts, robot datasets or learned policies.

I am prepared for this project in an unusual way. I was a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology. With Michael Grüninger I developed a machine-checkable theory of material constitution and mereological pluralism, now under review at Synthese, that says exactly when two part decompositions of one object are compatible — the question robot datasets pose and do not answer. And I have built ontology-governed data systems that ran in production at companies I founded, including one of the first AR object-interaction applications on the Apple Vision Pro. Formal theory that is machine-checked, and systems that run, are rarely held by the same person; both are needed here.

The societal question is concrete and its clock is set. From 2 August 2028 the EU AI Act applies to AI in machinery and robots. Regulators, standards bodies and the public will need evidence about training data that is checkable rather than asserted, and nobody has yet said what that evidence looks like. I will produce it in the open, take it into ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, where I already contribute, and convene yearly workshops that put roboticists, standards bodies, regulators and the people who will work beside these machines in one room. I will also say openly that I founded a company pursuing commercial data verification; the fellowship research is independent and its outputs are open, and I welcome the argument about whether assurance for physical AI should be a public good.

I would be glad to present this project in Zurich.
