## ARIA summary block (short answers for the form)

**The idea in one sentence.** Give robot-learning data the same standard of rigour as an engineering specification: a formally verified ontology of physical object parts against which every part-level dataset and every part-aware policy can be checked by machine, so that "this data is consistent" becomes a theorem rather than an opinion.

**Which assumption of the opportunity-space document this aligns with or challenges.** [Required by ARIA's seed criteria: cite the Summary, Belief or Observation by number/wording from the current opportunity-space PDF. Draft for Mathematics for Safe AI: the space holds that safety claims about AI systems should rest on machine-checkable mathematical evidence; this seed extends that belief from models to the *training data* of physical AI, where no such evidence exists today. Draft for Smarter Robot Bodies: the space assumes progress comes from bodies, control and learning; this seed challenges the implicit assumption that the part-level data those bodies learn from is fit to learn from, and supplies the missing check.]

**Why it matters.** Physical AI is trained on a handful of large part-level datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment, DROID [1–6] — which annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each defines "part" operationally; no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies, brittle generalization to new object categories — are observed but have never been measured, because there has been no formal specification to measure against.

**Why now.** The datasets exist at a scale where the theory can be tested; the theory exists (a first-order account of when two part decompositions of one object are compatible, submitted 2026 [7, 8]); the verification tooling exists (COLORE, Prover9/Mace4, SMT; TUpper as ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 [9–12]); and regulators will soon require evidence about training data for robots (EU AI Act Annex I applies to AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028; NIST AI RMF [13, 14]).

**What we will build in 12 months.** (i) A verified parts ontology (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as a modular extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language, released under an open licence and contributed to COLORE and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42. (ii) The first quantitative audit of parthood consistency across five robot datasets, with corrected annotations, provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings, a merged ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable audit toolkit. (iii) Evidence, in SAPIEN simulation, on whether ontology-consistent training improves generalization of part-aware manipulation policies to unseen categories, and whether ontology-violation rate predicts task failure.

**What would change the conversation.** If violation rate predicts failure, robot policies acquire an interpretable, machine-checkable failure signal that does not depend on task success labels; if ontology-aligned pooling transfers across part vocabularies, the field's separate datasets become one corpus. Either result changes how physical-AI data is built and bought.

## 1. Recent Progress

I work on what a robot-learning system's data and models are committed to about the physical world, and on making those commitments explicit and checkable: ontologies of physical objects and their parts written as first-order theories, verified by automated reasoning, and carried into engineering practice through an international standard, patents and production systems.

- **Top-level ontology as an international standard.** As a core contributor within ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 I worked on the axiomatization, modular organization and formal verification of TUpper, published as ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 [9], a top-level ontology each of whose modules carries a verified first-order axiomatization [confirm exact role wording and working-group designation]. Domain ontologies can be proved, not merely asserted, to be consistent extensions of it.
- **A verified theory of material constitution and mereological pluralism.** With Michael Grüninger I developed an account of constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, both submitted to *Synthese* in 2026 [7, 8]. The axioms are first-order; consistency and non-triviality are established with Prover9 and Mace4; the mapping is proved to preserve the parthood structure it must preserve. The theory says exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible — precisely the question posed by robot datasets, where kinematic, functional and visual decompositions co-exist without any statement of how they relate.
- **Knowledge-system architecture with industrial deployment.** My doctoral work (University of Toronto, 2025) developed an architecture in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer of an AI system; elements contributed to ISO/IEC 21838-4. As co-founder of Uing Technologies and founding-team member of MICAS I shipped ontology-governed data-integration and recommendation systems at production scale, and I am first inventor on granted patents in 3D recognition, indoor modelling and automatic reconstruction [reconcile count: deck 13 granted + 9 applications; drafts 3 CN + 9–10 DE].
- **AXIOMALITY knowledge kernel.** The company I founded in 2026 has built a four-layer ontology stack (TUpper; physical world; robotics domain; dataset instances) whose axioms are machine-proven consistent, an offline Engine (ingestion → scene graph → SMT check) with adapters for LeRobot, RLDS, ROS 2/MCAP, OpenUSD and PLY, and 100,000 measured 3D asset packages. UNKNOWN and timeout are first-class outputs of the checker, so abstention is auditable.

## 2. Objectives

Robot manipulation policies increasingly operate on part-level representations — segmented, articulated, functional parts — learned from annotations that carry no formal semantics: nothing requires the parts a model infers to form a consistent whole, nothing relates the part vocabularies of different datasets, and nothing lets a policy say why a part decomposition is wrong. The program is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

To address this challenge, the proposed seed has three primary objectives:

1. **Ontology as specification (O1).** Axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts — rigid, articulated, functional, assembly — as an extension of TUpper integrated with PSL [10] for state change under manipulation, with consistency, non-triviality, module relationships and representation theorems established in Prover9/Mace4 by the COLORE methodology [11, 15].
2. **Ontology as audit (O2).** Translate the annotation hierarchies of PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled and AgiBot World into ontology instances and check them with a two-tier pipeline (Datalog/SMT [16] for bulk; first-order theorem proving for residual hard cases), producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations, provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset mappings, a merged corpus and a reusable toolkit.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias (O3).** Use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies — differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses, constraint-guided augmentation for underrepresented categories, and verification-in-the-loop evaluation reporting ontology-violation rate alongside task success — and measure the effect on generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories in SAPIEN [2].

## 3. Literature Review

Part-level datasets [1–6] were built for perception and manipulation, not for reasoning: PartNet fixes a segmentation hierarchy per category, PartNet-Mobility and GAPartNet attach kinematic joints and actionable regions, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment and DROID record real-robot episodes with object labels of varying granularity. No dataset states axioms for its parthood relation, and no prior work has performed an ontological analysis of what those relations presuppose; Grüninger's Commonsense Cobotics program asks which mereotopologies are implicit in ShapeNet and PartNet decompositions, and whether multiple distinct parthood relations exist there [17] — this seed answers those questions at dataset scale and then acts on the answers. Formal mereology [18, 19] supplies the candidate axioms, and TUpper [9] and the COLORE hierarchies [11] supply verified mereotopologies, but the existing theories assume a single parthood relation, which is exactly what robot data violates; the pluralist account in [7, 8] removes that assumption. Cross-dataset alignment in the robot-learning community is done by hand-built label maps or by learned embeddings; neither yields a guarantee that a mapping preserves meaning. Neuro-symbolic methods that impose logical constraints as losses exist for perception, but none uses a *verified* theory whose models are characterized, so unintended models — annotations that satisfy the loss but not the intended semantics — remain possible [12, 20]. Data-quality practice in physical AI (dataset cards, statistics on episode counts) is descriptive; it has no notion of a specification against which the data can be shown to be wrong.

## 4. Methodology

The ontologies are designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of Grüninger and Fox [15], with the design-by-reuse, merging and transfer techniques of the COLORE repository [11], and are verified: we characterize the models of each module up to isomorphism and determine whether they coincide with the intended models. Competency questions come from the application — a dataset annotation, a cross-dataset label pair, or a policy's inferred part structure is *consistent* exactly when it is satisfiable in a model of the ontology, and two decompositions are *compatible* exactly when a parthood-preserving mapping between them exists [7]. Verification (are the models of the axioms the intended ones) is kept distinct from validation (are the intended models the right ones for the datasets); validation uses the audit results of Theme 2. Open research questions are tagged with the milestone (M) at which they are answered; the timeline is in `budget_and_timeline.md`.

### Theme 1: Parts — a verified specification (O1)

*Given the part decompositions used across robot-learning datasets, specify a modular ontology whose models are exactly the intended part structures, and prove when two decompositions of one object are compatible.*

**Project 1.1: Modular parts ontology.** Four modules — rigid parts (mereotopology from TUpper), articulated parts (joints as PSL fluents with allowed state changes), functional parts (affordance-bearing regions as in GAPartNet), assemblies (constitution mappings between modules) — designed by reuse from COLORE hierarchies and merged; each module verified for consistency and non-triviality; representation theorems relate the merged ontology to each dataset's annotation schema so that a cross-dataset mapping is meaning-preserving by proof.

Open Research Questions: Which COLORE mereotopologies are synonymous with the parthood relations implicit in PartNet and PartNet-Mobility, and does any dataset require a new hierarchy? (M3) · Does the axiomatization of articulated parts require the full PSL process ontology, or a definable fragment? (M4) · Are the four modules' parthood relations distinct up to definability, i.e., is pluralism forced by the data? (M4)

### Theme 2: Audit — ontology as a machine-checkable specification for data (O2)

*Given five part-level datasets and the verified ontology, measure how often and in what patterns the annotations violate the axioms, and produce mappings and corrections whose correctness is proved.*

**Project 2.1: Two-tier audit pipeline.** Annotation hierarchies are translated into ontology instances by per-dataset adapters (reusing AXIOMALITY's LeRobot/RLDS/OpenUSD/PLY adapters [confirm licence]); the bulk of instances ([of the order of 10^6] part instances across the five datasets — confirm from each dataset's published statistics) is checked by Datalog rules and SMT (Z3 [16]) compiled from the axioms; residual hard cases go to Prover9/Mace4; UNKNOWN/timeout are reported, never silently dropped. **Project 2.2: Alignment and release.** Cross-dataset label mappings are proposed from the representation theorems and checked; violating annotations are corrected or flagged with the axiom family they violate; outputs are a per-dataset audit report, corrected annotations, a merged ontology-aligned corpus and the toolkit, all released openly.

Open Research Questions: What fraction of annotations in each dataset violate a parthood axiom, and which axiom families (antisymmetry, transitivity, supplementation, articulation constraints) account for the violations? (M8) · Which label pairs across datasets admit a parthood-preserving mapping, and which provably do not? (M8) · Does the SMT-compiled subset decide the same instances as the first-order prover on a held-out sample, i.e., is the fast tier sound and complete for the audit? (M6) · Does the audit transfer, unchanged, to a non-robotics hierarchical annotation set (a CAD assembly or a medical-imaging part-label set) [choose one]? (M12, stretch)

### Theme 3: Learning — ontology as inductive bias (O3)

*Given a part-aware manipulation policy and the verified ontology, determine whether ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen object categories and whether violation rate predicts failure.*

**Project 3.1: Constraint-shaped training and verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** In SAPIEN with PartNet-Mobility [2], train part-aware policies [architecture chosen with the UK host; name the baseline and the number of categories held out so the experiment is sized, not open-ended] on (a) raw, (b) audited, and (c) audited-and-pooled data; add differentiable relaxations of the parthood axioms as auxiliary losses; augment underrepresented categories with ontology-consistent synthesized part configurations; evaluate on held-out categories with matched comparisons and confidence intervals, reporting ontology-violation rate of the policy's inferred part structure next to task success, with ablations by axiom family.

Open Research Questions: Does training on audited or ontology-aligned pooled data improve success on unseen PartNet-Mobility categories relative to raw data? (M11) · Does the violation rate of a policy's inferred part structure predict task failure, and with what lead? (M11) · Which axiom family contributes most to the gain, and does removing it remove the gain? (M12)

## 5. Impact

Near term, the seed delivers artefacts rather than papers: a verified parts ontology under an open licence, contributed to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first measured account of what physical-AI models are actually trained on, with corrected data; a merged corpus that lets separately built datasets be pooled with a proof that the pooling preserves meaning; and a toolkit that audits any hierarchical annotation against a specification. For robot builders these are benchmark quality and transfer; for AI assurance they are machine-checkable evidence about training data — the kind of evidence the EU AI Act's Annex I regime for robots (from 2 August 2028) and the NIST AI RMF will ask for [13, 14] — and an interpretable failure signal for deployed policies that does not depend on task labels.

Translation runs through AXIOMALITY, whose offline Engine and data Passport (a reproducible per-release evidence pack) are the commercial carrier for the audit; ARIA's Activation Partners are the natural route from seed results to a certified data product for robot OEMs, model developers and data suppliers. Longer term, the ontology is one more verified module in an open knowledge network of theories of the physical world, and the audit protocol is a template for treating every training corpus in physical AI as something that can be shown to be wrong — which is the precondition for showing it is right.
