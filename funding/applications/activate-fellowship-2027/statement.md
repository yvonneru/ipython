I am a knowledge-representation researcher who has spent a decade making formal logic do useful work outside the seminar room, and AXIOMALITY is the company that work has been converging on.

I trained as an industrial engineer at the University of Toronto and returned there for a doctorate in Information Engineering, completed in March 2025. My thesis built an architecture for AI systems in which a verified first-order ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer — a system where every claim the software makes about the world is either a theorem of the ontology or is flagged as unproven. Parts of that work entered ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology, to which I was a core contributor within ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 32. With Prof. Michael Grüninger I then developed a formal theory of material constitution — constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies — and a validation of mereological pluralism, both now under review at Synthese. The theory answers a question that sounds philosophical and is in fact practical: when two descriptions of the same object as an assembly of parts are put side by side, what must hold for them to be describing the same thing?

In parallel I built companies. At YourTable I built recommendation systems; at MICAS I designed and shipped the CRM, ERP and warehouse systems and the machine-learning marketing stack for a cross-border platform that passed USD 50 million in annual revenue and a hundred-person team within a year, backed by USD 5 million of angel capital including Accel Partners; at Uing Technologies, which I co-founded in 2022 and lead, we built structured 3D datasets of physical environments and shipped one of the first spatial applications on the Apple Vision Pro. I am a named inventor on 13 granted patents and 9 pending applications in 3D recognition, indoor modelling and automatic reconstruction [confirm first-inventor status and assignee for each; reconcile with the CV's counts]. I learned in those companies that a data model which is merely agreed upon fails at scale. AXIOMALITY tests whether one that is proven consistent can be made not to.

Physical AI has now produced the exact failure I learned to recognize. Robot-learning datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment, DROID — describe objects as hierarchies of parts, and each defines "part" its own way. Nobody checks the hierarchies against the axioms of parthood; nobody can say which labels in one dataset mean the same as labels in another; and models trained on pooled data inherit the contradictions silently. At the same time, the price of a raw robot hour is heading to zero while every deployment from 2028 will have to account, under the EU AI Act's Annex I and the NIST and ANSI/A3 frameworks, for the data it learned from. The value of robot data is moving from its volume to what can be proven about it. That is a problem a verified ontology solves, and I am one of very few people who have both written such an ontology into a standard and shipped data systems governed by one.

AXIOMALITY is the evidence layer: an offline Engine that ingests a dataset in the formats robotics actually uses, reconstructs scene graphs, checks them against a kernel whose axioms are machine-proven consistent and traceable to ISO/IEC 21838-4, and returns a data Passport — a reproducible evidence pack per release. The kernel exists and is verified; the Engine is in internal test this quarter; the first design partners are in discussion [confirm]; and the plan is three co-development partners and ten thousand verified episodes by the first quarter of 2027, recognition of the Passport by a standards or assessment body by the second, and five paying customers by month eighteen. I lead a CTO, a robotics lead and a senior 3D asset lead, and I own the kernel, the standards route and the first customers myself.

Why Activate, and why now. The company's next eighteen months are founder-limited: the milestones are customers, standards recognition and live-robot validation of the physics checks, and each needs my full attention. The fellowship's stipend lets me give it without raising equity before the product has proven itself with customers; the R&D support pays for the robot time, compute and assessment-body process the team cannot supply alone; a Boston host lab puts instrumented robot cells within reach at exactly the moment the Engine moves from logged data to live sensor streams [or: the Anywhere cohort lets me run that validation in [facility] which the company already controls]; and Activate's community is made of the OEMs, model developers and deep-tech investors who are AXIOMALITY's customers and future backers. I am applying to Activate rather than continuing on an academic track because the question I most want answered — can a verified specification of the physical world become the instrument a market is measured against — is one that only a company can answer, and I would like to be the one who answers it.
