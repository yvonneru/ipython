# Brief for referees — Stanford Science Fellows (2027 cohort)

Send with the reference request (emails.md, email 8). [Brackets] to confirm before sending.

**What the fellowship is.** The Stanford Science Fellows program is a university-wide postdoctoral fellowship run by the Office of the Vice Provost and Dean of Research for early-career scientists pursuing fundamental research in the natural sciences: a stipend of USD 98,000 a year plus research funds and professional development, for three years, in a cohort of fellows who run their own research programs with a Stanford faculty host. My proposed host is [name, department]. Official page: https://stanfordsciencefellows.stanford.edu/

**Deadline for your letter.** The application closes **16 October 2026 at 11:59 pm Eastern** [per the program FAQ; verify]. Letters are uploaded through Academic Jobs Online; you will receive a link once I enter your name. The program states that letters continue to be accepted after the deadline but that reviewers are not obliged to read a late one, so I have asked for letters by **12 October**.

**What the program asks.** Three letters of recommendation; the program advises that one come from the PhD advisor. There is no prescribed structure. A letter of one to two pages is typical for this kind of fellowship.

**What reviewers weigh (from the program's published description; no weights are given).** Scientific excellence; originality; research potential; the applicant's ability to contribute to Stanford's collaborative research community; and commitment to enhancing demographic and intellectual diversity in Stanford's scientific community. The committee is Stanford faculty from across the natural sciences, so a sentence that places the work for a non-specialist helps, and a sentence on whether this is fundamental science (rather than applied engineering) would answer the question a physical-sciences reader will ask first. The most useful letters give one or two concrete examples — a result you saw me obtain, a problem I solved without direction, a piece of work you would not have expected at my stage — and state plainly where I stand relative to other postdoctoral researchers you have known.

**Points that would help.**
1. The theoretical work on material constitution and mereological pluralism (two papers under review at *Synthese*), and why a machine-checkable account of when two part decompositions are compatible matters for what learning systems represent.
2. The ISO/IEC 21838-4 contribution and what it shows about rigour and the ability to work in a standards body.
3. The move from theory to deployed systems (patents, production data work at MICAS and YourTable, the 3D datasets and Vision Pro application at Uing Technologies) and why that predicts success with the audit pipeline and the policy experiments.
4. Independence and interdisciplinary reach: the program is my own, distinct from my thesis and from my collaborators' grants; it joins formal logic, robot learning and standards, and two of the datasets it audits (PartNet, DROID) were built [in part] at Stanford.
5. Community: graduate-student representation and union stewardship at Toronto, teaching, open standards and open tools — and anything you have seen of how I work with students and colleagues.

**Attached.** Research statement (2 pp plus references), career statement ([2] pp), current CV.
