## Verified Mereological Ontologies for Object–Part Representation in Physical AI

**1. Background and progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what an agent's terms mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects as theories in first-order logic and verifies them, so that every claim about the ontology is a theorem or a counter-model. As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology [1], I worked on its axiomatization, modular organization and machine-checked verification [confirm role wording]. With Michael Grüninger (University of Toronto) I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two papers under review at *Synthese* [2, 3]. The account states, in machine-checked first-order logic, exactly when two part decompositions of one object are compatible. My doctoral thesis [4] developed an architecture in which a verified ontology governs an AI system's data model, learned models and simulation layer; I have carried the same discipline into practice through ontology-governed data systems in production at companies I helped found, a consumer spatial application on the Apple Vision Pro, and [number — reconcile: 3 Chinese invention patents; 9–10 German utility patents] patents in 3D recognition and indoor modelling. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School [laboratory], working on [one sentence — confirm].

**2. Problem and objectives.** Robots learn to handle objects from datasets that describe each object as a hierarchy of parts: PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10] and DROID [11] annotate objects with kinematic, functional and visual part labels. Each defines "part" operationally — a segmentation mask, a movable link, an actionable region. No dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across vocabularies and brittle generalization to new object categories — are observed throughout embodied learning but never measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. The program asks a fundamental question about representation:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems acquire be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

Three objectives answer it in order. **O1, ontology as specification:** axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) extending TUpper and integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for change of parthood under manipulation. **O2, ontology as audit:** translate the annotation hierarchies of the major robot-learning datasets into ontology instances and check them automatically — the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot data, with provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings. **O3, ontology as inductive bias:** train and evaluate part-aware manipulation policies with the verified ontology as a constraint, and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories.

**State of the art.** Formal mereology [13] supplies axiom systems for parthood and TUpper's mereotopology modules are verified [1, 14], but no verified ontology distinguishes the rigid, articulated, functional and assembly senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data. Part-level datasets [5–11] carry no formal semantics; an ontological analysis of vision benchmarks [14] found logical errors in ShapeNet and PartNet but produced neither a scalable audit nor a cross-dataset alignment. Neuro-symbolic constraint methods [15] have not been applied to part structure in manipulation, and no evaluation protocol reports whether a policy's inferred object structure is consistent with any specification. The gap is threefold — no specification, no measurement, no training signal.

**3. Approach.** Ontologies are designed with the Grüninger–Fox lifecycle methodology [16] and verified with the COLORE repository methodology [17]: the models of each module are characterized up to isomorphism and compared with the intended models, using Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [18]. Verification (do the axioms have exactly the intended models?) is kept distinct from validation (are those the right models?), which is settled by competency questions drawn from the datasets: every annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute. All tools are open; Prof. Grüninger continues as external collaborator on Theme 1.

*Theme 1 — Parts (O1; Y1).* Axiomatize rigid, articulated (links and joints), functional (affordance-bearing regions) and assembly parthood as separate Common Logic [19] modules, each verified for consistency and non-triviality, with representation theorems characterizing their models (1.1). Apply the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2, 3] to state when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible, and integrate PSL so that changes of parthood under manipulation (detaching a lid, opening a drawer) are fluents (1.2). *Questions:* Are the four relations independent, or some definable from others? Is each dataset's part hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not?

*Theme 2 — Audit (O2; Y1–Y2).* A two-tier pipeline translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances through per-dataset adapters, checks the bulk (millions of part instances) with Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [20], sends residual hard cases to first-order theorem proving, and reports UNKNOWN and timeout as first-class outputs rather than silent passes (2.1). From the audit, derive provably meaning-preserving label mappings between datasets, correct violating annotations, and release a merged ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable audit toolkit (2.2). *Questions:* Which fragment compiles to SMT without losing violations the prover finds? Do violation rates differ between simulated (PartNet-Mobility) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora? Does the audit transfer to a second domain — CAD assemblies or medical-imaging part labels (Y3)?

*Theme 3 — Learning (O3; Y2–Y3).* Implement differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on a policy's inferred part relations [15], and constraint-guided augmentation that synthesizes ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories (3.1). Report, beside task success, the rate at which a policy's inferred object structure violates the ontology — a machine-checkable statement of what it has learned (3.2). Experiments run in SAPIEN [6] on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories, with confidence intervals and ablations by axiom family; in Y3, replication on [the host group's robot platform — confirm]. *Questions:* Does ontology-consistent training improve generalization to unseen categories? Does violation rate predict task failure? Do policies trained on pooled, aligned data transfer across vocabularies and embodiments?

**4. Why Stanford, and why three years.** The program needs, in one place, the groups that produce and consume part-level robot data, expertise in automated reasoning, and a community in which a claim about what a learned system represents is expected to be checkable. PartNet and ShapeNet were built [in part] at Stanford and DROID was collected there [confirm the host's authorship], so the datasets audited in Theme 2 can be discussed with the people who designed their annotation schemas [name the host; one sentence on the host's dataset or robot-learning work and platform — confirm]. Stanford's work in SMT solving [name the group — confirm] and in biomedical knowledge systems [name the group — confirm] supplies the second tier of the audit and the Y3 transfer domain. A university-wide fellowship suits a program whose instrument is logic, whose data are robotics corpora and whose evaluation is machine learning; three years makes Theme 3 possible, because the ontology and audit (Y1) must exist before the learning experiments (Y2) and the Y3 replications turn a result into a method.

**5. Significance.** The program delivers a verified parts ontology under an open licence, contributed to COLORE and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, with corrected annotations, mappings and a merged corpus released publicly; and an audit toolkit for any hierarchical annotation problem, including CAD assemblies and medical-imaging part labels. For robotics it supplies an interpretable, machine-checkable failure signal — ontology-violation rate — about what a policy has learned. For the sciences more broadly it turns the constitution and pluralism debates into theories with empirical consequences: a dataset either is or is not a model of a given mereology, and a policy either does or does not respect it. It extends COLORE's open network of verified theories to the part-level structure of the physical world, so that a robot's commonsense about parts and wholes can be proved correct rather than assumed.
