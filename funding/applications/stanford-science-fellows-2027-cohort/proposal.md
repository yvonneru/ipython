## Verified Mereological Ontologies for Object–Part Representation in Physical AI

**1. Recent Progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what terms an agent uses and what those terms mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects as theories in first-order logic and verifies them, so that every claim about the ontology is either a theorem or a counter-model. As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology [1], I worked on the axiomatization, modular organization and machine-checked verification of a top-level ontology adopted by ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 [confirm exact role wording]. With Michael Grüninger (University of Toronto) I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two papers submitted to *Synthese* [2, 3]. Classical mereology assumes a single parthood relation; the puzzles of constitution (the statue and the clay, the ship and its planks) arise because ordinary objects seem to demand more than one. Our account states, in first-order logic verified with automated provers and model finders, exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible. My doctoral research developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer [4], and I have carried the same discipline into practice: ontology-governed data-integration and recommendation systems that ran in production at two companies I co-founded, a consumer spatial application on the Apple Vision Pro built on 3D object recognition and spatial mapping, and [number — reconcile: 3 Chinese invention patents; 9–10 German utility patents] patents from applied work in 3D recognition and indoor modelling. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School [laboratory], working on [one sentence — confirm].

**2. Objectives.** Robots learn to handle objects from large datasets that describe each object as a hierarchy of parts. Manipulation policies are trained on part-level datasets — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10], DROID [11] — that annotate objects with kinematic, functional and visual part labels. Each dataset defines "part" operationally: a segmentation mask in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. No dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies and brittle generalization to new object categories — are observed throughout embodied learning but have never been measured, because there has been no formal specification to measure against. This matters beyond robotics research: a robot deployed in a home or a factory is trusted to have learned something about the structure of the objects it handles, and today there is no machine-checkable evidence of what that something is. Two of the corpora at the centre of this problem, PartNet/ShapeNet and DROID, were built at Stanford; the audit proposed here returns to their source. The proposed program is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

To address this challenge, the program has three primary objectives:
1. **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for state change under manipulation.
2. **Ontology as audit** — translate the annotation hierarchies of the major robot-learning datasets into ontology instances and check them automatically, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets and provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias** — develop training and evaluation methods that use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies, and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories.

**3. Literature Review.** Formal mereology [13] supplies axiom systems for parthood, and the mereotopology modules of TUpper have been verified [1, 14], but no verified ontology distinguishes the rigid, articulated, functional and assembly senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data, and the philosophical literature on constitution and pluralism [13, 2, 3] has not been connected to any dataset. Part-level datasets [5–11] carry no formal semantics. Ontological analysis of vision benchmarks [14] found logical errors in ShapeNet and PartNet but produced neither a scalable audit nor a cross-dataset alignment. Neuro-symbolic methods that impose logical constraints on learned models [15] have not been applied to part structure in manipulation, and work on safety and assurance for learned robot policies has concentrated on control-level guarantees rather than on the object-level knowledge a policy has acquired; no evaluation protocol reports whether a policy's inferred object structure is consistent with any specification. The gap is therefore threefold — no specification, no measurement, no training signal — and the three objectives close it in that order.

**4. Methodology.** The ontologies will be designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of Grüninger and Fox [16] and verified using the COLORE repository methodology [17]: we characterize the models of each module up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models, using Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [18]. Verification (do the axioms have exactly the intended models?) is kept distinct from validation (are the intended models the right ones?); the latter is settled by competency questions taken from the datasets, since every annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute. The tools are open (COLORE, Prover9/Mace4, Z3 [20]) and I bring the methodology with me; Prof. Grüninger continues as co-author on Theme 1. Open research questions are milestones tagged by fellowship year (Y1–Y3).

**Theme 1: Parts (Objective 1).** *Which parthood relations are implicit in robot-learning annotations, and can they be axiomatized as verified extensions of TUpper?* **Project 1.1 — Parts ontology.** Axiomatize rigid parthood, articulated parthood (links and joints), functional parthood (affordance-bearing regions) and assembly parthood as separate modules in Common Logic [19], each verified for consistency and non-triviality, with representation theorems that characterize their models. **Project 1.2 — Constitution mappings.** Apply the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2, 3] to state when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible, and integrate PSL so that changes of parthood under manipulation (detaching a lid, opening a drawer) are fluents. *Open research questions:* Do the four parthood relations require distinct axiomatizations, or are some definable from others? (Y1) Is every dataset's part hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not? (Y1)

**Theme 2: Audit (Objective 2).** *What is the rate and pattern of parthood-axiom violation in the datasets that physical-AI models are trained on?* **Project 2.1 — Two-tier audit pipeline.** Translate annotation hierarchies into ontology instances through adapters for each dataset format; check the bulk (millions of part instances) with Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [20]; send residual hard cases to first-order theorem proving; treat UNKNOWN and timeout as first-class outputs rather than silent passes. **Project 2.2 — Cross-dataset alignment.** Derive label mappings between datasets that are provably meaning-preserving under the constitution theory, correct violating annotations, and release a merged, ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable audit toolkit. *Open research questions:* Which fragment of the ontology can be compiled to SMT without losing violations that the prover finds? (Y1) Do violation rates differ systematically between simulated (PartNet-Mobility) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora? (Y1–Y2) Does the audit transfer, unchanged, to a second hierarchical-annotation domain — CAD assemblies or part labels in medical imaging? (Y3)

**Theme 3: Learning (Objective 3).** *Does enforcing the parthood axioms during training improve a manipulation policy's generalization to unseen object categories?* **Project 3.1 — Constraint-based training.** Implement differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on a policy's inferred part relations [15], and constraint-guided augmentation that synthesizes ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories. **Project 3.2 — Verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** Report, alongside task success, the rate at which a policy's inferred object structure violates the ontology, so that the evaluation yields a machine-checkable statement about what the policy has learned rather than a success rate alone. Experiments in SAPIEN [6] and [second simulator — confirm with host] on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories, with confidence intervals and ablations by axiom family; in Y3, replication on [host group's robot platform — confirm]. *Open research questions:* Does ontology-consistent training improve generalization to unseen categories? (Y2) Does violation rate predict task failure, and can it serve as an interpretable failure signal? (Y2) Do policies trained on pooled, ontology-aligned data transfer across part vocabularies and across embodiments? (Y2–Y3)

**Why Stanford, and what the fellowship makes possible.** The program needs three things in one place: the groups that produce and consume part-level robot data; formal methods and automated reasoning; and a community in which a claim about what a learned system represents is expected to be checkable. Stanford is where PartNet and ShapeNet were built and where DROID was collected, so the datasets audited in Theme 2 can be discussed with the people who designed their annotation schemas [name the host and one sentence on the host's robot-learning, 3D-perception or dataset work and the platform available — confirm]. Stanford's work in SMT solving and AI safety supplies the second [name the group — confirm], and its knowledge-systems tradition in biomedical ontology supplies a second annotation domain for the Y3 transfer study [name the group — confirm]. A university-wide fellowship, rather than a departmental one, is the right home for a program that argues a formal theory of parts and wholes should have engineering consequences. Three years, rather than the two most fellowships allow, is what makes Theme 3 and the Y3 extensions possible: the ontology and audit (Y1) must exist before the learning experiments (Y2) can be run, and the real-robot and second-domain replications (Y3) are what turn a result into a method.

**5. Impact.** The program delivers a verified parts ontology under an open licence, contributed to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, with corrected annotations, mappings and a merged corpus released publicly; and an audit toolkit that applies to any hierarchical annotation problem, including CAD assemblies, building information models and part labels in medical imaging. For robotics and AI assurance it supplies an interpretable failure signal — ontology-violation rate — that is machine-checkable evidence about what a policy has learned, the kind of evidence that the EU AI Act (applying to AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework will require. For the sciences more broadly it turns the constitution and pluralism debates into theories with empirical consequences: a dataset either is or is not a model of a given mereology, and a policy either does or does not respect it. In the long term the ontology extends COLORE's open network of verified first-order theories to the part-level structure of the physical world, giving neuro-symbolic robotics a foundation on which correctness with respect to human commonsense about parts and wholes can be proved rather than assumed.
