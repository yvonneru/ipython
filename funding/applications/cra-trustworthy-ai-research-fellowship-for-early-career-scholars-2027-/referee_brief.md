# Supporters' brief — CRA Trustworthy AI Research Fellowship (2027–28 cohort)

**No reference letters are required.** The cohort-2 application (spring 2026) consisted of a brief online form, a CV and short written responses; the funder's announcement stated that no letters of recommendation were needed. This brief therefore serves two purposes: (1) it tells the people whose cooperation the application depends on, the Harvard supervisor and Prof. Grüninger, what the program is and what is being asked of them; (2) it is ready to send to a referee if the 2027–28 call adds a letter [verify when the call opens].

## What the program is

A CRA–Microsoft fellowship for early-career computing researchers (1–3 years post-PhD) with social-science training, who hold a tenure-track faculty or visiting-researcher position (the funder's own examples: "postdoc or fellowship") at a US institution. Fellows receive a USD 17,000 stipend, attend a four-day in-person Trustworthy AI Field School (travel, lodging and meals covered; cohort 2's was held 26–31 July 2026 at Microsoft Research, Cambridge, MA), and are mentored by established scholars across computing, the humanistic social sciences, ethics and policy. The fellowship is held alongside the fellow's existing position; it is not a salaried postdoc. Cohort 2 ran June 2026 – August 2027 and drew more than 300 eligible applicants. Applications for the 2027–28 cohort are expected to open in early 2027; the prior cycle closed 31 March 2026. Official page: https://cra.org/cra-trustworthy-ai-research-fellowship/

## What the application asks

- Online form: contact and academic information, including current position and institution.
- CV.
- Three short written responses: interdisciplinary background; interest in the fellowship; experience with or interest in community-based and public-interest approaches to trustworthy AI.
- No letters, no research-proposal upload (cohort 2). Dr. Ru's three-page research plan is attached to this brief for context.

## What reviewers weigh (not published; working assumptions)

The funder does not publish criteria or weights. From its stated purpose the plausible criteria are: fit with trustworthy AI as interdisciplinary, community-based and public-interest work; a genuine computing-plus-social-science background; research quality and promise at 1–3 years post-PhD; what the applicant would contribute to and take from the cohort and mentorship; and eligibility on position and PhD date.
