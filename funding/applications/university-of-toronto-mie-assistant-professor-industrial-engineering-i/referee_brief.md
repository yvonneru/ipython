## Four points that would help
1. **Independence.** The applicant's program — a verified ontology of object parts as a TUpper–PSL extension; the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets; parthood constraints inside learned manipulation policies — is the applicant's own and is distinct from the programs of the applicant's collaborators, including Prof. Grüninger's NSERC programs on the Physical Turing Test and commonsense cobotics. A letter that says so, from someone in a position to know, answers the question a search committee will ask of a candidate applying to the department where they trained. The strongest form of this point is specific: which ideas in the Synthese papers and the program were the applicant's, and what the applicant has built (the industrial audit pipeline, the standards contribution) without the laboratory.
2. **Rigour that became a standard.** The core contribution to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 (TUpper), and what working in ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 shows about the applicant's ability to hold a theory to the standard of "every claim is a theorem or a counter-model" and to carry it through an international committee.
3. **Theory to deployed systems.** The material-constitution and mereological-pluralism theory (two first-author Synthese submissions, 2026) on one side; production data systems, 3D dataset construction, an Apple Vision Pro App Store launch and multiple patents [count as reconciled on the CV] on the other. Few candidates at this stage have both; it is why the audit and learning objectives are feasible rather than aspirational.
4. **Capacity to build a group.** Evidence that the applicant can supervise, fund and lead: the 100+ person team at MICAS, the TA record across four Information Engineering courses, graduate-student leadership (AMIGAS Vice President; Graduate Student Union; CUPE 3902 steward), and a five-year plan with a Discovery / JELF / Alliance sequence and six trainee slots.
