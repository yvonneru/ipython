# Brief for referees — Kempner Institute Research Fellowship (2027 cohort)

Send with the reference request (emails.md, email 7). [Brackets] to confirm before sending.

**What the fellowship is.** An independent postdoctoral research fellowship at the Kempner Institute for the Study of Natural and Artificial Intelligence, Harvard University: salary plus research support, office space, compute and engineering support, for up to three years starting 1 September 2027. Fellows run their own research program with 2–3 proposed mentors from Kempner-associated faculty; mine are [Mentor 1], [Mentor 2] and [Mentor 3, if named]. Official page: https://kempnerinstitute.harvard.edu/kempner-institute-research-fellowship/

**How letters work.** The official page asks applicants to provide "the emails of three individuals who can describe their work and potential for future discoveries." The application closes **1 October 2026 at 6:00 pm ET**. [Confirm with the program office whether the Institute contacts referees directly after the deadline, or whether letters must be uploaded by 1 October; until then, please be ready to send a letter by **30 September** so nothing is lost if the earlier date applies.]

**What the panel weighs (official page, checked 25 Sept 2026).** Fellows are selected on: (1) alignment of the proposed research with the Institute's mission, vision and values — understanding the basis of intelligence in natural and artificial systems, with rigor, collaboration and benefit to humanity; (2) the extent to which the research is a novel undertaking in the field; (3) the individual's potential to add value to and gain benefit from the Kempner community; and (4) a record of successfully pursuing ambitious projects independently. Finalists present in November (12–13 and 19–20 November 2026 are the reported dates). The most useful letters give one or two concrete examples — a result you saw me obtain, a problem I solved without direction, a piece of work you would not have expected at my stage — and state plainly where I stand relative to other postdoctoral researchers you have known.

**Points that would help.**
1. Criterion 4 (independent, ambitious work): the theoretical work on material constitution and mereological pluralism (two first-author papers under review at Synthese) — what I contributed, and why a machine-checkable account of when two part decompositions are compatible matters for what learning systems represent.
2. Criterion 4 and rigor: the ISO/IEC 21838-4 (TUpper) contribution and what it shows about the ability to carry formal work through an international standards body.
3. Criterion 3 (value to the community): the move from theory to practice — patents; production 3D datasets and ontology-based representations at Uing Technologies; data and machine-learning systems at MICAS and YourTable — and why that predicts delivery of the audit pipeline and the policy experiments, and a fellow who can build tools others use.
4. Criterion 1 (mission fit): the program is my own, distinct from my thesis and from my collaborators' grants; the learning experiments and the human-comparison study are its centre, and the ontology is the instrument, not the end. If you can speak to the natural-intelligence side (human part representation, cognitive science), please do — it is where the panel is most likely to probe.
