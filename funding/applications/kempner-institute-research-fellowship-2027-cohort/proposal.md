## What Learned Policies Know About Parts and Wholes: Verified Mereological Ontologies as Specification, Audit and Inductive Bias for Part-Aware Robot Learning

**1. Recent Progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what terms an agent uses and what those terms mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects as theories in first-order logic and verifies them, so that every claim about the ontology is a theorem or a counter-model. As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 (TUpper) [1], I worked on the axiomatization, modular organization and machine-checked verification of a top-level ontology adopted as an international standard. With Prof. Michael Grüninger (University of Toronto) I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two papers submitted to Synthese [2, 3]; the theory states exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible. My doctoral research developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data, learned-model and simulation layers [4], and I have shipped ontology-governed data-integration systems in production at two companies I co-founded. Since [month year] I have worked at Harvard Medical School on [one sentence on the current postdoctoral work].

**2. Objectives.** Humans represent objects as configurations of parts and use that structure to recognize and manipulate objects they have never seen. Robot manipulation policies are trained to do the same from part-level datasets — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10], DROID [11] — that annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each dataset defines "part" operationally; no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies and brittle generalization to new categories — are observed but unmeasured, because there has been no specification to measure against. The fellowship is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Do the part-level representations that learned manipulation policies acquire satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans rely on, and does enforcing those axioms make policies generalize the way humans do — to objects they have never seen?*

The program has three objectives. The third is the centre of the fellowship; the first two supply what it needs.

1. **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for state change under manipulation.
2. **Ontology as audit** — translate the annotation hierarchies of the major robot-learning datasets into ontology instances and check them automatically, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in the data that physical-AI models are trained on, and provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias** — develop training and evaluation methods that use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies, measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories, and compare the part structure a policy infers with the part structure humans report for the same objects.

**3. Literature Review.** Cognitive science holds that parts are the primary units of object representation: people agree on the parts of common objects [13] and recognize objects by their component structure [14]. Formal mereology [15] supplies axiom systems for parthood, and TUpper's mereotopology modules have been verified [1, 16], but no verified ontology distinguishes the rigid, articulated, functional and assembly senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data. Part-level datasets [5–11] carry no formal semantics: a part is a segmentation mask in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. Ontological analysis of vision benchmarks [16] found logical errors in ShapeNet and PartNet but produced neither a scalable audit nor a cross-dataset alignment. Neuro-symbolic methods that impose logical constraints on learned models [17] have not been applied to part structure in manipulation, and no evaluation protocol reports whether a policy's inferred object structure is consistent with any specification, let alone with human part judgements.

**4. Methodology.** The ontology will be designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of the Semantic Technologies Laboratory [18] and verified using the COLORE repository methodology [19]: we characterize the models of each module up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models, using Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [20]. Competency questions have two sources: every annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected in a dataset is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute, and every human part judgement in the norming literature [13] is a sentence the ontology must express. Open research questions are milestones tagged by fellowship year (Y1–Y3).

**Theme 1: Specification (Objective 1, Y1).** *Which parthood relations are implicit in robot-learning annotations, and can they be axiomatized as verified extensions of TUpper?* **Project 1.1 — Parts ontology.** Axiomatize rigid, articulated (links and joints), functional (affordance-bearing regions) and assembly parthood as separate Common Logic modules [21], each verified for consistency and non-triviality, with representation theorems. **Project 1.2 — Constitution mappings.** Apply the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2, 3] to state when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible; integrate PSL so that parthood changes under manipulation (detaching a lid) are fluents. *Open questions:* Are some of the four relations definable from the others? Is every dataset's part hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not?

**Theme 2: Audit (Objective 2, Y1–Y2).** *What is the rate and pattern of parthood-axiom violation in the datasets that physical-AI models are trained on?* **Project 2.1 — Two-tier audit pipeline.** Translate annotation hierarchies into ontology instances via adapters for each dataset format; check the bulk with Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [22]; send residual hard cases to first-order theorem proving, with UNKNOWN and timeout as first-class outputs. **Project 2.2 — Alignment and release.** Derive label mappings that are provably meaning-preserving, correct violating annotations, and release a merged, ontology-aligned corpus and an audit toolkit. *Open questions:* Which fragment of the ontology compiles to SMT without losing violations the prover finds? Do violation rates differ between simulated (PartNet-Mobility) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora?

**Theme 3: Learning (Objective 3, Y1–Y3).** *Does enforcing the parthood axioms during training improve a manipulation policy's generalization to unseen object categories, and does the structure it learns match the structure humans use?* **Project 3.1 — Constraint-based training.** Implement differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on a policy's inferred part relations [17], and constraint-guided augmentation that synthesizes ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories; train part-aware policies in SAPIEN [6] and [second simulator — confirm] on PartNet-Mobility with held-out categories, using [policy architecture in Mentor 1's group — confirm]. **Project 3.2 — Verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** Report, alongside task success, the rate at which a policy's inferred object structure violates the ontology, with confidence intervals over held-out categories and ablations by axiom family; test whether violation rate predicts task failure and whether policies trained on pooled, ontology-aligned data transfer across part vocabularies. **Project 3.3 — Human comparison.** Elicit part decompositions from human participants for a sample of PartNet-Mobility objects [with Mentor 2's group; protocol and ethics approval to be designed with the mentor], express both human and policy decompositions as ontology instances, and measure their agreement under the constitution theory rather than by label overlap. *Open questions:* Does ontology-consistent training improve generalization to unseen categories (Y2)? Does violation rate predict task failure (Y2)? Which axiom families account for the gain (Y2–Y3)? Do humans and ontology-trained policies disagree on the same objects, and are their disagreements violations of the same axioms (Y3)?

**5. Impact.** For the study of artificial intelligence, the program delivers a testbed in which a claim about what a learned system knows about parts and wholes is machine-checkable: a verified ontology under an open licence, contributed to COLORE and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first quantitative audit of the datasets much of the field trains on, with corrected annotations, mappings and a merged corpus released publicly; and an interpretable failure signal — ontology-violation rate — for manipulation policies. For the study of natural intelligence, it supplies a formal account of human commonsense about parts against which both people and machines can be measured, turning "the model represents parts the way humans do" from an analogy into a theorem or a counter-model. The audit toolkit applies to any hierarchical annotation problem, including part labels in medical imaging and CAD assemblies, and the violation signal is the machine-checkable evidence about training data and policies that the EU AI Act (applying to AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework will require. In the long term the ontology extends COLORE's open network of verified first-order theories to the part-level structure of the physical world, giving neuro-symbolic robotics a foundation on which agreement with human commonsense about parts and wholes can be proved rather than assumed.
