## Verified mereological ontologies for object–part representation in physical AI: specification, audit and inductive bias for robot-learning data

**1. Recent Progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what terms a system uses and what they mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects and their parts as theories in first-order logic and verifies them, so that every claim about the ontology is a theorem or a counter-model rather than an informal gloss.

- **Top-level ontology as an international standard.** As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the TUpper top-level ontology [1], I worked on the axiomatization, the modular organization and the machine-checked verification of consistency and of the relationships between modules of a top-level ontology adopted by ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 [confirm exact role wording and modules]. TUpper gives implementers a reference theory against which domain ontologies can be proven, not merely asserted, to be consistent extensions; the parts ontology proposed here is built as such an extension.
- **A formal theory of material constitution and mereological pluralism.** With Prof. Grüninger I developed an account of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism by the verification techniques used for ontologies; both papers are submitted to Synthese [2, 3]. Consistency and non-triviality are established with automated provers and model finders, and the mapping is proved to preserve the parthood structure it must preserve. The theory states exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible — the question that robot datasets pose, where kinematic, functional and visual decompositions co-exist without any statement of how they relate.
- **Knowledge-system architecture with production deployment.** My doctoral research at the University of Toronto developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer [4]; elements of it contributed to ISO/IEC 21838-4. Outside the university I designed ontology-governed data-integration and recommendation systems that ran in production at companies I co-founded, and I am first inventor on patents in 3D recognition, indoor modelling and automatic reconstruction [reconcile counts and list numbers]. I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School [laboratory/department; one sentence on the work].

**2. Objectives.** Robot manipulation is now learned from a small number of large part-level datasets — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], and real-robot corpora such as AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10] and DROID [11] — that annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each dataset defines "part" operationally: a segmentation label in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. No dataset and no learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; and pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies, brittle generalization to new categories — are observed but never measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. For an engineering faculty whose robots, digital twins and product models all depend on part structure, this is a data-quality problem with a formal-methods answer. The proposed program is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

To address this challenge, the program has three primary objectives:
1. **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper, integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for state change under manipulation.
2. **Ontology as audit** — build a two-tier pipeline that translates annotation hierarchies into ontology instances and checks them, yielding the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, corrected annotations, provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings and a merged, ontology-aligned corpus.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias** — develop training and evaluation methods that use the ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies, and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories in simulation.

**3. Literature Review.** Very few representations of physical objects and their parts have been formalized to the extent suitable for automated reasoning. Formal mereology [13] supplies axioms of parthood, and the mereotopology modules of TUpper have been verified [1, 14], but no verified ontology distinguishes the rigid, articulated, functional and assembly senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data, and none relates parthood to the state changes that manipulation produces. The closest prior work, the ontological analysis of vision benchmarks in Prof. Grüninger's laboratory [14], asks which mereotopologies the static part decompositions of ShapeNet and PartNet presuppose; it does not treat articulated, functional or assembly parthood, parthood change under manipulation, real-robot corpora, audit at dataset scale, or cross-dataset alignment. Those five gaps are what this program adds, and none of them is an objective of the laboratory's current NSERC programs; the shared element is the verification methodology, which is the reason for the choice of supervisor.

On the learning side, datasets are known to carry pervasive label errors that destabilize benchmarks [15] and to be documented well below the standard their downstream use demands [16]; the tools that exist (label-noise estimation, datasheets, schema matching) treat labels as flat categories and never check a hierarchy against the axioms it is supposed to satisfy. Neuro-symbolic methods that impose logical constraints on learned models [17] have not been applied to part structure in manipulation, and no evaluation protocol reports whether a policy's inferred object structure is consistent with any specification.

**4. Methodology.** The ontology will be designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of the Semantic Technologies Laboratory [18] and verified using the COLORE repository methodology [19]: I characterize the models of each module up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models, using Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [20]. Competency questions come from the data: every annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute. All axioms, adapters, mappings, corrected annotations and evaluation code are versioned and released, so that each reported number is reproducible from public inputs. Open research questions are milestones tagged by fellowship year (Y1, Y2).

**Theme 1: Parts (Objective 1).** *Which parthood relations are implicit in robot-learning annotations, and can they be axiomatized as verified extensions of TUpper?*
**Project 1.1 — Parts ontology.** I axiomatize rigid parthood, articulated parthood (links and joints), functional parthood (affordance-bearing regions) and assembly parthood as separate modules in Common Logic [21], verify each for consistency and non-triviality, and prove representation theorems that characterize their models.
**Project 1.2 — Constitution mappings and change.** I apply the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2, 3] to fix when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible, and integrate PSL so that changes of parthood under manipulation (detaching a lid, opening a drawer) are fluents whose preconditions and effects are axiomatized.
*Open research questions:* Do the four parthood relations require distinct axiomatizations, or are some definable from others? (Y1) Is every dataset's part hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not? (Y1) Does articulated parthood require the PSL ontology of activities, or can it be captured by a static mereotopology with kinematic constraints? (Y1)

**Theme 2: Audit (Objective 2).** *What is the rate and pattern of parthood-axiom violation in the datasets that physical-AI models are trained on, and which cross-dataset label mappings are meaning-preserving?*
**Project 2.1 — Two-tier audit pipeline.** Adapters translate each dataset's annotation hierarchy into ontology instances; Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [22] check the bulk (millions of part instances), and residual hard cases go to first-order theorem proving. UNKNOWN and timeout are first-class outputs, and the audit reports coverage by source dataset, object category and region so that gaps are documented rather than hidden.
**Project 2.2 — Cross-dataset alignment and release.** I derive label mappings between datasets that are provably meaning-preserving under the constitution theory, correct violating annotations, and release a merged, ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable audit toolkit.
*Open research questions:* Which fragment of the ontology compiles to SMT without losing violations that the prover finds? (Y1) Do violation rates differ systematically between simulated (PartNet-Mobility) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora, and between annotation pipelines? (Y1–Y2) Can meaning-preserving mappings be found for every pair of datasets, or do some pairs admit only partial mappings, and which axioms block them? (Y2)

**Theme 3: Learning (Objective 3).** *Does ontology alignment let data pool without loss, and does enforcing the parthood axioms improve a manipulation policy's generalization to unseen object categories?*
**Project 3.1 — Pooling and constraint-based training.** I train part-aware manipulation policies on pooled ontology-aligned versus unaligned data, implement differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on inferred part relations [17], and use constraint-guided augmentation to synthesize ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories.
**Project 3.2 — Verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** I report, alongside task success, the rate at which a policy's inferred object structure violates the ontology. Experiments use the part-aware manipulation baselines released with SAPIEN and GAPartNet [6, 7], not a new architecture, on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories in SAPIEN [6] and [second simulator — confirm], with repeated seeds and category splits, confidence intervals, and ablations by axiom family; co-mentor [name] provides the policy-learning pipeline and evaluation design, and compute comes from [Vector Institute allocation / co-mentor's laboratory — confirm] with the fellowship's research funds as overflow. If co-mentor or compute is not secured by month 12, Project 3.2 runs on released policies (evaluation only) and Project 3.1 is scoped to one axiom family; Themes 1 and 2 do not depend on Theme 3.
*Open research questions:* Does ontology-consistent training improve generalization to unseen categories? (Y2) Does violation rate predict task failure, and can it serve as an interpretable failure signal? (Y2) Do policies trained on pooled, ontology-aligned data transfer across part vocabularies? (Y2)

**5. Impact.** The program delivers a verified parts ontology under an open licence, contributed to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, with corrected annotations, cross-dataset mappings and a merged corpus released publicly; an audit toolkit packaged for reuse; and three papers — two at machine-learning venues and one at a robotics venue — over the 24 months. For the Faculty of Applied Science & Engineering the near-term impact is engineering: the same ontology and audit pipeline apply to CAD assemblies and product-lifecycle data in intelligent manufacturing, to the digital twins of articulated equipment, and to the benchmark quality on which the Robotics Institute's learned systems depend. The audit also supplies the kind of machine-checkable evidence about training data that the EU AI Act (which applies to AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework will require of robot deployments, and that ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 is positioned to standardize.

In the long term the ontology extends COLORE's open network of verified first-order theories to the part-level structure of the physical world, so that what a physical-AI system has learned about parts and wholes can be proved correct rather than assumed. The program is my own, complementary to but distinct from Prof. Grüninger's NSERC programs on the Physical Turing Test and commonsense cobotics: it takes the laboratory's verification methodology to the datasets and policies of robot learning, and it is the research agenda on which I intend to build an independent academic career in knowledge representation for engineering systems.
