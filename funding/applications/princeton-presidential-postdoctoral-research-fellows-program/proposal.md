## Verified Mereological Ontologies for Object–Part Representation in Physical AI

**1. Recent Progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what terms an agent uses and what they mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects as first-order theories and verifies them, so that every claim about the ontology is either a theorem or a counter-model. As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023, the international standard for the TUpper top-level ontology [1], I worked on the axiomatization, modular organization and machine-checked verification of a top-level ontology adopted by ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 [confirm exact role wording]. With Michael Grüninger (University of Toronto) I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two papers submitted to *Synthese* [2, 3]. Classical mereology assumes a single parthood relation; the puzzles of constitution (the statue and the clay) arise because ordinary objects seem to demand more than one. Our account states, in first-order logic verified with automated provers and model finders, exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible. My doctoral research developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data model, the learned models and the simulation layer [4], and I have carried the same discipline into practice through production data systems at companies I co-founded and [number — reconcile: 3 Chinese invention patents; 9–10 German utility patents] patents in 3D recognition and indoor modelling. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School [laboratory — confirm appointment and describe the work in one sentence].

**2. Objectives.** Robot manipulation policies are trained on part-level datasets — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10], DROID [11] — that annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels, each defining "part" operationally: a segmentation mask in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. No dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible part vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across vocabularies and brittle generalization to new object categories — are observed throughout embodied learning but have never been measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. This matters beyond robotics research: a robot deployed in a home or a factory is trusted to have learned something about the structure of the objects it handles, and today there is no machine-checkable evidence of what that something is. The proposed program is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

To address this challenge, the program has three primary objectives:
1. **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for state change under manipulation.
2. **Ontology as audit** — translate the annotation hierarchies of the major robot-learning datasets into ontology instances and check them automatically, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets and provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias** — develop training and evaluation methods that use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies, and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories.

**3. Literature Review.** Formal mereology [13] supplies axiom systems for parthood, and the mereotopology modules of TUpper have been verified [1, 14], but no verified ontology distinguishes the rigid, articulated, functional and assembly senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data, and the philosophical literature on constitution and pluralism [13, 2, 3] has not been connected to any dataset. Part-level datasets [5–11] carry no formal semantics; ontological analysis of vision benchmarks [14] found logical errors in ShapeNet and PartNet but produced neither a scalable audit nor a cross-dataset alignment. Neuro-symbolic methods that impose logical constraints on learned models [15] have not been applied to part structure in manipulation, and assurance work on learned robot policies concentrates on control-level guarantees rather than on the object-level knowledge a policy has acquired. The gap is threefold — no specification, no measurement, no training signal — and the three objectives close it in that order.

**4. Methodology.** The ontologies will be designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of Grüninger and Fox [16] and verified using the COLORE repository methodology [17]: we characterize the models of each module up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models, using Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [18]. Verification (do the axioms have exactly the intended models?) is kept distinct from validation (are the intended models the right ones?); the latter is settled by competency questions taken from the datasets, since every annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute. The tools are open and I bring the methodology with me; Prof. Grüninger continues as co-author on Theme 1 [subject to his agreement — pending]. Open research questions are milestones tagged by fellowship year (Y1–Y2); items marked (beyond) continue after the fellowship with the sponsor's group.

**Theme 1: Parts (Objective 1).** *Which parthood relations are implicit in robot-learning annotations, and can they be axiomatized as verified extensions of TUpper?* **Project 1.1 — Parts ontology.** Axiomatize rigid, articulated (links and joints), functional (affordance-bearing regions) and assembly parthood as separate modules in Common Logic [19], each verified for consistency and non-triviality, with representation theorems characterizing their models. **Project 1.2 — Constitution mappings.** Apply the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2, 3] to state when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible, and integrate PSL so that changes of parthood under manipulation (detaching a lid, opening a drawer) are fluents. *Open research questions:* Do the four parthood relations require distinct axiomatizations, or are some definable from others? (Y1) Is every dataset's part hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not? (Y1)

**Theme 2: Audit (Objective 2).** *What is the rate and pattern of parthood-axiom violation in the datasets that physical-AI models are trained on?* **Project 2.1 — Two-tier audit pipeline.** Translate annotation hierarchies into ontology instances through adapters for each dataset format; check the bulk (millions of part instances) with Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [20]; send residual hard cases to first-order theorem proving; treat UNKNOWN and timeout as first-class outputs, not silent passes. **Project 2.2 — Cross-dataset alignment.** Derive label mappings between datasets that are provably meaning-preserving under the constitution theory, correct violating annotations, and release a merged, ontology-aligned corpus and an audit toolkit. *Open research questions:* Which fragment of the ontology can be compiled to SMT without losing violations that the prover finds? (Y1) Do violation rates differ systematically between simulated (PartNet-Mobility) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora? (Y1–Y2) Does the audit transfer, unchanged, to a second hierarchical-annotation domain such as CAD assemblies? (beyond)

**Theme 3: Learning (Objective 3).** *Does enforcing the parthood axioms during training improve a manipulation policy's generalization to unseen object categories?* **Project 3.1 — Constraint-based training.** Implement differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on a policy's inferred part relations [15], and constraint-guided augmentation synthesizing ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories. **Project 3.2 — Verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** Report, alongside task success, the rate at which a policy's inferred object structure violates the ontology, so that evaluation yields a machine-checkable statement about what the policy has learned. Experiments in SAPIEN [6] and [second simulator — confirm with the sponsor] on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories, with confidence intervals and ablations by axiom family; a real-robot replication on [the sponsor group's platform — confirm] is planned for the second half of Y2 if the platform is available, and otherwise continues beyond the fellowship. *Open research questions:* Does ontology-consistent training improve generalization to unseen categories? (Y2) Does violation rate predict task failure, and can it serve as an interpretable failure signal? (Y2) Do policies trained on pooled, ontology-aligned data transfer across part vocabularies and across embodiments? (Y2, beyond)

**Why Princeton, and what the fellowship makes possible.** The program needs three things in one place: formal methods and automated reasoning; a robotics group that produces and consumes part-level data and cares about assurance; and a philosophical community in which the theory of parts and wholes is taken seriously as a subject. [Sponsor name]'s group supplies [the second — one sentence on the sponsor's robot-learning, safety-and-assurance or 3D-perception work and the platform available; confirm]; Princeton Computer Science's work in formal verification and SMT-based reasoning supplies the first [name the group — confirm]; and the Department of Philosophy supplies the third [name a metaphysics faculty member willing to act as informal second mentor — confirm]. A university-wide fellowship is the right home for a program that argues a metaphysical theory should have engineering consequences. The two-year term is sized to the plan: the ontology and the audit (Y1) are complete, released deliverables in their own right, and the learning experiments (Y2) are designed so that a null result — enforcing the axioms does not improve generalization — is itself a publishable, machine-checkable finding about what part-level training data can and cannot teach.

**5. Impact.** The program delivers a verified parts ontology under an open licence, contributed to COLORE and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, with corrected annotations, mappings and a merged corpus released publicly; and an audit toolkit that applies to any hierarchical annotation problem, including CAD assemblies. For robotics and AI assurance it supplies an interpretable failure signal — ontology-violation rate — that is machine-checkable evidence about what a policy has learned, of the kind the EU AI Act (applying to AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework will require. For philosophy it turns the constitution and pluralism debates into theories with empirical consequences: a dataset either is or is not a model of a given mereology, and a policy either does or does not respect it. In the long term the ontology extends COLORE's open network of verified theories to the part-level structure of the physical world, so that a robot's correctness with respect to human commonsense about parts and wholes can be proved rather than assumed.
