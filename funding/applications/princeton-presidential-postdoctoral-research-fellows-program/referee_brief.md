## What the selection committee weighs
The program is university-wide, so the committee compares candidates from fields it cannot judge technically. The published language [verify the current wording; the criteria were revised in 2025] describes "outstanding scholars primed to make important contributions in their fields" who will "contribute to the University's diversity, broadly defined"; the sponsor's nomination letter must set out the research plan and how the candidate will diversify the department or discipline. The most useful letters therefore (a) explain the work in terms a scholar from another field will understand, (b) give one or two concrete examples — a result you saw me obtain, a problem I solved without direction, a piece of work you would not have expected at my stage — and (c) state plainly where I stand relative to other early-career researchers you have known. If you can speak to mentoring, teaching, service, or to what I add to a group that it would not otherwise have, one concrete example is worth more than a general endorsement.

Where you can, please also mention the graduate-student representation, teaching or mentoring you have observed.
