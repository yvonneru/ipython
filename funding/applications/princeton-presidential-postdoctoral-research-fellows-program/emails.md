## 2. To a prospective faculty sponsor at Princeton — send today to each candidate (one at a time, or two in parallel with disclosure)

**To:** [Prof. name, department — verify current Princeton appointment as assistant, associate or full professor; each may nominate one candidate per cycle]
**Subject:** Presidential Postdoctoral Research Fellowship — would you consider nominating a proposal on verified part ontologies for robot-learning data?

Dear Professor [name],

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School [confirm] and completed my PhD in Information Engineering at the University of Toronto in 2025. I am writing to ask whether you would consider nominating me for Princeton's Presidential Postdoctoral Research Fellows Program, the Dean of the Faculty's university-wide fellowship (one year renewable to two, salary and research support paid by the program rather than by the sponsor's grants). Nominations are due 13 November 2026, and I know each faculty member may nominate only one candidate, so I would understand entirely if you already have one.

The proposed program applies verified formal ontologies to the part-level datasets on which robot manipulation policies are trained. Datasets such as PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, AgiBot World, Open X-Embodiment and DROID annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels, but no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood, and nobody has checked them against any formal specification. I propose to (1) axiomatize and machine-verify an ontology of object parts as an extension of ISO/IEC 21838-4, on which I was a core contributor; (2) audit those datasets automatically, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot data and provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset mappings, released publicly; and (3) use the ontology as an inductive bias and as an evaluation signal — ontology-violation rate alongside task success — for part-aware manipulation policies, measuring the effect on generalization to unseen object categories. [One sentence on why this connects to the sponsor's work — e.g., their work on safety and assurance for learned robot policies, articulated-object manipulation, 3D perception, or SMT-based verification — confirm before sending.]

My background is in formal knowledge representation (ISO/IEC 21838-4; a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, with Michael Grüninger, under review at Synthese) and in building production data systems as a founder of technology companies. What I would ask of a sponsor: the letter of nomination (which the program asks to cover the research plan and how the candidate would diversify the department — I will supply a one-page brief and drafts for both), submission of the nomination by 13 November, and roughly fortnightly meetings; access to a simulator and, where possible in year two, a robot platform for the evaluation component. What the sponsor's group would get: a verified, released parts ontology and audit toolkit usable in its own work, a public aligned corpus, co-authorship on the audit and learning papers, and a fellow who has built data pipelines at production scale.

I attach the three-page research statement, a short text on my contributions to the academic community and my CV. I would be glad to talk by video at any time this week or next. [If writing to two candidates in parallel: I should say that I am also approaching one other Princeton faculty member about nominating; I will make the choice, if both are open to it, in consultation with both of you.]
