## Part A — Research plan summary (form paragraph)

Robot-learning datasets annotate objects as assemblies of parts, but each defines "part" operationally and no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood. I will (1) axiomatize and verify an ontology of physical object parts as a modular extension of ISO/IEC 21838-4 (TUpper) integrated with the Process Specification Language, using the COLORE verification methodology of the Semantic Technologies Laboratory (Prover9 theorems, Mace4 counter-models, representation theorems); (2) align the part vocabularies of PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled and AgiBot World to it and audit them automatically for parthood violations and cross-dataset inconsistency, with a two-tier Datalog/SMT and theorem-proving pipeline; and (3) use the verified ontology as a consistency layer on part-aware manipulation policies in SAPIEN simulation and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories. Outputs: a verified parts ontology contributed to the COLORE repository and to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, with corrected annotations, cross-dataset mappings and a merged corpus released publicly; a reusable audit toolkit; and policy results reporting ontology-violation rate alongside task success. The program is my own and is complementary to the nominator's NSERC programs; it brings the laboratory's verification methodology to the part-level datasets now driving physical AI, and sits squarely in the AI priority area. Training environment: Semantic Technologies Laboratory (MIE), with collaborators at the University of Toronto Robotics Institute and the Vector Institute.

## Part B — Two-page research plan

## Verified Mereological Ontologies for Object–Part Representation in Physical AI

**1. Recent Progress.** An ontology is a computer-interpretable specification of what terms an agent uses and what those terms mean. My research axiomatizes ontologies of physical objects as theories in first-order logic and verifies them: every claim is a theorem or a counter-model. As a core contributor to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 (TUpper) [1], I worked on the axiomatization, modular organization and machine-checked verification of a top-level ontology adopted as an international standard. With Prof. Grüninger I developed a theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between mereologies, and a validation of mereological pluralism, in two papers submitted to Synthese [2, 3]; the theory states exactly what must hold for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible. My doctoral research developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data, learned-model and simulation layers [4], and I have shipped ontology-governed data-integration and 3D-dataset systems in production at two companies I co-founded. Since [month year] I have been a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School, [one sentence on the work], which has given me a second data regime against which to test formal methods.

**2. Objectives.** Robot manipulation policies are trained on part-level datasets — PartNet [5], PartNet-Mobility/SAPIEN [6], GAPartNet [7], PartNet-Ensembled [8], AgiBot World [9], Open X-Embodiment [10], DROID [11] — that annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. Each dataset defines "part" operationally; no dataset or learned model is required to satisfy the axioms of parthood; there is no principled way to map labels across datasets; pooled training silently mixes incompatible vocabularies. The consequences — poor transfer across part vocabularies and brittle generalization to new categories — are observed but have never been measured, because there has been no specification to measure against. The proposed program is motivated by the following long-term challenge:

*Can the part-level representations that robot-learning systems learn be shown to satisfy the axioms of parthood that humans use, and does enforcing those axioms improve generalization?*

To address this challenge, the program has three primary objectives:
1. **Ontology as specification** — axiomatize and verify a modular first-order ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) as an extension of TUpper integrated with the Process Specification Language (PSL) [12] for state change under manipulation.
2. **Ontology as audit** — translate the annotation hierarchies of the major robot-learning datasets into ontology instances and check them automatically, producing the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets and provably meaning-preserving cross-dataset label mappings.
3. **Ontology as inductive bias** — develop training and evaluation methods that use the verified ontology to shape part-aware manipulation policies, and measure the effect on generalization to unseen object categories.

**3. Literature Review.** Formal mereology [13] supplies axiom systems for parthood, and TUpper's mereotopology modules have been verified [1, 14], but no verified ontology distinguishes the rigid, articulated, functional and assembly senses of "part" that co-exist in robot data. Part-level datasets [5–11] carry no formal semantics: a part is a segmentation mask in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. Ontological analysis of vision benchmarks [14] found logical errors in ShapeNet and PartNet but produced neither a scalable audit nor a cross-dataset alignment. Neuro-symbolic methods that impose logical constraints on learned models [15] have not been applied to part structure in manipulation, and no evaluation protocol reports whether a policy's inferred object structure is consistent with any specification.

**4. Methodology.** The ontologies will be designed with the ontology lifecycle methodology of the Semantic Technologies Laboratory [16] and verified using the COLORE repository methodology [17]: we characterize the models of each module up to isomorphism and determine whether they are equivalent to the intended models, using Prover9 for theorems and Mace4 for counter-models [18]. Competency questions come from the datasets: every annotation pattern to be accepted or rejected is a sentence the ontology must entail or refute. Open research questions are milestones tagged by award year (Y1, Y2); those tagged (G) are suitable for a co-supervised graduate student in the laboratory.

**Theme 1: Parts (Objective 1).** *Which parthood relations are implicit in robot-learning annotations, and can they be axiomatized as verified extensions of TUpper?* **Project 1.1 — Parts ontology.** Axiomatize rigid parthood, articulated parthood (links and joints), functional parthood (affordance-bearing regions) and assembly parthood as separate modules in Common Logic [19], each verified for consistency and non-triviality, with representation theorems that characterize their models. **Project 1.2 — Constitution mappings.** Apply the parthood-preserving-mapping theory [2, 3] to state when kinematic, functional and visual decompositions of one object are compatible, and integrate PSL so that parthood changes under manipulation (detaching a lid) are fluents. *Open questions:* Do the four parthood relations require distinct axiomatizations, or are some definable from others? (Y1) Is every dataset's part hierarchy a model of at least one module, and which axioms fail when it is not? (Y1, G)

**Theme 2: Audit (Objective 2).** *What is the rate and pattern of parthood-axiom violation in the datasets that physical-AI models are trained on?* **Project 2.1 — Two-tier audit pipeline.** Translate annotation hierarchies into ontology instances via adapters for each dataset format; check the bulk (millions of parts) with Datalog/SMT rules compiled from the axioms [20], and send residual hard cases to first-order theorem proving; UNKNOWN and timeout are first-class outputs. **Project 2.2 — Cross-dataset alignment.** Derive label mappings between datasets that are provably meaning-preserving under the constitution theory, correct violating annotations, and release a merged, ontology-aligned corpus and a reusable audit toolkit. *Open questions:* Which fragment of the ontology is decidable enough to compile to SMT without losing violations found by the prover? (Y1) Do violation rates differ systematically between simulated (PartNet-Mobility) and real-robot (AgiBot World, DROID) corpora? (Y1–Y2, G)

**Theme 3: Learning (Objective 3).** *Does enforcing the parthood axioms during training improve a manipulation policy's generalization to unseen object categories?* **Project 3.1 — Constraint-based training.** Implement differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on a policy's inferred part relations [15], and constraint-guided augmentation that synthesizes ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories. **Project 3.2 — Verification-in-the-loop evaluation.** Report, alongside task success, the rate at which a policy's inferred object structure violates the ontology. Experiments in SAPIEN [6] and [second simulator — confirm] on held-out PartNet-Mobility categories, with confidence intervals and ablations by axiom family, with a Robotics Institute collaborator [name] and Vector Institute compute. *Open questions:* Does ontology-consistent training improve generalization to unseen categories? (Y2) Does violation rate predict task failure? (Y2) Do policies trained on pooled, ontology-aligned data transfer across part vocabularies? (Y2)

**5. Impact.** The program delivers a verified parts ontology under an open licence, contributed to COLORE and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, with corrected annotations, mappings and a merged corpus released publicly; and an audit toolkit that applies to any hierarchical annotation problem, including CAD assemblies, building information models and part labels in medical imaging. For robotics it supplies an interpretable failure signal — ontology-violation rate — that is machine-checkable evidence about what a policy has learned, the kind of evidence that the EU AI Act (applying to AI in machinery and robots from 2 August 2028) and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework will require. For Canada it returns a U of T-trained researcher from Harvard to the only laboratory in which this work can be done, and trains graduate students in a verification discipline that industry in advanced digital technologies and manufacturing now needs. In the long term the ontology extends COLORE's open network of verified first-order theories to the part-level structure of the physical world, giving neuro-symbolic robotics a foundation on which correctness with respect to human commonsense about parts and wholes can be proved rather than assumed.
