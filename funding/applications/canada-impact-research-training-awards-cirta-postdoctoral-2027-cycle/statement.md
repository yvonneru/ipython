**Why this program, and why Toronto.** The proposed research requires three things to be in one place: a verified-ontology methodology with tool support (the Semantic Technologies Laboratory at MIE), a robotics community generating and consuming part-level data (the University of Toronto Robotics Institute and the Vector Institute), and a researcher who has done both formal theory and production data integration. I left Toronto after my doctorate; I return not for continuity but for a specific capability, and I do so with a research program that is my own. The nominator's Discovery Grant programs ask which mereotopologies the static part decompositions of ShapeNet and PartNet presuppose; my program adds five things they do not treat — articulated, functional and assembly parthood, parthood change under manipulation, real-robot corpora, audit at dataset scale, and cross-dataset alignment — and none of its datasets, methods or experiments appears in my thesis, which was on knowledge-system architecture and top-level ontology. The two Synthese papers provide the theory; the physical-AI datasets released over the past three years provide, for the first time, data at a scale where the theory can be tested; and the award's two-year term matches the Y1–Y2 sequence in the plan.
