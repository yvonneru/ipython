Title
Auditing and aligning part-level annotations in robot datasets: a verified-ontology approach to data integration for physical AI
Co-supervisors
Prof. Michael Grüninger, Mechanical and Industrial Engineering (formal ontology, automated reasoning, verification). The data problem
Physical AI is being built on a handful of large part-level datasets — PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PartNet-Ensembled and real-robot corpora such as AgiBot World — that annotate objects as hierarchies of parts with kinematic, functional and visual labels. These datasets are combined freely in training pipelines, yet their annotation schemas were designed independently: a “part” is a segmentation label in one, a movable link in another, an actionable region in a third. There is no shared definition, no consistency requirement within a dataset (a part may be annotated as both contained in and disjoint from another), and no principled way to map labels across datasets. The consequences are visible in the field — poor transfer across part vocabularies, silent errors when datasets are pooled — but have never been measured, because there has been no formal specification against which to measure them.
Approach
1. A verified specification of part structure. We formalize an ontology of physical object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly) in first-order logic as a modular extension of the ISO/IEC 21838-4 top-level ontology, and verify it — consistency, non-triviality and the relationships between modules — with automated theorem provers and model finders using the COLORE repository methodology. The ontology fixes what it means for two part decompositions of the same object to be compatible, drawing on the co-applicant’s theory of material constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping.
2. Scalable consistency auditing. Annotation hierarchies are translated into ontology instances and checked by a two-tier pipeline: Datalog/SMT rules for the bulk of the data (millions of part instances), first-order theorem proving for the residual hard cases. The output is, for each dataset, the rate and pattern of parthood-axiom violations, and across datasets, the set of label mappings that are provably meaning-preserving. This is a data-science contribution in its own right: a general method for auditing hierarchical annotations against a formal specification, applicable beyond robotics (medical image part labels, CAD assemblies, building information models).
3. Measured effect on learning. We test whether ontology-aligned annotations can be pooled across datasets without loss, and whether constraining a part-aware manipulation policy to satisfy the ontology improves generalization to unseen object categories in SAPIEN simulation. Evaluation is statistical: matched comparisons across held-out categories with confidence intervals, and ablations by axiom family. Co-supervisor [name] provides the policy-learning pipeline and evaluation design.
Deliverables
A verified parts ontology (open licence; contributed to COLORE and ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42). The first quantitative audit of parthood consistency in robot datasets, with corrected annotations and cross-dataset mappings released publicly. A merged, ontology-aligned part-level corpus. An audit toolkit packaged for reuse on other hierarchical annotation problems. Two publications: an audit/data paper at a leading AI or robotics venue and a methods paper on specification-based annotation auditing.
Alignment with DSI
The project is methods development in data science — a specification-based approach to data quality and integration — applied to a domain where the datasets are large, public and consequential. It is interdisciplinary by construction (formal methods and robotics/statistics), reproducible by design (all axioms, mappings and code released), and produces reusable tooling and data rather than a single result.
