Justification for eligibility of proposed research (optional attachment, 1 page)
Read only by NSERC staff, not by reviewers. Included because the program touches learned policies and object manipulation, areas where an application could conceivably be routed elsewhere.
The proposed research falls within the natural sciences and engineering. Its subject matter is the formal representation of physical objects and their parts, the verification of first-order theories by automated reasoning, and the use of those theories to audit engineering datasets and to constrain machine-learned manipulation policies. The methods are those of knowledge representation, mathematical logic, software engineering and robotics; the data are engineering datasets of three-dimensional objects and robot trajectories; the evaluation is by theorem proving, dataset statistics and simulation experiments on manipulation tasks.
The research is not health research and does not involve human participants, animals, clinical data or biological material; it is therefore outside the CIHR mandate. It is not social-sciences or humanities research: although the theory of parts and wholes has a philosophical literature, the program uses that literature as a source of axioms to be verified and applied, not as an object of philosophical inquiry, and its outputs are verified ontologies, software, aligned datasets and measured effects on robot policy generalization. The two submitted papers cited in the outline are formal results in knowledge representation that were submitted to a philosophy-of-science journal because that journal publishes formal mereology; the proposed work is their engineering application.
The applicant’s doctorate is in Information Engineering; the proposed supervisor holds an NSERC-funded [confirm] laboratory in a faculty of engineering; and the outputs will be disseminated in AI and robotics venues and through ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42. On every criterion NSERC uses to determine the appropriate agency — subject matter, methodology, training environment and dissemination — this is NSERC research.

What the system asks. Two text boxes with character limits (roughly half a page and three-quarters of a page). The first asks for an assessment of the applicant’s research ability and potential — originality, independence, judgement, productivity relative to career stage. The second asks about relevant skills and experience: communication, collaboration, leadership, initiative, and the ability to see a program through. There is no attachment; text only. Please write in the boxes rather than pasting a letter that will be truncated.
What reviewers score. Research ability and potential (50%), and the quality of the proposed research (50%). The most useful letters give one or two concrete examples — a result you saw the applicant obtain, a problem they solved without direction, a piece of work you would not have expected at their stage — and state plainly where the applicant stands relative to other postdoctoral researchers you have known.
Points that would help. (1) The theoretical work on material constitution and mereological pluralism, and why it matters for knowledge representation. (2) The ISO/IEC 21838-4 contribution and what it shows about rigour and ability to work in a standards body. (3) The move from theory to deployed systems (patents, production data-integration work) and why that predicts success with O2 and O3. (4) Independence: the proposed program is the applicant’s own, complementary to but distinct from the supervisor’s work.
