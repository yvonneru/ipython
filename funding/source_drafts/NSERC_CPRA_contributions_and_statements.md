Part II — Most significant contributions
The three contributions below are those I consider most significant to my field and most relevant to the proposed research. For each I state my role, the significance of the work, and its bearing on the program of work in the attached outline.
1. Axiomatization and verification of TUpper as an international standard (ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023). Top-level ontologies are the foundation on which domain ontologies are built, yet before this standard no top-level ontology with a complete first-order axiomatization and machine-checked verification had been adopted internationally. As a core contributor within ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, I worked on the axiomatization of TUpper, its modular organization, and the formal verification of consistency and of the relationships between its modules. [Specify precisely which modules and which verification results you were responsible for.] The significance is twofold. Scientifically, TUpper demonstrated that a top-level ontology can be given the same standard of rigour as a mathematical theory — every claim is a theorem or a counter-model — rather than the informal glosses that characterize most upper ontologies. Practically, the standard gives implementers a reference theory against which domain ontologies can be proven, not merely asserted, to be consistent extensions. This is directly the methodology the proposed program applies: the physical-object parts ontology in O1 will be built as a verified modular extension of TUpper, and my familiarity with the standard, its editors and SC 42 procedures is what makes the dissemination plan (contribution back to the standard) realistic.
2. A formal theory of material constitution and mereological pluralism (with M. Grüninger; two papers submitted to Synthese, 2026). Classical mereology assumes a single parthood relation; the puzzles of material constitution (the statue and the clay, the ship and its planks) arise because ordinary objects seem to demand more than one. My work treats constitution as a parthood-preserving mapping between distinct mereologies, and shows that the resulting pluralism can be validated by the same verification techniques used for ontologies: the axioms are stated in first-order logic, consistency and non-triviality are established with automated provers and model finders, and the mapping is proved to preserve the parthood structure it is required to preserve. [Insert one sentence with the paper’s central theorem in plain language.] I developed the theory and the proofs and wrote both papers; Prof. Grüninger contributed the verification framework and the placement of the results within the COLORE repository. The significance for knowledge representation is that the account is not a philosophical proposal but a machine-checkable theory that a reasoning system can use: given two part decompositions of the same object, the theory says exactly what must hold for them to be compatible. That is precisely the question posed by robot datasets, where kinematic, functional and visual part decompositions of the same object co-exist without any statement of how they relate. Objectives O1 and O2 of the outline are the empirical continuation of this theoretical work.
3. Knowledge-system architecture for machine-learning-based simulation, with industrial deployment. My doctoral research at the University of Toronto developed an architecture for AI knowledge systems in which a verified ontology governs the data, model and simulation layers, and demonstrated it with machine-learning-based simulation in engineering settings. [Insert one sentence naming the application domain of the thesis and the principal quantitative result.] In parallel, as a founding team member of MICAS (cross-border direct-to-consumer commerce) and co-founder of YourTable Inc., I designed and shipped production recommendation, CRM/ERP and influencer-allocation systems whose data models were ontology-governed, and I hold [3] Chinese invention patents and [9–10] German utility patents arising from applied work in [name the domains — e.g., health-screening devices, energy systems]. The significance is that the ontology-first architecture has been shown to work outside the laboratory, at production scale, with real data-integration problems — the same class of problem, at the same scale, that O2 addresses for robot datasets. This contribution is also the basis of my claim to be able to deliver O3: I have integrated formal constraints with learned models in deployed systems before, and the engineering discipline required (versioned axioms, regression tests on inferences, reproducible pipelines) is one I have practised rather than proposed.
