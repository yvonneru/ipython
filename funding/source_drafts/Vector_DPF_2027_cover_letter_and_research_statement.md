Research statement
Motivation
Robot manipulation policies increasingly operate on part-level representations of objects — segmented, articulated, functional parts — learned from datasets such as PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet and AgiBot World. These representations are learned from annotations that carry no formal semantics: nothing requires the parts a model infers to form a consistent whole, nothing relates the part vocabularies of different datasets, and nothing lets a policy reason about why a part decomposition is wrong. The result is a familiar pattern in embodied learning: strong in-distribution performance and brittle transfer to new object categories, with failures that are diagnosed by inspection rather than by principle. I propose to supply the missing semantics with a verified ontology of physical object parts and to use it, in three ways, to improve what models learn.
Prior work
My doctoral work developed an architecture in which a verified ontology governs data, model and simulation layers of an AI system, and contributed to ISO/IEC 21838-4:2023 (TUpper). Proposed research
1. Ontology as specification. A verified ontology of object parts (rigid, articulated, functional, assembly), built as a modular extension of TUpper with the Process Specification Language for state change under manipulation, and proved consistent with automated provers. Representation theorems relate the ontology to the annotation schemas of the major datasets so that cross-dataset mappings are meaning-preserving by proof.
2. Ontology as audit. A scalable pipeline (Datalog/SMT with theorem proving for hard cases) checks annotation hierarchies against the ontology, yielding the first quantitative measurement of parthood consistency in robot datasets, a released set of corrected annotations and mappings, and a merged corpus. This is also a study of what current models are actually trained on.
3. Ontology as inductive bias. The machine-learning core. I will develop training methods that use the ontology to shape part-aware policies: (a) differentiable relaxations of parthood constraints as auxiliary losses on inferred part relations; (b) constraint-guided data augmentation that synthesizes ontology-consistent part configurations for underrepresented categories; and (c) a verification-in-the-loop evaluation protocol that reports, alongside task success, the rate at which a policy’s inferred object structure violates the ontology. Hypotheses: ontology-consistent training improves generalization to unseen PartNet-Mobility categories; violation rate predicts task failure and can serve as an interpretable failure signal; and pooled, ontology-aligned data trains policies that transfer across part vocabularies.
